## Key Fact (generated by LLM)

Marfan syndrome is an autosomal dominant genetic disorder, meaning a single copy of the mutated gene is sufficient to cause the condition, which fits the pedigree image showing affected individuals in each generation. This pattern is characteristic of dominant inheritance, where an affected parent has a 50% chance of passing the mutated gene to each offspring. The condition is caused by mutations in the FBN1 gene, leading to defects in fibrillin-1 protein and affecting connective tissue. Marfan syndrome is characterized by tall stature, long limbs, and increased risk of aortic aneurysm and dissection.
