# biochemistry_Q13

## Question

Fatty acids cross the inner mitochondrial membrane via:

## Options

- A) Acyl carrier protein
- B) Carnitine shuttle ✅
- C) Lecithin-cholesterol acyltransferase
- D) Albumin

## Your Answer

C) Lecithin-cholesterol acyltransferase

## Correct Answer

B) Carnitine shuttle

## Key Fact (generated by LLM)

Fatty acids cross the inner mitochondrial membrane via the carnitine shuttle, which involves the conversion of fatty acyl-CoA to fatty acyl-carnitine by carnitine palmitoyltransferase I, then transport across the membrane, and finally conversion back to fatty acyl-CoA by carnitine palmitoyltransferase II. This shuttle is necessary because the inner mitochondrial membrane is impermeable to long-chain fatty acids. The correct answer is thus B) Carnitine shuttle. 

## Why You Got It Wrong (generated by LLM)

The student's answer, C) Lecithin-cholesterol acyltransferase, is incorrect because this enzyme is involved in cholesterol esterification in the plasma, not in fatty acid transport into mitochondria.
