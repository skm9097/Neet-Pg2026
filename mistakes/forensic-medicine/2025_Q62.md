## 2025_Q62 — forensic-medicine

- **Q:** A patient with RTA, normal CT, dies within 3 hours. Autopsy shows retraction balls and petechial hemorrhages in corpus callosum. Diagnosis?
- **Got wrong:** A) Diffuse vascular injury
- **Correct:** B) Diffuse axonal injury ✅
- **Key fact:** Diffuse axonal injury (DAI) is a type of brain injury caused by shearing forces that occur when the brain is rapidly accelerated or decelerated, leading to damage of axons and retraction balls. The presence of retraction balls and petechial hemorrhages in the corpus callosum on autopsy is characteristic of DAI. This type of injury is common in patients with road traffic accidents (RTA) and can lead to rapid deterioration and death. The normal CT scan does not rule out DAI as it may not detect the subtle changes associated with this injury.
- **Why wrong:** The student incorrectly chose diffuse vascular injury, which is not supported by the autopsy findings of retraction balls and petechial hemorrhages. This suggests a lack of understanding of the pathology of brain injuries.
