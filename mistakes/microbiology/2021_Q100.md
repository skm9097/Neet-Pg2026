## 2021_Q100 — microbiology

- **Q:** Farmer with fever, myalgia, conjunctival congestion, jaundice, elevated bilirubin?
- **Got wrong:** D) Enteric fever
- **Correct:** B) Weil's syndrome (leptospirosis) ✅
- **Key fact:** Weil's syndrome, a severe form of leptospirosis, presents with fever, myalgia, conjunctival congestion, jaundice, and elevated bilirubin due to the bacterium Leptospira, which is commonly found in water or soil contaminated with the urine of infected animals, often affecting farmers or those in contact with contaminated water. The symptoms are a result of the bacteria's invasion of the bloodstream and subsequent damage to organs such as the liver and kidneys. This condition is distinct from enteric fever, which does not typically cause conjunctival congestion or such severe jaundice. 
- **Why wrong:** The student incorrectly chose enteric fever, which, although it can cause fever and jaundice, does not typically present with the combination of conjunctival congestion and severe jaundice seen in Weil's syndrome. The student likely overlooked the distinctive symptoms of Weil's syndrome.
