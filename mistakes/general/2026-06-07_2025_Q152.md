Child with progressive neurological decline, cherry-red spot, hexosaminidase A deficiency. Diagnosis?

## Options

- B) GM2 gangliosidosis (Tay-Sachs) ✅
- C) Niemann-Pick disease

## Correct Answer

B) GM2 gangliosidosis (Tay-Sachs)

## Key Fact (generated by LLM)

GM2 gangliosidosis, also known as Tay-Sachs disease, is a genetic disorder caused by a deficiency of the enzyme hexosaminidase A, leading to the accumulation of GM2 gangliosides in neurons and resulting in progressive neurological decline and the characteristic cherry-red spot on the macula. This disease is typically diagnosed in infancy or early childhood. The deficiency of hexosaminidase A is a direct cause of the disease, making option B the correct answer.

## Why You Got It Wrong (generated by LLM)

The student incorrectly chose Niemann-Pick disease, which is a different lysosomal storage disorder caused by a deficiency of sphingomyelinase, not hexosaminidase A.
