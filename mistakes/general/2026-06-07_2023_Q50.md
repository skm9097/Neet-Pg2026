## Question

RTA with severe muscle injury, K⁺ = 5.5 mEq/L. What happens to resting membrane potential?

## Options

- A) No change
- B) Becomes more negative (hyperpolarization)
- C) Becomes more positive (depolarization) ✅
- D) First more positive then negative

## Your Answer

B) Becomes more negative (hyperpolarization)

## Correct Answer

C) Becomes more positive (depolarization)

## Key Fact (generated by LLM)

In the context of a road traffic accident (RTA) with severe muscle injury leading to elevated potassium (K⁺) levels of 5.5 mEq/L, the resting membrane potential becomes more positive due to depolarization. This occurs because high extracellular potassium levels reduce the gradient between the intracellular and extracellular spaces, leading to a less negative resting membrane potential. As a result, the neuron or muscle cell becomes more excitable. 

## Why You Got It Wrong (generated by LLM)

The student incorrectly chose hyperpolarization, which is the opposite effect and occurs with decreased potassium levels or increased sodium levels. This mistake indicates a misunderstanding of how ion balance affects membrane potential
