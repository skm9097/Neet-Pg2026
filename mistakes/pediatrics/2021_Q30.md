## 2021_Q30 — pediatrics

- **Q:** A 10-year-old with seizures and prior fever/rash at 1 year. Diagnostic?
- **Got wrong:** B) MRI mesial temporal sclerosis
- **Correct:** A) IgG measles in CSF ✅
- **Key fact:** The correct answer is IgG measles in CSF, which indicates a past measles infection that can cause subacute sclerosing panencephalitis (SSPE), a rare but serious condition that can lead to seizures and neurological deterioration. The presence of IgG antibodies in the CSF suggests a chronic infection. This condition typically presents years after the initial measles infection, which fits with the timeline of the patient's fever and rash at 1 year of age. The patient's current seizures are a symptom of SSPE. 
- **Why wrong:** The student's answer, MRI mesial temporal sclerosis, is incorrect because it is more commonly associated with temporal lobe epilepsy, which does not fit with the patient's history of prior fever and rash.
