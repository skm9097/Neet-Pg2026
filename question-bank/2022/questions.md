## Microbiology

### Q1 — Microfilariae with sheathed tail + two nuclei

Microfilariae with sheathed tail and two nuclei at the tail suggests:

- A. *Wuchereria bancrofti*
- B. *Brugia malayi*
- C. *Loa loa*
- D. *Onchocerca volvulus*

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. *Brugia malayi*** — Sheathed; terminal & subterminal nuclei at the tail tip. *W. bancrofti* sheathed but tail nuclei absent.
</details>

### Q2 — Louse-borne typhus

A militant with rashes sparing palms/soles, fever; lice noted. Organism?

- A. *Rickettsia typhi*
- B. *Rickettsia prowazekii*
- C. *Rickettsia akari*
- D. *Rickettsia conorii*

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. *Rickettsia prowazekii*** — Epidemic typhus; body louse vector. Rash spares palms/soles. Brill–Zinsser is the recrudescent form.
</details>

## Biochemistry

### Q4 — Primaquine-induced haemolysis

Mediterranean patient develops haemolytic anaemia after primaquine. Affected pathway?

- A. Glycolysis
- B. Gluconeogenesis
- C. Hexose monophosphate pathway
- D. Luebering–Rapoport

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Hexose monophosphate pathway** — G6PD deficiency → NADPH ↓ → glutathione cannot be reduced → oxidative haemolysis.
</details>

### Q5 — Autosomal recessive disorder

Which is autosomal recessive?

- A. Huntington's
- B. Treacher Collins
- C. Cystic fibrosis
- D. Achondroplasia

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Cystic fibrosis** — CFTR gene mutation. Huntington, Treacher Collins, Achondroplasia are autosomal dominant.
</details>

## Ophthalmology

### Q6 — Sympathetic ophthalmitis

Penetrating eye injury; sympathetic ophthalmitis confirmed. Finding?

- A. Acute anterior uveitis
- B. Pars planitis
- C. Panuveitis
- D. Chronic anterior uveitis

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Panuveitis** — Granulomatous, bilateral. Earliest sign: retro-lental yellow-white spots (Dalen–Fuchs nodules).
</details>

### Q7 — Diabetic retinopathy — neovascularisation

Diabetic patient: VA 6/9, preretinal haemorrhages, neovascularisation at optic disc. Management?

- A. Focal laser
- B. Pan-retinal photocoagulation (PRP)
- C. Grid laser
- D. Scleral buckling

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. PRP** — Proliferative diabetic retinopathy (NVD/NVE) → PRP is standard.
</details>

## Surgery

### Q8 — Post-thyroidectomy perioral numbness

Day 3 post-thyroidectomy, patient develops perioral numbness. Investigation?

- A. Free T3, T4
- B. T3, T4, TSH
- C. Radioiodine scan
- D. Calcium, phosphate, PTH

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. Ca, PO₄, PTH** — Hypocalcaemia from inadvertent parathyroidectomy. Chvostek/Trousseau positive.
</details>

### Q9 — Saccular aneurysm rupture

Saccular aneurysm rupture causes?

- A. Subdural haemorrhage
- B. Subarachnoid haemorrhage
- C. Intracerebral haemorrhage
- D. Hydrocephalus

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Subarachnoid haemorrhage** — Berry aneurysm (Circle of Willis) rupture → SAH.
</details>

## Dermatology

### Q10 — High-risk HPV — anogenital warts

Multiple anogenital warts; biopsy shows squamous atypia. High-risk HPV?

- A. HPV 2
- B. HPV 18
- C. HPV 6
- D. HPV 11

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. HPV 18** — Along with HPV 16, cause most cervical/anogenital cancers. HPV 6 and 11 = low-risk (condyloma acuminatum).
</details>

## Pharmacology

### Q12 — Theophylline diuretic effect

COPD patient on theophylline; increased urine output next day. Mechanism?

- A. IL-10
- B. Histone deacetylase
- C. Adenosine A1
- D. β2 adrenergic receptors

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Adenosine A1** — Theophylline antagonises adenosine receptors → renal vasodilation & natriuresis.
</details>

### Q13 — Drug NOT causing pulmonary fibrosis

Which drug is **NOT** likely to cause pulmonary fibrosis?

- A. Metformin
- B. Methotrexate
- C. Bleomycin
- D. Nitrofurantoin

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Metformin** — Not associated with pulmonary fibrosis. The others are classic culprits ("BBM ANI": bleomycin, busulfan, MTX, amiodarone, nitrofurantoin, isoniazid).
</details>

## Orthopaedics

### Q14 — Fetus with multiple long-bone fractures

Intrauterine scan at 13 weeks: fetus with multiple long-bone fractures.

- A. Achondroplasia
- B. Osteogenesis imperfecta
- C. Cretinism
- D. Marfan syndrome

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Osteogenesis imperfecta** — Type II (perinatal lethal) presents in utero with fractures and short bowed limbs.
</details>

### Q15 — Post-RTA dyspnoea + petechiae

Post-RTA patient develops dyspnoea, petechiae over whole body, O₂ saturation drop 2 days later.

- A. Fat embolism
- B. Air embolism
- C. VTE
- D. Pulmonary hypertension

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Fat embolism syndrome** — Triad of respiratory, neurological, and petechial rash (Gurd's criteria). 24–72 hours after long bone fracture.
</details>

## Physiology

### Q18 — Nerve fibre susceptibility to pressure

Person develops paresis (no numbness) after sleeping with arm under head.

- A. C fibres more sensitive to pressure than A
- B. A fibres more sensitive to hypoxia than B
- C. A fibres more susceptible to pressure than C
- D. A fibres more susceptible to hypoxia than C

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. A fibres more susceptible to pressure than C** — Larger fibres are more pressure-sensitive; smaller (pain/temp C) are more anoxia-sensitive (Saturday night palsy).
</details>

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Subthalamic nuclei** — Contralateral STN lesion → hemiballismus.
</details>

### Q26 — Decreased liquor + small fetus

Woman at 26 weeks; fundal height = 24 wks; decreased liquor. Likely?

- A. Renal agenesis
- B. TEF

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

### Q27 — MgSO₄ toxicity post-cesarean

Type 1 diabetic on MgSO₄ post-cesarean for preeclampsia: delirium, RR 10, oliguria, absent knee reflexes. Cause?

- A. Magnesium sulfate toxicity
- B. DKA
- C. Eclampsia
- D. Diabetes insipidus

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. MgSO₄ toxicity** — Loss of patellar reflex (>10 mEq/L), RR<12, oliguria. Antidote: calcium gluconate.
</details>
