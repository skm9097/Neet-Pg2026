## Q1 — Anatomy / Cerebellum

A 6-year-old boy with chickenpox diagnosis has difficulty walking, broad-based gait, and slow speech. Loss of inhibitory signals to which structure is responsible?

- A. Golgi cell
- B. Basket cell
- C. Vestibular nuclei
- D. Deep cerebellar nuclei

**Correct Answer:** D. Deep cerebellar nuclei
**Explanation:** Purkinje cells inhibit the deep cerebellar nuclei through GABAergic inhibitory projections. Damage results in ataxia and loss of motor coordination.

## Q2 — Anatomy / Neural Tube Defects

A 1-day-old boy has spinal cord protruding, hairy patch, and pouch-like bulge on back. Which defect causes this?

- A. Non-fusion of anterior neuropore
- B. Non-fusion of posterior neuropore
- C. Non-fusion of both neuropores
- D. None of the above

**Correct Answer:** B. Non-fusion of posterior neuropore
**Explanation:** Rachischisis is due to non-closure of posterior neuropore, resulting in exposed spinal cord, open vertebra, and exposed skin with CSF leakage.

## Q3 — Anatomy / Sensory Testing

Neurological exam where patient identifies an object with eyes closed. What does this test?

- A. Posterior column
- B. Posterior spinocerebellar tract
- C. Spino-olivary tract
- D. Spinothalamic tract

**Correct Answer:** A. Posterior column
**Explanation:** Stereognosis — ability to recognize form and texture of unseen familiar object — functions through the posterior column-medial lemniscus system.

## Q4 — Anatomy / Inner Ear

Which inner ear structure is the endolymphatic sac?

- A. Macula
- B. Utricle
- C. Saccule
- D. Endolymphatic sac

**Correct Answer:** D. Endolymphatic sac
**Explanation:** Utricle and saccule give off small ducts which fuse to form a Y-shaped endolymphatic duct that traverses the vestibular aqueduct and terminates as the endolymphatic sac.

## Q5 — Anatomy / Sacral Hiatus

A 32-year-old male with sacral compression presents with lower back pain and groin sensory loss. Which structure passing through sacral hiatus is involved?

- A. S2 spinal nerve
- B. S3 spinal nerve
- C. S4 spinal nerve
- D. S5 spinal nerve

**Correct Answer:** D. S5 spinal nerve
**Explanation:** Structures passing through sacral hiatus are a pair of 5th sacral nerves, coccygeal nerves, and filum terminale external.

## Q6 — Anatomy / Gynecological Procedure

For culdocentesis, through which structure should the needle be inserted?

- A. Anterior fornix of vagina
- B. Posterior fornix of vagina
- C. Anterior wall of rectum
- D. Posterior wall of uterine body

**Correct Answer:** B. Posterior fornix of vagina
**Explanation:** Culdocentesis involves puncturing the posterior vaginal fornix to access the cul-de-sac for aspirating fluid from the pouch of Douglas.

## Q7 — Anatomy / Hirschsprung Disease

A 4-day-old baby with no defecation but normal feeding. After rectal exam, large fecal mass expelled. What condition is suspected?

- A. Imperforate anus
- B. Anal agenesis
- C. Anorectal agenesis
- D. Colonic aganglionosis

**Correct Answer:** D. Colonic aganglionosis
**Explanation:** Hirschsprung disease results from failure of formation of parasympathetic ganglia in colon/rectum wall, causing fecal retention and megacolon from absent peristalsis.

## Q8 — Anatomy / Hip Flexor

Which is the strongest hip flexor muscle?

- A. Sartorius
- B. Gluteus maximus
- C. Iliopsoas
- D. Pectineus

**Correct Answer:** C. Iliopsoas
**Explanation:** Iliopsoas is the chief flexor at the hip joint, assisted by Sartorius and Pectineus as accessory muscles.

## Q9 — Anatomy / Coronary Arteries

Cardiac catheterization reveals thrombus in the right coronary artery. Which is NOT a branch of the right coronary artery?

- A. Anterior interventricular artery
- B. SA nodal artery
- C. Posterior interventricular artery
- D. AV nodal artery

**Correct Answer:** A. Anterior interventricular artery
**Explanation:** The anterior interventricular artery is a branch of the left coronary artery, not the right coronary artery.

## Q10 — Anatomy / Nerve Injury

A cyclist with fracture of the surgical neck of humerus from a fall. Which nerve is most likely damaged?

- A. Musculocutaneous nerve
- B. Median nerve
- C. Ulnar nerve
- D. Axillary nerve

**Correct Answer:** D. Axillary nerve
**Explanation:** Axillary nerve is damaged by dislocation of shoulder or fracture of surgical neck of humerus, causing impaired shoulder abduction and sensory loss in the regimental badge area.
