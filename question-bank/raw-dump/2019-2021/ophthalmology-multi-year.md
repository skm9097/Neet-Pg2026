## Batch A — PrepLadder last-5-year PYQs in ophthalmology (prepladder.com)

Q: Which layer preserves the moisture and clarity of the cornea?
A: Endothelium

Q: Distinguishing characteristic of a fungal corneal ulcer?
A: Satellite lesion

Q: Additional row of eyelashes located behind the grey line?
A: Distichiasis

Q: Middle-aged woman with bilateral proptosis, restricted EOM, chemosis, hyperthyroidism. Diagnosis?
A: Grave's ophthalmopathy

Q: The photostress test distinguishes between?
A: Macula and optic nerve disease

Q: Shifting fluid sign is seen in?
A: Exudative retinal detachment

Q: Secondary deviation > primary deviation, forced duction test negative. Type of squint?
A: Paralytic squint

Q: Convergent squint in a small child is associated with?
A: Hyperopia

Q: Sympathetic ophthalmia occurs after?
A: Penetrating trauma

Q: Unilateral and bilateral abducent palsy – likely cause?
A: Cavernous sinus thrombosis

Q: Location of lesion in Wernicke's hemianopic pupil?
A: Optic tract

Q: Contact lens user with watering, redness, foreign body sensation. Diagnosis?
A: Giant papillary conjunctivitis

Q: Progressive painless vision loss with hard exudates. Probable cause?
A: Diabetes mellitus

Q: 15-year-old girl with myopic astigmatism non-adherent to spectacles. Management?
A: Spherical equivalent spectacles

Q: White pupillary reflex, enucleation, Flexner-Winterstein rosettes on biopsy. Diagnosis?
A: Retinoblastoma

Q: Gray-white fibrillary material on anterior lens surface. Diagnosis?
A: Pseudoexfoliation syndrome

Q: Sympathetic ophthalmitis after penetrating eye injury shows?
A: Panuveitis

Q: Diabetic with preretinal hemorrhages and NVD (neovascularization at disc), VA 6/9. Management?
A: Pan-retinal photocoagulation (PRP)

Q: Treatment for simple myopic astigmatism (−1.00 D astigmatism at 180°)?
A: −1.00 DC × 180 Degree
