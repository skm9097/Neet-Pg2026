## Batch A — PrepLadder last-5-year PYQs in radiology (prepladder.com)

Q: Mickey Mouse sign at saphenofemoral junction — name for USG appearance?
A: Mickey Mouse sign (common femoral vein, artery, great saphenous vein in transverse view)

Q: Cyanotic CHD with plethoric lung fields?
A: TAPVC (Total Anomalous Pulmonary Venous Connection)

Q: CT brain with hyperdensity in sulci/cisterns, sudden severe headache. Diagnosis?
A: Subarachnoid hemorrhage

Q: TB spine — cold abscess tracking to psoas muscle. Identified muscle?
A: Psoas muscle (muscle 2)

Q: Acute pancreatitis with bilateral diffuse pulmonary infiltrates. Diagnosis?
A: ARDS

Q: Pituitary fossa lesion → visual field defect?
A: Bitemporal hemianopia

Q: Lap-belt injury with horizontal vertebral fracture?
A: Chance fracture

Q: MRCP with fusiform CBD dilatation. Diagnosis?
A: Choledochal cyst (Todani Type I)

Q: Lung cavity with air-fluid level and foul sputum?
A: Lung abscess

Q: Small scattered pancreatic calcifications on X-ray?
A: Chronic calcific pancreatitis

## Batch B — PrepLadder image-based radiology MCQs (prepladder.com)

Q: X-rays are produced by?
A: Sudden deceleration of moving electrons (Bremsstrahlung)

Q: Post-cranial surgery CT showing low density within skull. Appearance due to?
A: Air (pneumocephalus)

Q: Thermoluminescent dosimeter — correct statement?
A: Worn under (not over) the lead apron

Q: PA chest X-ray rib level at which the aorta knuckle is seen?
A: 5th Posterior rib

Q: Hyperlucent left lung with displaced mediastinum. Diagnosis?
A: Bullous emphysema (if unilateral hyperlucency)

Q: Post-trauma CXR with collapsed lung?
A: Pneumothorax with ipsilateral lung collapse

Q: COPD patient with decreased breath sounds — pleural cause?
A: Pleural effusion

Q: CHF patient with pleural opacity mistaken for lung mass?
A: Encysted (loculated) pleural effusion (may mimic mass)

Q: Contrast CT with fever and pleuritic pain — split pleura sign?
A: Empyema

Q: Smoking history with upper lobe opacity — Golden S sign indicates?
A: Right upper lobe collapse (central obstructing mass)

Q: Miliary nodules on CXR with constitutional symptoms?
A: Miliary tuberculosis

Q: Double density sign in paranasal sinuses on Caldwell view?
A: Allergic fungal sinusitis

Q: Nosebleed following fall — fracture of?
A: Chevallet fracture (of nasal septum)

Q: Right ear bleeding post-head trauma. Type and side of fracture?
A: Longitudinal fracture of right temporal bone

## Batch C — PrepLadder INI-CET radiology PYQs (prepladder.com)

Q: Dilated haustrated colon with bird's beak deformity. Diagnosis?
A: Large bowel obstruction due to sigmoid volvulus

Q: Kidney mass on CT with spoke-wheel vascularity and central scar. Diagnosis?
A: Renal oncocytoma (vs. RCC)

Q: Double bleb sign on early pregnancy USG?
A: Yolk sac and amniotic sac

Q: CT number of which substance is 0?
A: Water

Q: Preferred contrast agent in renal failure?
A: USG contrast (gadolinium contraindicated — NSF risk; iodinated also risky)

Q: Endovascular aneurysm management by interventional radiologist?
A: Coiling

Q: Dilated bowel loops with haustrations = ?
A: Large bowel obstruction

Q: Traumatic brain injury with shear at grey-white matter junction on diffusion MRI?
A: Diffuse axonal injury

## Batch D — PG Blazer radiology MCQs (pgblazer.com)

Q: Threshold radiation dose for haematological syndrome?
A: 1 Gy

Q: Best investigation for detecting bone metastases?
A: Bone scan (Tc-99m MDP scintigraphy)

Q: Preferred contrast for prevention of contrast nephropathy?
A: Low osmolar (non-ionic) contrast agent

Q: "Egg on side" appearance on CXR is characteristic of?
A: Transposition of the Great Arteries (TGA)

Q: Most ionizing radiation among X-ray, gamma, alpha, beta?
A: Alpha rays (highest ionization per unit path, lowest penetration)

Q: Substances coating CT scan room walls for radiation shielding?
A: Lead

Q: Tumor showing calcification on CT?
A: Meningioma (also ependymoma, craniopharyngioma)

Q: Most preferred route for cerebral angiography?
A: Transfemoral route (Seldinger technique)
