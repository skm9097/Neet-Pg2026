### Q1 — Thickened nerve identification *(image-based)*

Leprosy patient: thickened nerve marked by arrow at angle of jaw. Nerve identified?

- A. Great auricular nerve
- B. Lesser occipital nerve
- C. Transverse cervical nerve
- D. Greater occipital nerve

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Great auricular nerve** — Great auricular nerve (C2, C3) is the most commonly enlarged peripheral nerve in leprosy; winds around posterior border of SCM at Erb's point. Transverse cervical nerve runs below it at the same level.

</details>

### Q2 — Broca's area location

Broca's area (motor speech area) is situated in the:

- A. Inferior frontal gyrus
- B. Superior temporal gyrus
- C. Angular gyrus
- D. Postcentral gyrus

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Inferior frontal gyrus** — Broca's area (Brodmann 44, 45) in dominant inferior frontal gyrus (pars triangularis, pars opercularis). Wernicke's area = superior temporal gyrus. Angular gyrus = reading comprehension.

</details>

### Q3 — SVA column — NOT a member

Which cranial nerve nucleus is NOT part of the special visceral afferent (SVA) column?

- A. Facial nerve
- B. Trochlear nerve
- C. Vagus nerve
- D. Glossopharyngeal nerve

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Trochlear nerve** — Trochlear nucleus belongs to the general somatic efferent (GSE) column; it innervates the superior oblique muscle. SVA column (taste): facial (VII), glossopharyngeal (IX), vagus (X). GSE column (extraocular + tongue muscles): III, IV, VI, XII.

</details>

### Q4 — Interchondral rib joint type

The joint between the attachment of the 8th and 9th rib to the 7th rib is a:

- A. Fibrocartilaginous joint
- B. Symphyseal joint
- C. Synovial joint
- D. Fibrous joint

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Synovial joint** — Interchondral joints (7th–10th ribs) = synovial type; their presence allows thoracic cavity expansion during inspiration. Note: interchondral joint between 9th and 10th cartilages is never synovial. Costochondral joints = primary cartilaginous (synchondrosis).

</details>

### Q5 — Last fontanelle to close

Which is the last fontanelle to close?

- A. Anterior
- B. Posterior
- C. Sphenoidal
- D. Mastoid

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Anterior** — Anterior (bregmatic) fontanelle closes last, by 18–24 months. Posterior = 2–3 months; Sphenoidal = 2–3 months; Mastoid = 12 months. Delayed closure in hypothyroidism, rickets, Down syndrome.

</details>

### Q6 — Sibson's fascia — incorrect statement

Which among the following is incorrect regarding Sibson's fascia?

- A. Attached to the inner border of the 2nd rib
- B. Covers the apical part of the lung
- C. Strengthened by fibers from scalene muscles
- D. Subclavian vessels pass above (on top of) it

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Attached to the inner border of the 2nd rib** — Sibson's fascia (suprapleural membrane) is attached to the inner border of the **1st** rib (not 2nd), and posteriorly to the transverse process of C7. It covers the lung apex, is reinforced by scalenus minimus fibers, and has subclavian vessels lying on its superior surface.

</details>

### Q7 — Aortic hiatus — structure NOT transmitted

The structure NOT passing through the aortic opening of the diaphragm is:

- A. Aorta
- B. Azygous vein
- C. Thoracic duct
- D. Vagal trunk

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. Vagal trunk** — Aortic hiatus (T12) transmits: aorta, thoracic duct, azygos vein, hemiazygos vein. Vagal trunks pass through the **oesophageal hiatus** (T10) along with the oesophagus and left gastric vessels.

</details>

### Q8 — Hard palate histology

The hard palate contains:

- A. Keratinised epithelium, submucosa, minor salivary glands
- B. Keratinised epithelium, submucosa, no salivary glands
- C. Non-keratinised epithelium, submucosa, minor salivary glands
- D. Non-keratinised epithelium, absent submucosa, minor salivary glands

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Keratinised epithelium, submucosa, minor salivary glands** — Hard palate is lined by ortho/parakeratinised stratified squamous epithelium (same as gingiva), has a submucosa, and contains minor mucous salivary glands. Soft palate has non-keratinised epithelium.

</details>

### Q9 — Lumbrical function at MCP joint *(image-based)*

Image shows palmar hand muscles. What is the function of the lumbrical (muscle A) at the metacarpophalangeal (MCP) joint?

- A. Flexion
- B. Extension
- C. Adduction
- D. Abduction

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Flexion** — Lumbricals flex the MCP joint (via attachment to radial side of extensor expansion) and simultaneously extend the IP joints. In ulnar nerve injury (medial two lumbricals lost) → claw hand: MCP hyperextension + IP flexion.

</details>

### Q10 — Tensor of vocal cords

Which is the tensor of vocal cords?

- A. Cricothyroid
- B. Lateral cricoarytenoid
- C. Thyroarytenoid
- D. Posterior cricoarytenoid

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Cricothyroid** — Functional roles: Cricothyroid = tensor; Thyroarytenoid = relaxer; Posterior cricoarytenoid = only abductor (safety muscle); Lateral cricoarytenoid = adductor + whispering muscle; Vocalis = modulator. All supplied by recurrent laryngeal nerve except cricothyroid (superior laryngeal nerve).

</details>

### Q11 — Nerve of 2nd pharyngeal arch

The nerve of the second pharyngeal arch is:

- A. Glossopharyngeal nerve
- B. Trigeminal nerve
- C. Facial nerve
- D. Vagal nerve

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Facial nerve** — Arch nerves: 1st = mandibular (V3); 2nd = facial (VII); 3rd = glossopharyngeal (IX); 4th = superior laryngeal (X); 6th = recurrent laryngeal (X). Arch 2 muscles (stapedius, stylohyoid, posterior digastric, muscles of facial expression) all supplied by facial nerve.

</details>

### Q12 — Iliopubic tract — true statement

Which of the following statements is true about the iliopubic tract?

- A. It is a thickening of the lumbar fascia
- B. It lies anterior to Poupart's (inguinal) ligament
- C. It inserts into the pubic tubercle in front of the conjoint tendon
- D. Its fibers fan out with fascia iliaca laterally

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. Its fibers fan out with fascia iliaca laterally** — Iliopubic tract = thickening of **transversalis fascia** (not lumbar fascia), lying immediately **posterior** to inguinal ligament. Medially it inserts into pubic tubercle and pectineal line **behind** the conjoint tendon. Laterally its fibers blend with fascia iliaca. Important landmark in laparoscopic inguinal hernia repair.

</details>

### Q13 — Ureteric bud origin

Ureteric bud develops from:

- A. Mesonephric (Wolffian) duct
- B. Metanephros
- C. Pronephros
- D. Urogenital sinus

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Mesonephric (Wolffian) duct** — Ureteric bud sprouts from mesonephric duct → induces metanephric mesoderm → forms ureter, renal pelvis, calyces, collecting tubules. Failure of ureteric bud induction → renal agenesis. Mesonephric duct also gives rise to: epididymis, vas deferens, seminal vesicle, ejaculatory duct, trigone of bladder.

</details>

### Q14 — Heart development — beating onset

The heart starts to beat in which week of development?

- A. 2nd week
- B. 4th week
- C. 6th week
- D. 10th week

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. 4th week** — Cardiac tube begins beating around day 22–23 (4th week). Heart is the first organ to become functional. Cardiac loop forms by day 28. Cardiac septation complete by week 7–8.

</details>
