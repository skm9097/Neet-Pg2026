### Q1 — Atlanto-axial rotation

Which joint permits looking right and left?

- A. Atlanto-occipital
- B. Atlanto-axial
- C. C2–C3
- D. C3–C4

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Atlanto-axial** — Pivot joint; rotation of head ("no" movement). Atlanto-occipital = flexion-extension ("yes").
</details>

### Q3 — Atlanto-axial joint — rotation

The movements at which joint permit a person to look to the right and left?

- A. Atlanto-occipital
- B. Atlanto-axial
- C. C2–C3
- D. C3–C4

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Atlanto-axial** — Atlanto-axial joint allows rotation (≈40°) = "No" movement (looking left/right). Three synovial joints: 1 median (dens + anterior arch + transverse ligament) + 2 lateral. Atlanto-occipital = "Yes" movement (nodding, flexion/extension).

</details>

### Q4 — Right coronary artery — false statement

All of the following are true about the right coronary artery EXCEPT:

- A. Its diameter is less than the left coronary artery
- B. It arises from the anterior aortic sinus
- C. It gives rise to the circumflex artery
- D. Right conal artery is one of its branches

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. It gives rise to the circumflex artery** — Circumflex artery is a branch of the **left** coronary artery. RCA branches: SA nodal (55%), right conal, right ventricular marginals, posterior interventricular (PDA in right-dominant = 85%), AV nodal. RCA arises from right (anterior) aortic sinus; its diameter is usually less than the LCA.

</details>

### Q5 — Omphalocele — basis of formation

What is the basis for the formation of omphalocele?

- A. Excessive length of the intestine
- B. Physiological hernia failing to reduce
- C. Herniation of liver through abdominal wall
- D. Herniation of umbilical cord contents

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Physiological hernia failing to reduce** — Physiological umbilical herniation occurs at week 6 (midgut too large for abdominal cavity); returns by week 10–12. Failure → omphalocele (sac-covered, central defect ≥4 cm, contains bowel ± liver). Gastroschisis = no sac, to the right of umbilicus, bowel only.

</details>

### Q6 — Cerebral white matter fiber type *(image-based)*

Image shows horizontal section of cerebral hemispheres with a fiber bundle marked by arrow (internal capsule). Fiber type?

- A. Short association fibers
- B. Long association fibers
- C. Projection fibers
- D. Commissural fibers

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Projection fibers** — Internal capsule = projection fibers (connecting cortex to subcortical structures). Association fibers connect cortical areas within the same hemisphere (short = U-fibers, long = uncinate, arcuate, cingulum). Commissural fibers cross midline (corpus callosum, anterior commissure).

</details>

### Q8 — Calot's triangle — boundary EXCEPT

Boundaries of Calot's triangle are formed by all EXCEPT:

- A. Common hepatic duct
- B. Cystic duct
- C. Cystic artery
- D. Gall bladder

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. Gall bladder** — Calot's original triangle: medially = common hepatic duct, inferiorly = cystic duct, superiorly = cystic artery. Gall bladder is NOT a boundary. Content: branching of cystic artery + lymph node of Lund (sentinel LN of gallbladder). Note: modern hepatobiliary triangle replaces cystic artery with inferior surface of liver.

</details>

### Q13 — Cantlie line (cholecysto-vena caval line)

Cholecysto-vena caval line (Cantlie line) separates which of the following?

- A. Gallbladder and inferior vena cava
- B. Porta hepatis and inferior vena cava
- C. Caudate lobe and quadrangular lobe
- D. Right and left lobes of the liver

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. Right and left lobes of the liver** — Cantlie's line: imaginary line from midpoint of gallbladder fossa (inferior border) to midpoint of IVC; divides liver into right and left hemilivers (anatomical lobes). Contains middle hepatic vein. Basis of segmental liver anatomy (Couinaud).

</details>

### Q14 — Skin — incorrect statement

Which of the following is incorrect regarding skin?

- A. Dermis is derived from ectoderm
- B. Skin constitutes 15% of total body weight
- C. Epidermis is derived from ectoderm
- D. Dermis is made up of type I and type III collagen

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

</details>

### Q17 — Foot drop — nerve damaged

Foot drop is caused by damage to:

- A. Common peroneal nerve
- B. Femoral nerve
- C. Tibial nerve
- D. Sciatic nerve

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Common peroneal nerve** — Common peroneal nerve (L4, L5, S1, S2) winds around fibular neck → injury here → foot drop (loss of dorsiflexion + eversion) + sensory loss over dorsum of foot. Most vulnerable peripheral nerve due to its superficial course. Tibial nerve injury → loss of plantarflexion/inversion.

</details>

### Q18 — De Quervain's tenosynovitis — true statement

Which of the following is TRUE regarding De Quervain's tenosynovitis?

- A. Fingers are held in mild extension
- B. It affects abductor pollicis longus (APL) and extensor pollicis brevis (EPB)
- C. Most common involvement is the index finger
- D. Treatment is always surgical

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. It affects APL and EPB** — De Quervain's = stenosing tenosynovitis of 1st dorsal compartment tendons (APL + EPB). Thumb is most commonly involved (not index). Most patients respond to medical management (splinting, NSAIDs, steroid injection); surgery (release of 1st dorsal compartment) only for refractory cases. Finkelstein's test is diagnostic.

</details>
