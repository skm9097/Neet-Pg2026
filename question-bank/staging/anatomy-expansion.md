
### Q21 — Cerebellum / Purkinje cell inhibition

6-year-old with ataxia, broad-based gait, slow speech post-chickenpox. Loss of inhibitory signals to which structure?

- A. Golgi cell
- B. Basket cell
- C. Vestibular nuclei
- D. Deep cerebellar nuclei

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. Deep cerebellar nuclei** — Purkinje cells (GABAergic) tonically inhibit deep cerebellar nuclei (dentate, emboliform, globose, fastigial). Loss of inhibition → cerebellar ataxia. Post-varicella cerebellar ataxia is common and usually self-limiting.

</details>

### Q22 — Neural tube / Rachischisis

1-day-old with spinal cord protruding through open vertebral defect, hairy patch on back — defect cause?

- A. Non-fusion of anterior neuropore
- B. Non-fusion of posterior neuropore
- C. Non-fusion of both neuropores
- D. Failure of caudal cell mass regression

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Non-fusion of posterior neuropore** — Posterior neuropore closes by day 27. Failure → rachischisis (open spina bifida). Anterior neuropore closure failure = anencephaly. Spina bifida occulta = bony defect only, no neural tissue exposed.

</details>

### Q24 — Inner ear / Endolymphatic sac

The endolymphatic sac is formed by fusion of ductules from which structures?

- A. Cochlea and saccule
- B. Utricle and saccule
- C. Semicircular canals only
- D. Cochlea and semicircular canals

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Utricle and saccule** — Utricle and saccule each give off small ductules that fuse to form Y-shaped endolymphatic duct → traverses vestibular aqueduct → terminates as endolymphatic sac in posterior cranial fossa. Menière's disease = endolymphatic hydrops.

</details>

### Q27 — Neonatal anatomy / Hirschsprung disease

4-day-old baby, no defecation, normal feeding, large fecal mass expelled after rectal exam — diagnosis?

- A. Imperforate anus
- B. Anal agenesis
- C. Anorectal agenesis
- D. Colonic aganglionosis (Hirschsprung disease)

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. Colonic aganglionosis** — Hirschsprung disease: failure of neural crest cell migration → absent Auerbach/Meissner plexus in rectosigmoid → functional obstruction → megacolon proximal to aganglionic segment. Rectal biopsy: absent ganglion cells + hypertrophied nerve trunks.

</details>

### Q28 — Hip flexors / Strongest

Strongest hip flexor muscle?

- A. Sartorius
- B. Gluteus maximus
- C. Iliopsoas
- D. Pectineus

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Iliopsoas** — Iliacus (L2-L4) + psoas major (L1-L3) → strongest hip flexor. Sartorius and rectus femoris are accessory. Tested with straight leg raise. Psoas abscess → pain on passive hip extension (psoas sign).

</details>

### Q29 — Coronary anatomy / NOT branch of RCA

Which vessel is NOT a branch of the right coronary artery?

- A. Anterior interventricular artery (LAD)
- B. SA nodal artery (55% cases)
- C. Posterior interventricular artery (PDA)
- D. AV nodal artery

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Anterior interventricular artery (LAD)** — LAD is the largest branch of the LEFT anterior descending artery. RCA branches: SA nodal (55%), RV marginals, posterior interventricular (PDA in right-dominant heart — 85%), AV nodal.

</details>

### Q30 — Nerve injury / Surgical neck of humerus

Fracture of surgical neck of humerus — most commonly injured nerve?

- A. Musculocutaneous nerve
- B. Median nerve
- C. Ulnar nerve
- D. Axillary nerve

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. Axillary nerve** — Axillary nerve (C5, C6) winds around surgical neck of humerus in quadrangular space; injury → loss of deltoid (shoulder abduction 15–90°) + teres minor + sensory loss over regimental badge area (lateral arm).

</details>
