### Q17 — Altitude sickness / NOT used in management

Which drug is NOT used in acute mountain sickness management?

- A. Acetazolamide
- B. Dexamethasone
- C. Digoxin
- D. Nifedipine

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Digoxin** — Altitude sickness management: descent, supplemental O2, acetazolamide (carbonic anhydrase inhibitor → metabolic acidosis → stimulates breathing), dexamethasone (HACE), nifedipine (HAPE). Digoxin is not used.

</details>

### Q18 — Prolactin / Peak secretion timing

Prolactin level is highest at which time point?

- A. At the time of delivery
- B. 24 hours after delivery
- C. 48 hours after delivery
- D. On day 5 postpartum

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. 24 hours after delivery** — Serum prolactin peaks at 24 hours after delivery. During pregnancy, prolactin rises progressively but milk production is suppressed by estrogen/progesterone. Suckling then maintains prolactin levels.

</details>

### Q19 — JVP waveform / c wave

JVP "c" wave represents?

- A. Atrial contraction
- B. Tricuspid valve bulge into RA during isovolumetric ventricular contraction
- C. Passive atrial filling
- D. Atrial relaxation

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Tricuspid valve closure + bulge into RA** — "a" = atrial contraction; "c" = tricuspid closure + bulge; "x" = atrial relaxation + tricuspid descent; "v" = passive venous filling; "y" = tricuspid opens, RA empties. "c" wave absent in tricuspid regurgitation.

</details>

### Q20 — Renal / Glomerular charge selectivity

Which molecule in the glomerular basement membrane provides charge-selective filtration barrier?

- A. Type IV collagen
- B. Laminin
- C. Heparan sulfate proteoglycan
- D. Fibronectin

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Heparan sulfate proteoglycan** — Negatively charged GAG (agrin, perlecan) in GBM repels negatively charged plasma proteins (albumin). Heparan sulfate depletion → proteinuria in diabetic nephropathy.

</details>

### Q21 — Coagulation / Warfarin mechanism

Warfarin decreases gamma-carboxyglutamate (Gla) residues in which coagulation factor?

- A. Factor I (fibrinogen)
- B. Factor II (prothrombin)
- C. Factor V
- D. Factor VIII

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Factor II (prothrombin)** — Warfarin inhibits vitamin K epoxide reductase → impairs gamma-carboxylation of factors II, VII, IX, X, Protein C, Protein S. Factor II (prothrombin) is the key clotting factor affected.

</details>

### Q22 — Nerve / Saturday night palsy — fibres

Saturday night palsy (radial nerve compression) — paresis without numbness due to?

- A. C fibres (pain/temperature) are more resistant to pressure
- B. A-alpha fibres are more susceptible to pressure than C fibres
- C. A fibres (motor/touch) are more susceptible to pressure than C fibres
- D. Autonomic fibres are selectively affected

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. A fibres more susceptible to pressure than C fibres** — Pressure blocks large myelinated A fibres (motor, proprioception, light touch) first; smaller unmyelinated C fibres (pain/temperature) are more resistant. Hence paresis without analgesia.

</details>

### Q23 — Homeostasis / Baroreceptor reflex

Baroreceptor reflex is an example of which type of feedback?

- A. Positive feedback
- B. Negative feedback
- C. Feed-forward control
- D. Anticipatory reflex

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Negative feedback** — Baroreceptors sense increased BP → reflex bradycardia + vasodilation to restore BP to setpoint. Negative feedback maintains homeostasis. Exception: parturition, LH surge = positive feedback.

</details>

### Q24 — Cell signalling / Paracrine

Cells that signal neighbouring cells through local diffusion of signalling molecules?

- A. Endocrine signalling
- B. Autocrine signalling
- C. Paracrine signalling
- D. Juxtacrine signalling

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Paracrine signalling** — Acts on neighbouring cells via local diffusion (short range). Autocrine = same cell as source. Endocrine = distant cells via bloodstream. Juxtacrine = direct cell-to-cell contact.

</details>

### Q25 — Micturition / Cystometrogram

Cystometrogram segment that reflects Laplace's law (tension vs pressure relationship)?

- A. Segment Ia (initial pressure rise)
- B. Segment Ib (flat compliance segment)
- C. Segment II (active filling)
- D. Segment III (voiding contraction)

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Segment Ib** — Bladder accommodates large volumes with little pressure change (high compliance); Laplace's law: as radius increases, wall tension increases proportionally → pressure stays flat. Segment Ib = high compliance phase.

</details>

### Q26 — Thermoregulation / Fever — what does NOT occur during rise

During fever (rising phase of resetting thermostat) — which does NOT occur?

- A. Shivering
- B. Vasoconstriction
- C. Sweating
- D. Increased metabolism

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Sweating** — During fever rise: hypothalamic setpoint ↑ → shivering (generate heat), vasoconstriction (conserve heat), piloerection. Sweating occurs when fever breaks (defervescence) as heat is dissipated.

</details>

### Q27 — Renal / Myogenic autoregulation mechanism

In myogenic autoregulation of GFR, afferent arteriole responds to increased pressure by?

- A. Activating ATP-sensitive K+ channels
- B. Opening stretch-activated Ca2+ channels → vasoconstriction
- C. Releasing renin
- D. Activating macula densa sensing

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Stretch-activated Ca2+ channels → vasoconstriction** — Increased perfusion pressure → stretch of afferent arteriole wall → Ca2+ influx → myosin light chain phosphorylation → vasoconstriction → maintains GFR constant (autoregulation range: MAP 80–180 mmHg).

</details>

### Q28 — Posture / Decerebrate vs decorticate

Decorticate posturing is characterized by?

- A. Extension of all four limbs
- B. Flexion of upper limbs + extension of lower limbs
- C. Extension of upper limbs + flexion of lower limbs
- D. Flaccid paralysis

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Flexion of upper limbs + extension of lower limbs** — Decorticate = corticospinal lesion above midbrain → FLEX upper, extend lower (remember: "de-cortic-ate = Flex toward core"). Decerebrate = midbrain/pons lesion → extension of all four limbs (worse prognosis).

</details>

### Q29 — Blood banking / Preservation duration

RBC preservation duration: ACD vs CPDA-1?

- A. 14 days vs 28 days
- B. 21 days vs 35 days
- C. 28 days vs 42 days
- D. 35 days vs 56 days

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. 21 days (ACD) vs 35 days (CPDA-1)** — ACD (acid citrate dextrose) = 21 days at 4°C. CPDA-1 (citrate phosphate dextrose adenine) = 35 days. Additive solutions (AS-1, AS-3) = 42 days. Storage lesion: ↓2,3-DPG, ↑K+, hemolysis.

</details>

### Q30 — Lung volumes / FRC formula

Functional residual capacity (FRC) equals?

- A. TV + IRV (inspiratory reserve)
- B. ERV + RV (expiratory reserve volume + residual volume)
- C. TLC − IRV
- D. IC + ERV

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. ERV + RV** — FRC = volume in lungs at end of normal expiration (passive). FRC = ERV + RV. FRC cannot be measured by spirometry (RV can't be exhaled); measured by helium dilution, nitrogen washout, or body plethysmography.

</details>
