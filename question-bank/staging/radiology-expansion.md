### Q16 — USG / Saphenofemoral junction

Mickey Mouse sign on Doppler USG at saphenofemoral junction represents?

- A. Common femoral artery, saphenous artery, femoral vein
- B. Common femoral vein, femoral artery, great saphenous vein
- C. Superficial femoral artery, deep femoral, femoral vein
- D. Long saphenous vein, short saphenous vein, popliteal vein

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Common femoral vein, femoral artery, great saphenous vein** — In cross-sectional USG: CFV (large), CFA (pulsatile) and GSV (at junction) form the Mickey Mouse appearance; used to evaluate saphenofemoral junction competence.

</details>

### Q17 — CXR / Cyanotic CHD with plethoric lungs

Cyanotic congenital heart disease with plethoric (increased) lung fields on CXR?

- A. Tetralogy of Fallot
- B. Pulmonary atresia
- C. TAPVC (Total Anomalous Pulmonary Venous Connection)
- D. Tricuspid atresia

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. TAPVC** — Cyanosis + increased pulmonary vascularity (plethora). ToF = oligemic lungs. TAPVC: all 4 pulmonary veins drain anomalously → right heart overload → pulmonary plethora despite cyanosis.

</details>

### Q18 — CT brain / Subarachnoid hemorrhage

Sudden severe headache, CT brain shows hyperdensity in sulci and basal cisterns — diagnosis?

- A. Subdural hematoma
- B. Epidural hematoma
- C. Subarachnoid hemorrhage
- D. Intracerebral hemorrhage

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Subarachnoid hemorrhage** — Hyperdense blood in subarachnoid space (cisterns/sulci) on non-contrast CT. "Thunderclap headache." Cause: ruptured berry aneurysm most common. CT sensitivity >95% in first 6 hours.

</details>

Spinal TB with cold abscess tracking along psoas sheath — imaging modality of choice?

- A. X-ray spine
- B. CT myelography
- C. MRI spine with contrast

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. MRI spine with contrast** — Best for soft tissue detail; shows vertebral body destruction, disc involvement, epidural/paravertebral abscess, cord compression, psoas abscess tracking. CT = better for bony detail.

### Q20 — CT / Acute pancreatitis + ARDS

Acute pancreatitis with bilateral diffuse pulmonary infiltrates — diagnosis?

- A. Pneumonia
- B. Pulmonary edema from IV fluids
- C. ARDS (Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome)
- D. Pleural effusion

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. ARDS** — Acute pancreatitis releases cytokines/phospholipase A2 → systemic inflammatory response → diffuse alveolar damage → bilateral infiltrates without elevated wedge pressure = ARDS. Berlin definition: PaO₂/FiO₂ <300.

</details>

### Q24 — X-ray / Bremsstrahlung radiation

X-rays are produced by which process?

- A. Characteristic radiation from K-shell electrons
- B. Sudden deceleration of fast electrons (Bremsstrahlung)
- C. Nuclear fission
- D. Radioactive decay

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Bremsstrahlung (braking radiation)** — Electrons decelerated by nucleus → energy released as X-ray photons. Also: characteristic X-rays when inner-shell electrons are ejected and outer-shell electrons fill in.

</details>

### Q27 — CT/MRI / Golden S sign

Smoking history, right upper lobe collapse, Golden S sign on CT — what does it indicate?

- A. Post-inflammatory fibrosis
- B. Central obstructing mass causing lobar collapse
- C. Hilar lymphadenopathy

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Central obstructing mass** — Golden S sign = concave lateral border (collapsed lobe) + convex medial border (central mass) forming an S-shape. Pathognomonic of central bronchogenic carcinoma causing obstructive collapse.

### Q29 — CT / Diffuse axonal injury

Post-trauma MRI brain showing shear injuries at grey-white matter junction on diffusion-weighted imaging?

- A. Contusion
- B. Diffuse axonal injury (DAI)
- C. Subdural hematoma
- D. Coup-contrecoup injury

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Diffuse axonal injury (DAI)** — Rotational acceleration-deceleration → axonal shearing at grey-white matter junction, corpus callosum, brainstem. DWI most sensitive (restricted diffusion). GCS low despite normal CT in early DAI.

</details>

### Q31 — CT / Hounsfield units — water

CT number (Hounsfield Unit) of water is?

- A. −1000 HU
- B. 0 HU
- C. +40 HU
- D. +1000 HU

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. 0 HU** — Hounsfield scale: water = 0; air = −1000; fat = −100 to −50; soft tissue = +20 to +80; bone = +400 to +1000. Calcification/dense bone = +1000.

</details>

### Q32 — Radiology / Radiation dose — haematological syndrome

Threshold radiation dose for acute haematological syndrome?

- A. 0.5 Gy
- B. 1 Gy
- C. 6 Gy
- D. 10 Gy

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. 1 Gy** — Hematopoietic syndrome threshold: 1 Gy (bone marrow depression). GI syndrome: 6 Gy. CNS/cardiovascular syndrome: >20 Gy. LD50/60 in humans ≈ 3–4 Gy (without medical support).

</details>

### Q33 — Nuclear medicine / Bone metastases

Best investigation for detecting bone metastases (skeletal survey)?

- A. Plain X-rays
- B. Bone scan (Tc-99m MDP)
- C. MRI spine
- D. PET-CT

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Bone scan (Tc-99m MDP scintigraphy)** — Most sensitive for osteoblastic metastases; whole-body survey in single scan. PET-CT better for osteolytic lesions and FDG-avid tumors. MRI best for spinal cord compression.

</details>

### Q34 — CXR / Egg-on-side appearance

"Egg on its side" appearance on CXR — characteristic of?

- A. Total anomalous pulmonary venous connection
- B. Transposition of the great arteries (TGA)
- C. Truncus arteriosus
- D. Ebstein's anomaly

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. TGA** — Narrow mediastinum (aorta and PA in AP relationship, not side-by-side) + enlarged cardiac shadow = egg-on-side. Prominent right heart. Treat with balloon atrial septostomy; definitive = arterial switch operation (Jatene).

</details>

### Q35 — Radiation / Ionizing power of particles

Most ionizing radiation per unit path length among: X-ray, gamma, alpha, beta?

- A. X-rays
- B. Gamma rays
- C. Alpha particles
- D. Beta particles

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Alpha particles** — Highest ionization density (LET) but lowest penetration (stopped by paper/skin). Gamma/X-rays = lowest ionization density, highest penetration. Beta = intermediate. Important for Bragg peak radiotherapy.

</details>

### Q36 — Interventional radiology / Cerebral angiography

Most preferred route for diagnostic cerebral angiography?

- A. Radial artery (transradial)
- B. Femoral artery (transfemoral — Seldinger technique)
- C. Brachial artery
- D. Carotid artery direct puncture

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Transfemoral — Seldinger technique** — Traditional gold standard; common femoral artery puncture, guidewire, catheter introduced → selective catheterization of cerebral vessels. Transradial increasingly popular for reduced access site complications.

</details>
