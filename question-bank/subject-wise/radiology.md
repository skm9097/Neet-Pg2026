### Q1 — Mickey Mouse sign at saphenofemoral junction

USG showing common femoral vein, common femoral artery, and great saphenous vein at the saphenofemoral junction is called:

- A. Mickey Mouse sign
- B. String sign
- C. Tillaux sign
- D. Stamers sign

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Mickey Mouse sign** — Three round structures visible in transverse view; landmark for SFJ. Useful for varicose vein assessment, GSV cannulation.
</details>

### Q2 — Plethoric lung fields in cyanotic CHD

Plethoric lung fields are seen in:

- A. TAPVC (Total Anomalous Pulmonary Venous Connection)
- B. Tricuspid atresia
- C. Ebstein anomaly
- D. Tetralogy of Fallot

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. TAPVC** — Cyanotic CHD with increased pulmonary blood flow → plethoric (hyper-vascular) lungs. "Snowman" sign on CXR (supracardiac type). TOF/tricuspid atresia/Ebstein = decreased pulmonary flow (oligaemic lungs).
</details>

### Q3 — Subarachnoid haemorrhage on CT

CT brain with neck stiffness and severe sudden headache showing hyperdensity in sulci/cisterns. Diagnosis?

- A. Meningitis
- B. Subarachnoid haemorrhage
- C. Intraparenchymal haemorrhage
- D. Hemorrhagic stroke

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Subarachnoid haemorrhage** — Hyperdense blood in basal cisterns, sylvian fissures, sulci ± intraventricular extension. "Thunderclap" headache. Workup: CT angiography for aneurysm.
</details>

### Q4 — Psoas abscess in TB spine

15-year-old with past spinal TB; CT shows fluid collection in psoas muscle. Identified muscle?

- A. 1
- B. 2 (Psoas)
- C. 3
- D. 4

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Psoas (muscle 2)** — Cold abscess from TB spine tracks down psoas to inguinal area (Petit's triangle). Drainage + ATT.
</details>

### Q5 — ARDS in acute pancreatitis

25-year-old with acute pancreatitis develops bilateral diffuse pulmonary infiltrates. Most likely?

- A. ARDS
- B. Acute pulmonary oedema
- C. Pulmonary thromboembolism
- D. Miliary tuberculosis

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. ARDS** — Severe pancreatitis releases inflammatory mediators → ARDS (Berlin definition: bilateral opacities, PaO₂/FiO₂ < 300, not cardiogenic). Common cause of mortality in severe pancreatitis.
</details>

### Q6 — Pituitary lesion → bitemporal hemianopia

Lesion at pituitary fossa on imaging → visual field defect?

- A. Altitudinal defect
- B. Bitemporal hemianopia
- C. Inferior arcuate scotoma
- D. Bjerrum scotoma

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Bitemporal hemianopia** — Pituitary tumour compresses optic chiasm → loss of crossing nasal fibres → bitemporal field loss (peripheral temporal vision lost first).
</details>

### Q8 — Choledochal cyst on MRCP

MRCP showing fusiform dilatation of CBD and common hepatic duct. Diagnosis?

- A. Choledochal cyst
- B. Cholangiocarcinoma
- C. Cholelithiasis
- D. Choledocholithiasis

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Choledochal cyst** — Todani classification (Type I most common = fusiform dilation of CBD). Triad: jaundice + RUQ mass + pain. Risk of malignancy → surgical excision with hepaticojejunostomy.
</details>

### Q9 — Lung abscess

CXR with cavity containing air-fluid level + foul sputum + fever. Diagnosis?

- A. Lung abscess
- B. Pneumothorax
- C. Pleural effusion
- D. Pericardial effusion

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Lung abscess** — Cavity with air-fluid level. Aspiration aetiology common; anaerobes (Bacteroides, Fusobacterium, Peptostreptococcus). Treatment: clindamycin or beta-lactam-β-lactamase inhibitor combo.
</details>

### Q10 — Pancreatic calcification (chronic pancreatitis)

Radiograph with small scattered calcifications across pancreas. Diagnosis?

- A. Pancreatic calcification (chronic pancreatitis)
- B. Mesenteric calcification
- C. Horseshoe kidney
- D. Jejunal faecolith

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Pancreatic calcification** — Calcifications within pancreatic ducts/parenchyma; pathognomonic of chronic calcific pancreatitis (alcoholic or tropical). Often associated with diabetes and steatorrhoea.
</details>

### Q11 — Penetrating power of radiation

Highest penetrating power among:

- A. Alpha
- B. Beta
- C. Gamma
- D. Neutrons

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. Neutrons** — Neutrons have very high penetration; require thick concrete/water shielding. Gamma > beta > alpha. Alpha = stopped by paper/skin.
</details>

### Q12 — PACS acronym

PACS in radiology stands for:

- A. Picture Archiving and Communication System
- B. Patient Access Control System

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

### Q13 — Hounsfield units: most negative

Among water, fat, blood, bone — which has the most negative HU value?

- A. Water (0 HU)
- B. Fat (−100 HU)
- C. Blood (+40 HU)
- D. Bone (+1000 HU)

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Fat (−100 HU)** — HU scale: air −1000, fat −100, water 0, blood 40 (clotted 60), muscle 40–60, bone +400 to +1000. Negative HU = less dense than water.
</details>

### Q14 — Non-ionising imaging modality

Which does NOT use ionising radiation?

- A. CT scan
- B. X-ray
- C. FAST ultrasound
- D. Fluoroscopy

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. FAST ultrasound** — USG uses sound waves (non-ionising). MRI also non-ionising. CT, X-ray, fluoroscopy, nuclear medicine all use ionising radiation.
</details>

### Q15 — SPECT abbreviation

SPECT stands for:

- A. Single Photon Emission Computed Tomography
- B. Slow Phase Emission CT
- C. Specific Photon Emission CT
- D. Spectral Photonic CT

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Single Photon Emission Computed Tomography** — Nuclear medicine technique using gamma-emitting radiotracers (Tc-99m). Provides functional/perfusion imaging. PET = positron emission tomography (uses F-18 FDG).
</details>
