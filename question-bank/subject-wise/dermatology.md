### Q1 — Lupus vulgaris treatment

Annular plaque with central scarring and apple-jelly nodules on diascopy. Treatment?

- A. Anti-fungal therapy
- B. Anti-tubercular therapy (ATT)
- C. Topical steroids
- D. Anti-leprosy treatment

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Anti-tubercular therapy** — Lupus vulgaris = cutaneous TB (most common form in India); apple-jelly nodules on diascopy; soft brownish-red plaques; responds to standard ATT (2HRZE/4HR). Biopsy shows caseating granulomas.
</details>

### Q2 — Psoriasis: Koebner (isomorphic) phenomenon

Pustular psoriasis image shown. Associated finding?

- A. Isomorphic (Köbner) phenomenon
- B. Meyerson phenomenon
- C. Gottron's papule
- D. Nikolsky's sign

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Isomorphic (Köbner) phenomenon** — New lesions appear at sites of trauma/injury in psoriasis, lichen planus, vitiligo, warts. Gottron's papules = dermatomyositis. Nikolsky's sign = pemphigus (epidermal shear). Meyerson = halo around melanocytic naevus.
</details>

### Q3 — Herpes labialis (image)

Vesicles and crusts around lips. Diagnosis?

- A. Herpes labialis
- B. Herpangina
- C. Molluscum contagiosum
- D. Impetigo

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Herpes labialis** — Reactivation of HSV-1 at vermillion border (cold sores); painful vesicles → crusting over 7–10 days; triggers: sun, stress, fever, menstruation. Herpangina (Coxsackie A) = posterior oropharyngeal vesicles.
</details>

### Q4 — Morpholine nail lacquer

Which morpholine antifungal is used as a nail lacquer?

- A. Amorolfine
- B. Oxiconazole
- C. Ciclopirox olamine
- D. Tioconazole

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Amorolfine** — 5% amorolfine nail lacquer; morpholine antifungal; inhibits ergosterol synthesis (Δ14 reductase and Δ8→7 isomerase); applied weekly for onychomycosis. Ciclopirox olamine is also a nail lacquer but not a morpholine.
</details>

### Q5 — Harlequin ichthyosis

Newborn with thick armour-like plaques, ectropion, eclabium. Diagnosis?

- A. Harlequin ichthyosis
- B. Lamellar ichthyosis
- C. Bathing suit ichthyosis
- D. Ichthyosis vulgaris

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Harlequin ichthyosis** — Severe ABCA12 mutation; thick keratin plates cracked at flexures (harlequin pattern); ectropion, eclabium; high mortality in infancy. Treated with retinoids (acitretin). Most severe ichthyosis.
</details>

### Q6 — Kerion (tinea capitis)

10-year-old boy with painful scalp swelling for 3 months; has a pet dog. Diagnosis?

- A. Furuncle
- B. Epidermoid cyst
- C. Kerion
- D. Folliculitis

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Kerion** — Inflammatory tinea capitis; boggy, painful, boggy swelling with pustules and alopecia; caused by zoophilic dermatophytes (Microsporum canis from dogs/cats). Treated with systemic griseofulvin/terbinafine + short course steroids.
</details>

### Q7 — Phrynoderma (vitamin A deficiency)

Child with night blindness and follicular hyperkeratotic papules. Diagnosis?

- A. Phrynoderma
- B. Keratosis pilaris
- C. Darier disease
- D. Follicular eczema

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Phrynoderma (toad skin)** — Vitamin A deficiency; follicular hyperkeratotic papules on extensor surfaces; associated with night blindness, xerophthalmia. Treated with Vitamin A supplementation. Keratosis pilaris = genetic, not nutritional.
</details>

### Q8 — Pemphigus vulgaris

30-year-old with flaccid bullae prone to easy rupture; biopsy shows suprabasal acantholysis. Diagnosis?

- A. Pemphigus vulgaris
- B. Pemphigus foliaceus
- C. Pemphigus vegetans
- D. Erythema multiforme

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Pemphigus vulgaris** — Anti-desmoglein 3 (± desmoglein 1) antibodies; intraepidermal blistering (suprabasal acantholysis); Nikolsky's sign positive; IgG deposition on cell surface (DIF). Treated with systemic steroids + immunosuppressants.
</details>

### Q9 — Paronychia treatment

Treatment of choice for paronychia?

- A. Amoxicillin-clavulanate (Amoxiclav)
- B. Metronidazole
- C. Amikacin
- D. Norfloxacin

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Amoxicillin-clavulanate** — Acute paronychia (usually S. aureus or mixed bacteria) → co-amoxiclav (covers MSSA and anaerobes); incision and drainage if abscess present. Chronic paronychia = usually Candida → topical antifungals.
</details>

### Q10 — Lepra reaction management

Patient on MDT develops worsening of existing lesions with nerve involvement. Next step?

- A. Stop MDT, start systemic corticosteroids
- B. Continue MDT, start systemic steroids
- C. Stop MDT, start thalidomide
- D. Continue MDT, start thalidomide

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Continue MDT, start systemic steroids** — Type 1 lepra reaction (reversal reaction): continue MDT + prednisolone for nerve involvement. Never stop MDT during reactions. Thalidomide (ENL/Type 2 reaction, male patients only) + continue MDT.
</details>

### Q11 — High-risk HPV types

Which HPV type is considered high-risk (oncogenic)?

- A. HPV 2
- B. HPV 18
- C. HPV 6
- D. HPV 11

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. HPV 18** — High-risk oncogenic types: HPV 16, 18, 31, 33, 45 (cervical, oropharyngeal, anal cancer). Low-risk types: HPV 6, 11 (genital warts, recurrent respiratory papillomatosis). HPV 2 causes common warts (verruca vulgaris).
</details>

### Q12 — Mycetoma: true statement

Foot swelling with multiple discharging sinuses and granules. True statement?

- A. Both bacteria and fungi can be causative
- B. Spreads via lymphatics
- C. Lymphocyte accumulation is hallmark
- D. Involves only superficial tissues

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Both bacteria and fungi can be causative** — Mycetoma: eumycetoma (fungi, e.g., Madurella) and actinomycetoma (bacteria, e.g., Nocardia, Actinomadura). Spreads via direct extension, not lymphatics. Can involve deep structures including bone.
</details>

### Q13 — Chromoblastomycosis

Cauliflower-shaped foot mass after minor injury; microscopy shows copper-penny (sclerotic) bodies. Diagnosis?

- A. Chromoblastomycosis
- B. Blastomycosis
- C. Sporotrichosis
- D. Phaeohyphomycosis

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Chromoblastomycosis** — Caused by dematiaceous (melanised) fungi (Fonsecaea, Cladophialophora, Phialophora); copper-penny (Medlar) bodies = muriform cells on histology; cauliflower-like warty lesions. Rx: itraconazole + terbinafine.
</details>

### Q14 — Nail psoriasis features

Irregular pitting of nails with subungual hyperkeratosis is seen in:

- A. Lichen planus
- B. Psoriasis
- C. Atopic dermatitis
- D. Alopecia areata

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Psoriasis** — Nail psoriasis: pitting (irregular, large), onycholysis, subungual hyperkeratosis, oil drop sign (salmon patch), splinter haemorrhages. Lichen planus nails: longitudinal ridging, pterygium formation. Alopecia areata = geometric pitting.
</details>

### Q15 — Telogen effluvium post-COVID

35-year-old with diffuse hair loss 3 months after COVID-19 infection 8 months ago. Diagnosis?

- A. Tinea capitis
- B. Telogen effluvium
- C. Trichotillomania
- D. Female pattern androgenetic alopecia

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Telogen effluvium** — Diffuse non-scarring alopecia 2–4 months after a trigger (fever/illness, surgery, childbirth, stress); COVID-19 is a recognised trigger. Pull test positive; self-limiting (regrowth in 6–12 months).
</details>

### Q16 — Atopic dermatitis

15-year-old girl with itchy flexural lesions; family history of asthma. Diagnosis?

- A. Seborrhoeic dermatitis
- B. Atopic dermatitis (eczema)
- C. Allergic contact dermatitis
- D. Erysipelas

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Atopic dermatitis** — Hanifin-Rajka criteria: pruritus + typical distribution (flexures in children) + chronic/relapsing course + atopic history (asthma/rhinitis/family history). IgE-mediated; FLG (filaggrin) mutation common.
</details>

### Q17 — Bindi leucoderma chemical

Bindi leucoderma is caused by which chemical?

- A. Monobenzyl ether of hydroquinone (MBEH)
- B. Crocein scarlet + solvent yellow 3
- C. p-phenylenediamine (PPD)
- D. Para-tertiary butylphenol (PTBP)

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. Para-tertiary butylphenol (PTBP)** — Contact leukoderma from PTBP used as an adhesive in bindi; selectively destroys melanocytes; occupational leucoderma also seen in rubber industry workers. MBEH = intentional depigmentation agent.
</details>

### Q18 — Erythema multiforme most common trigger

Most frequent trigger of erythema multiforme (target lesions)?

- A. Vaccination
- B. Malignancy
- C. Drugs
- D. Infection (HSV)

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. Infection (HSV)** — HSV is the most common cause of recurrent erythema multiforme minor (~70%). Stevens-Johnson syndrome/TEN = drugs (sulfonamides, anticonvulsants, allopurinol). EM major = drugs; EM minor = usually HSV.
</details>

### Q19 — Trichomoniasis treatment

Young female with vaginal itching, green frothy discharge, strawberry cervix. Drug of choice?

- A. Doxycycline
- B. Oral fluconazole
- C. Metronidazole
- D. Azithromycin

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Metronidazole** — Oral metronidazole 2 g single dose (or 400 mg BD × 7 days); treat partner simultaneously. Trichomonas vaginalis = flagellated protozoan; strawberry cervix = punctate haemorrhages on colposcopy (2% of cases).
</details>

### Q20 — Herpes zoster: true statement

True statement about herpes zoster?

- A. Lesions are not infectious
- B. Trigeminal dermatome most commonly affected
- C. Anterior nerve roots more commonly involved
- D. Mucous membranes within affected dermatome are involved

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. Mucous membranes within the affected dermatome are involved** — HZ can involve mucosal surfaces in the same dermatome (e.g., Ramsay-Hunt: EAC + tympanic membrane mucosa). Thoracic dermatomes are most common (T3-L3). Posterior sensory root ganglia are affected (not anterior motor roots). Active lesions ARE infectious (spread by direct contact).
</details>
