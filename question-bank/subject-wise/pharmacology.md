### Q1 — Isoniazid-induced peripheral neuropathy

INH causes peripheral neuropathy by interfering with which vitamin?

- A. Pyridoxine (B6)
- B. Niacin (B3)
- C. Riboflavin (B2)
- D. Ascorbic acid (C)

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Pyridoxine** — INH structurally resembles pyridoxine; competes with it and forms inactive hydrazones → B6 deficiency → peripheral neuropathy. Prevented by co-administration of pyridoxine 10–25 mg/day.
</details>

### Q2 — Drug most commonly causing Torsades de Pointes

Which drug most commonly causes Torsades de Pointes (TdP)?

- A. Amiodarone
- B. Sotalol
- C. Verapamil
- D. Lignocaine

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Sotalol** — Class III antiarrhythmic; prolongs QT → TdP risk (~2–5%). Amiodarone also prolongs QT but TdP is rare due to homogeneous repolarisation effect.
</details>

### Q3 — Best antiemetic for delayed chemotherapy nausea

Best antiemetic for delayed chemotherapy-induced nausea?

- A. Metoclopramide
- B. Ondansetron
- C. Domperidone
- D. Aprepitant

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. Aprepitant** — NK1 receptor antagonist; more effective for delayed nausea (24–120 hours post-chemo) than 5-HT3 antagonists. Combined with dexamethasone + ondansetron for highly emetogenic regimens.
</details>

### Q4 — Ivermectin mechanism

Mechanism of action of ivermectin?

- A. Inhibits microtubules
- B. Enhances GABA-mediated Cl⁻ influx (in invertebrates)
- C. Inhibits DNA synthesis
- D. Calcium channel blocker

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Enhances GABA-mediated Cl⁻ influx** — Opens glutamate-gated Cl⁻ channels in nematodes/arthropods → paralysis. Safe in mammals (BBB blocks penetration). Used for scabies, strongyloidiasis, onchocerciasis.
</details>

### Q5 — Longest-acting insulin

Which insulin has the longest duration of action?

- A. NPH (intermediate)
- B. Glargine (long)
- C. Lispro (rapid)
- D. Regular (short)

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Glargine** — Long-acting basal insulin; ~24 hours duration; no peak. Detemir similar. Degludec is even longer (>42 h). Lispro/aspart = rapid-acting; regular = short-acting.
</details>

### Q6 — Antihypertensive contraindicated in pregnancy

Which antihypertensive is contraindicated in pregnancy?

- A. Methyldopa
- B. Labetalol
- C. Enalapril
- D. Nifedipine

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Enalapril (ACE inhibitor)** — Teratogenic (renal dysgenesis, oligohydramnios, foetal hypotension); contraindicated in 2nd and 3rd trimesters. Safe options: methyldopa, labetalol, nifedipine, hydralazine.
</details>

### Q7 — Antibiotics unsafe in pregnancy

Which antibiotic is unsafe in pregnancy?

- A. Penicillin
- B. Erythromycin
- C. Tetracycline
- D. Cephalexin

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Tetracycline** — Causes permanent teeth discolouration in fetus + bone growth inhibition; binds to calcium in developing teeth. Avoid in pregnancy and children < 8 years.
</details>

### Q8 — Open-angle glaucoma first-line drug

First-line topical drug for primary open-angle glaucoma?

- A. Timolol (β-blocker)
- B. Atropine
- C. Pilocarpine
- D. Acetazolamide

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Timolol** — Non-selective β-blocker; reduces aqueous humour production. Note: Prostaglandin analogues (latanoprost) are now preferred first-line. Atropine = pupil dilator (worsens angle-closure). Pilocarpine = miotic (older agent).
</details>

### Q9 — Rifampicin–warfarin interaction

How does rifampicin affect warfarin levels?

- A. Increase INR
- B. Decrease INR
- C. No change
- D. Fluctuate

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Decrease INR** — Rifampicin is a potent CYP3A4 + CYP2C9 inducer; increases warfarin metabolism → reduced anticoagulant effect → decreased INR. Need to monitor INR and increase warfarin dose.
</details>

### Q10 — Selective COX-2 inhibitor

Which is a selective COX-2 inhibitor?

- A. Aspirin
- B. Diclofenac
- C. Celecoxib
- D. Ibuprofen

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Celecoxib** — Selective COX-2 inhibitor; less GI toxicity; risk of cardiovascular events (rofecoxib withdrawn). Diclofenac is COX-2 preferential but not selective. Aspirin/ibuprofen = non-selective.
</details>
