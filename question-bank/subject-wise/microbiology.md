### Q1 — Oral candidiasis: HIV patient

35-year-old HIV-positive patient with mucosal lesions; microscopy shows budding yeasts and pseudohyphae. Diagnosis?

- A. Oral candidiasis
- B. Hairy leukoplakia
- C. Lichen planus
- D. Diphtheria

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Oral candidiasis** — Candida albicans: budding yeasts + pseudohyphae on KOH/Gram stain. Most common oral opportunistic infection in HIV. Hairy leukoplakia (EBV on lateral tongue) does not show pseudohyphae.
</details>

### Q5 — Staphylococcal food poisoning (vomiting within 3 hours)

14-year-old, vomiting within 3 hours after eating at a party. Most probable cause?

- A. Staphylococcus aureus
- B. Clostridium botulinum
- C. Clostridium perfringens
- D. Salmonella typhi

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Staphylococcus aureus** — Heat-stable enterotoxin (preformed); onset 1–6 hours; nausea/vomiting dominant; cream/custard/egg salads. Cl. perfringens: 8–22 h, diarrhoea. Bacillus cereus emetic: 1–6 h, vomiting.
</details>

### Q12 — Measles virus nature

Unimmunized 2-year-old with Koplik spots, then maculopapular rash. Virus nature?

- A. Enveloped ssRNA
- B. Naked ssRNA

### Q13 — Food poisoning after pastries: organism

Two people vomit early morning after eating pastries. Probable cause?

- A. Staphylococcal enterotoxin
- B. Bacillus cereus emetic toxin
- C. E. coli verocytotoxin
- D. Shiga toxin

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Bacillus cereus emetic toxin** — Emetic syndrome: cereulide toxin (preformed, heat-stable) in starchy/rice/pastry foods; onset 1–6 hours; vomiting dominant. Note: S. aureus also causes similar pattern but B. cereus is classic for pastries/rice.
</details>

### Q15 — Dark-field microscopy for syphilis

Truck driver with painless indurated genital ulcer. Which microscope detects organism motility?

### Q16 — Brugia malayi microfilaria identification

Microfilariae with sheathed tail and two nuclei at the tail tip indicate:

- A. Wuchereria bancrofti
- B. Brugia malayi

### Q17 — Paediatric anaesthesia: NOT used for induction

Which is NOT used for induction of anaesthesia in paediatric patients?

### Q19 — EHEC O157:H7 — "H" antigen

In E. coli O157:H7, "H" represents:

- A. Capsule
- B. Flagella (H antigen = flagellar antigen)
- C. Fimbriae
- D. Lipopolysaccharide

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Flagella** — Kauffmann-White scheme: O = somatic LPS antigen; H = flagellar antigen; K = capsular antigen. O157:H7 = somatic serotype 157, flagellar serotype 7. EHEC O157:H7 produces Shiga toxin → HUS.
</details>
