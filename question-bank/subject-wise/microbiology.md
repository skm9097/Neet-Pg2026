### Q1 — Oral candidiasis: HIV patient

35-year-old HIV-positive patient with mucosal lesions; microscopy shows budding yeasts and pseudohyphae. Diagnosis?

- A. Oral candidiasis
- B. Hairy leukoplakia
- C. Lichen planus
- D. Diphtheria

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Oral candidiasis** — Candida albicans: budding yeasts + pseudohyphae on KOH/Gram stain. Most common oral opportunistic infection in HIV. Hairy leukoplakia (EBV on lateral tongue) does not show pseudohyphae.
</details>

### Q2 — Zika virus vector

Zika virus is transmitted by:

- A. Aedes aegypti
- B. Culex
- C. Anopheles
- D. Phlebotomus

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Aedes aegypti** — Also transmitted by Aedes albopictus; causes microcephaly in neonates (congenital Zika), Guillain-Barré syndrome. Sexual and vertical transmission also documented.
</details>

### Q3 — Sporothrix schenckii: route of infection

Which organism causes illness after traumatic skin inoculation?

- A. Sporothrix schenckii
- B. Blastomyces dermatitidis
- C. Coccidioides immitis
- D. Paracoccidioides brasiliensis

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Sporothrix schenckii** — "Rose-thorn" disease; gardeners/farmers; inoculation → fixed cutaneous or lymphocutaneous sporotrichosis (ascending nodules along lymphatics). Dx: culture; Rx: itraconazole.
</details>

### Q4 — Dimeric immunoglobulin

The dimeric immunoglobulin shown in the image is:

- A. IgA
- B. IgG
- C. IgM
- D. IgE

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. IgA** — Secretory IgA is a dimer held by J-chain + secretory component; found in mucosal secretions (saliva, tears, breast milk, GI tract). IgM is pentameric. IgG is monomeric.
</details>

### Q5 — Staphylococcal food poisoning (vomiting within 3 hours)

14-year-old, vomiting within 3 hours after eating at a party. Most probable cause?

- A. Staphylococcus aureus
- B. Clostridium botulinum
- C. Clostridium perfringens
- D. Salmonella typhi

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Staphylococcus aureus** — Heat-stable enterotoxin (preformed); onset 1–6 hours; nausea/vomiting dominant; cream/custard/egg salads. Cl. perfringens: 8–22 h, diarrhoea. Bacillus cereus emetic: 1–6 h, vomiting.
</details>

### Q6 — Superantigen production

Which organism produces superantigens?

- A. Staphylococcus epidermidis
- B. Streptococcus pyogenes
- C. Clostridium perfringens
- D. Vibrio cholerae

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Streptococcus pyogenes** — Streptococcal pyrogenic exotoxins (SPE A, B, C) are superantigens → massive T-cell activation → toxic shock-like syndrome. S. aureus also produces superantigens (TSST-1, enterotoxins).
</details>

### Q7 — Filariasis: night blood test

Female from northeastern India with gradually increasing upper limb swelling. Most appropriate next step?

- A. Echocardiography
- B. Night blood smear for microfilariae
- C. Duplex venous scan
- D. ECG

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Night blood smear** — Wuchereria bancrofti shows nocturnal periodicity of microfilariae in peripheral blood; sample between 10 PM–2 AM. Brugia timori also periodic. Brugia malayi: subperiodic forms in some regions.
</details>

### Q8 — Trichuris trichiura egg: barrel-shaped

Barrel-shaped egg with polar plugs in stool. Organism?

- A. Trichuris trichiura
- B. Ancylostoma duodenale
- C. Necator americanus
- D. Ascaris lumbricoides

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Trichuris trichiura (whipworm)** — Characteristic lemon/barrel-shaped eggs with prominent bipolar plugs (hyaline plugs). Lives in cecum and colon. Causes trichuriasis (dysentery, rectal prolapse in heavy infection).
</details>

### Q9 — Multiple ring forms in peripheral smear

30-year-old from tribal area with fever; peripheral smear shows multiple ring forms. Most likely?

- A. P. falciparum
- B. P. malariae
- C. P. ovale
- D. P. vivax

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. P. falciparum** — Multiple rings per RBC, appliqué (accolé) forms, small ring forms, no enlarged RBCs; banana-shaped gametocytes. Most dangerous; causes cerebral malaria, ARDS, blackwater fever.
</details>

### Q10 — HBsAg rapid card test principle

The HBsAg rapid card test works on:

- A. ELISA
- B. Immunochromatography
- C. Immunofluorescence
- D. Chemiluminescence

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Immunochromatography** — Lateral flow assay (immunochromatographic strip); coloured antibody-coated particles + antigen migrate along membrane → visible band at test line. Rapid, bedside, no equipment needed.
</details>

### Q11 — Kala-azar vector

Patient from UP with fever, hepatosplenomegaly, pancytopenia; macrophages with kinetoplast-containing organisms. Vector?

- A. Sandfly (Phlebotomus argentipes)
- B. Tse-Tse fly
- C. Triatomine bug
- D. Female Anopheles mosquito

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Sandfly (Phlebotomus argentipes)** — Transmits Leishmania donovani (kala-azar) in India. Organisms (amastigotes) seen in macrophages with characteristic kinetoplast. Rx: AmBisome (liposomal amphotericin B).
</details>

### Q12 — Measles virus nature

Unimmunized 2-year-old with Koplik spots, then maculopapular rash. Virus nature?

- A. Enveloped ssRNA
- B. Naked ssRNA
- C. Naked dsRNA
- D. Enveloped dsRNA

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Enveloped ssRNA (negative-sense)** — Measles virus (Paramyxoviridae, Morbillivirus): enveloped, (−)ssRNA; H protein (haemagglutinin-neuraminidase) and F protein (fusion). Koplik spots are pathognomonic.
</details>

### Q13 — Food poisoning after pastries: organism

Two people vomit early morning after eating pastries. Probable cause?

- A. Staphylococcal enterotoxin
- B. Bacillus cereus emetic toxin
- C. E. coli verocytotoxin
- D. Shiga toxin

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Bacillus cereus emetic toxin** — Emetic syndrome: cereulide toxin (preformed, heat-stable) in starchy/rice/pastry foods; onset 1–6 hours; vomiting dominant. Note: S. aureus also causes similar pattern but B. cereus is classic for pastries/rice.
</details>

### Q14 — Enrichment medium for Shigella in stool

3-year-old with bloody diarrhoea. Which enrichment medium for stool culture?

- A. Robertson cooked meat broth
- B. Alkaline peptone water
- C. Selenite F broth
- D. Brain heart infusion broth

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Selenite F broth** — Enrichment medium for Salmonella and Shigella; inhibits coliforms, allows Salmonella/Shigella to proliferate. Alkaline peptone water = Vibrio enrichment.
</details>

### Q15 — Dark-field microscopy for syphilis

Truck driver with painless indurated genital ulcer. Which microscope detects organism motility?

- A. Dark-field microscope
- B. Electron microscope
- C. Light microscope
- D. Fluorescent microscope

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Dark-field microscope** — Treponema pallidum: too thin to see by light microscopy; dark-field shows characteristic corkscrew motility with tight coils. Cannot be cultured routinely. Serology (RPR/VDRL + TPHA/FTA-ABS) used for diagnosis.
</details>

### Q16 — Brugia malayi microfilaria identification

Microfilariae with sheathed tail and two nuclei at the tail tip indicate:

- A. Wuchereria bancrofti
- B. Brugia malayi
- C. Loa loa
- D. Onchocerca volvulus

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Brugia malayi** — Sheath + two discrete nuclei at tail tip. W. bancrofti: sheathed, no nuclei at tail. Loa loa: sheathed, nuclei throughout including tail. Onchocerca: unsheathed, no nuclei at tail.
</details>

### Q17 — Paediatric anaesthesia: NOT used for induction

Which is NOT used for induction of anaesthesia in paediatric patients?

- A. Halothane
- B. Sevoflurane
- C. Desflurane
- D. Nitrous oxide

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Desflurane** — Desflurane is pungent and causes airway irritation; NOT suitable for mask induction in children (may cause laryngospasm, breath-holding). Sevoflurane is the agent of choice for paediatric inhalational induction.
</details>

### Q18 — Candida from central line (oval budding organism)

ICU patient, central line infection; Gram stain shows oval organisms reproducing by budding. Organism?

- A. Staphylococcus aureus
- B. Candida species
- C. Staphylococcus epidermidis
- D. E. coli

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Candida** — Candida species: oval budding yeasts (2–6 µm); pseudohyphae in tissue; common cause of CRBSI (catheter-related bloodstream infection). Rx: echinocandin (caspofungin) or fluconazole.
</details>

### Q19 — EHEC O157:H7 — "H" antigen

In E. coli O157:H7, "H" represents:

- A. Capsule
- B. Flagella (H antigen = flagellar antigen)
- C. Fimbriae
- D. Lipopolysaccharide

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Flagella** — Kauffmann-White scheme: O = somatic LPS antigen; H = flagellar antigen; K = capsular antigen. O157:H7 = somatic serotype 157, flagellar serotype 7. EHEC O157:H7 produces Shiga toxin → HUS.
</details>

### Q20 — Nocardia from foot wound

Farmer with foot wound and discharging ulcer; Gram-positive branching filamentous organism. Identification?

- A. Nocardia
- B. Sporothrix
- C. Histoplasma
- D. Candida

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Nocardia** — Aerobic, Gram-positive, weakly acid-fast, beaded branching filaments; mycetoma-like lesions from soil inoculation. Actinomycetoma with Nocardia: white/yellow granules. Rx: TMP-SMX + imipenem.
</details>
