### Q1 — Oral candidiasis: HIV patient

35-year-old HIV-positive patient with mucosal lesions; microscopy shows budding yeasts and pseudohyphae. Diagnosis?

- A. Oral candidiasis
- B. Hairy leukoplakia
- C. Lichen planus
- D. Diphtheria

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Oral candidiasis** — Candida albicans: budding yeasts + pseudohyphae on KOH/Gram stain. Most common oral opportunistic infection in HIV. Hairy leukoplakia (EBV on lateral tongue) does not show pseudohyphae.
</details>

### Q5 — Staphylococcal food poisoning (vomiting within 3 hours)

14-year-old, vomiting within 3 hours after eating at a party. Most probable cause?

- A. Staphylococcus aureus
- B. Clostridium botulinum
- C. Clostridium perfringens
- D. Salmonella typhi

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Staphylococcus aureus** — Heat-stable enterotoxin (preformed); onset 1–6 hours; nausea/vomiting dominant; cream/custard/egg salads. Cl. perfringens: 8–22 h, diarrhoea. Bacillus cereus emetic: 1–6 h, vomiting.
</details>

### Q9 — Multiple ring forms in peripheral smear

### Q12 — Measles virus nature

Unimmunized 2-year-old with Koplik spots, then maculopapular rash. Virus nature?

- A. Enveloped ssRNA
- B. Naked ssRNA
- C. Naked dsRNA

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Enveloped ssRNA (negative-sense)** — Measles virus (Paramyxoviridae, Morbillivirus): enveloped, (−)ssRNA; H protein (haemagglutinin-neuraminidase) and F protein (fusion). Koplik spots are pathognomonic.

### Q13 — Food poisoning after pastries: organism

Two people vomit early morning after eating pastries. Probable cause?

- A. Staphylococcal enterotoxin
- B. Bacillus cereus emetic toxin
- C. E. coli verocytotoxin
- D. Shiga toxin

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Bacillus cereus emetic toxin** — Emetic syndrome: cereulide toxin (preformed, heat-stable) in starchy/rice/pastry foods; onset 1–6 hours; vomiting dominant. Note: S. aureus also causes similar pattern but B. cereus is classic for pastries/rice.
</details>

### Q15 — Dark-field microscopy for syphilis

Truck driver with painless indurated genital ulcer. Which microscope detects organism motility?

- A. Dark-field microscope

### Q16 — Brugia malayi microfilaria identification

Microfilariae with sheathed tail and two nuclei at the tail tip indicate:

- A. Wuchereria bancrofti
- B. Brugia malayi
- C. Loa loa

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Brugia malayi** — Sheath + two discrete nuclei at tail tip. W. bancrofti: sheathed, no nuclei at tail. Loa loa: sheathed, nuclei throughout including tail. Onchocerca: unsheathed, no nuclei at tail.

### Q17 — Paediatric anaesthesia: NOT used for induction

Which is NOT used for induction of anaesthesia in paediatric patients?

- A. Halothane

### Q19 — EHEC O157:H7 — "H" antigen

In E. coli O157:H7, "H" represents:

- A. Capsule
- B. Flagella (H antigen = flagellar antigen)
- C. Fimbriae
- D. Lipopolysaccharide

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Flagella** — Kauffmann-White scheme: O = somatic LPS antigen; H = flagellar antigen; K = capsular antigen. O157:H7 = somatic serotype 157, flagellar serotype 7. EHEC O157:H7 produces Shiga toxin → HUS.
</details>

- A. Nocardia

</details>

A 5-year-old with intense nocturnal perianal itching. Microscopy of perianal tape shows the causative egg. Organism?

- A. Enterobius vermicularis
- B. Ancylostoma duodenale
- C. Hymenolepis nana
- D. Trichuris trichiura

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Enterobius vermicularis (pinworm)** — Nocturnal perianal pruritus; diagnosis by Scotch tape test; eggs are D-shaped (plano-convex). Female migrates to perianal region to lay eggs at night.
</details>

### Q134 — Chronic cough, weight loss, narrow-based budding yeast

Respiratory sample shows yeast with narrow-based budding on culture. Most probable?

- A. Blastomycosis
- B. Histoplasmosis
- C. Cryptococcosis
- D. Coccidioidomycosis

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Histoplasmosis** — Histoplasma capsulatum: narrow-based budding yeast (intracellular in macrophages), chronic cough, weight loss, endemic in river valleys.

### Q136 — Recurrent Neisseria gonorrhoeae and complement defect

Which investigation detects a complement pathway defect predisposing to recurrent Neisseria infections?

- A. C1 esterase inhibitor assay

### Q137 — Progressive leg swelling, lymphatic obstruction parasite

A man from endemic region with progressive leg swelling; peripheral smear shows a parasitic organism. Pathophysiology?

- A. Hypoalbuminemia
- B. Lymphatic obstruction
- C. Hypoproteinemia
- D. Increased hydrostatic pressure

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Lymphatic obstruction** — Filariasis (Wuchereria bancrofti/Brugia malayi): adult worms in lymphatics → lymphatic obstruction → elephantiasis.
</details>

### Q138 — Sewage worker, fever, scleral congestion, jaundice, oliguria

25-year-old sewage worker, fever for a week, then sudden weakness, scleral congestion, jaundice, oliguria, raised bilirubin. Diagnosis?

- A. Brucellosis
- B. Weil's disease
- C. Enteric fever
- D. Dengue haemorrhagic fever

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Weil's disease (severe leptospirosis)** — Classic triad: jaundice + AKI + hemorrhage in occupational exposure (sewage/flood water). Conjunctival suffusion is a hallmark.
</details>
