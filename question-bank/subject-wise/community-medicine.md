### Q1 — Sharp waste disposal bag colour

Needles and scalpel blades (sharps) are disposed in which coloured bag/container?

- A. Yellow bag
- B. Red bag
- C. Blue bag
- D. White/translucent puncture-proof container

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. White/translucent puncture-proof container** — Bio-Medical Waste Rules (India): sharps (needles, scalpels, broken glass) → white translucent puncture-proof container. Yellow = anatomical waste, soiled items. Red = recyclable plastic waste.
</details>

### Q2 — Factories Act 1948 maximum weekly work hours

Maximum weekly working hours including overtime under the Factories Act 1948?

- A. 48 hours
- B. 72 hours
- C. 60 hours
- D. 66 hours

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. 60 hours** — Normal limit: 48 hours/week (9 hours/day). With overtime: maximum 60 hours/week and 50 hours overtime per quarter. Overtime is paid at double the ordinary rate.
</details>

### Q3 — Prospective screening example

Which of the following is an example of prospective screening?

- A. Cervical Pap smear in 40-year-old
- B. Neonatal hypothyroidism screening
- C. Screening of immigrants for tuberculosis
- D. Urine sugar in a clinic patient

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Screening of immigrants for infectious diseases** — Prospective screening: applied to population groups expected to enter a community (immigrants, recruits); screens before problems arise. Other options are mass/selective screening applied to existing populations.
</details>

### Q4 — Financial advantage quantification in health programmes

Which analysis measures programme outcomes in monetary terms?

- A. Cost-effectiveness analysis
- B. Cost-benefit analysis
- C. Network analysis
- D. Input-output analysis

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Cost-benefit analysis** — Converts both costs AND benefits to monetary units (rupees); calculates benefit-to-cost ratio. Cost-effectiveness analysis = compares cost per unit of health outcome (e.g., cost per DALY averted) without monetary conversion of outcomes.
</details>

### Q5 — Pneumonia in 24-month-old: management

24-month-old child diagnosed with pneumonia (no danger signs). Correct management?

- A. Oral ampicillin for 5 days
- B. Urgent tertiary referral
- C. IV antibiotics with 24-hour follow-up
- D. Oral amoxicillin for 5 days (with home care)

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. Oral amoxicillin for 5 days** — IMNCI/WHO guidelines: pneumonia in child 2 months–5 years without danger signs = oral amoxicillin 40 mg/kg/day × 5 days at home; fast breathing is key sign; return if worsening or not improving in 2 days.
</details>

### Q6 — Air velocity measurement instrument

Which instrument measures air velocity/movement?

- A. Kata thermometer
- B. Globe thermometer
- C. Wet globe thermometer
- D. Dial thermometer

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Kata thermometer** — Measures air velocity by timing how long it takes for the large alcohol bulb to cool from 38°C to 35°C; used in occupational settings. Anemometer is more modern air velocity tool. Globe thermometer = radiant heat.
</details>

### Q7 — Bubonic plague vector

Buboes (painful enlarged lymph nodes) are transmitted by which vector?

- A. Xenopsylla cheopis (oriental rat flea)
- B. Phlebotomus argentipes
- C. Ixodes tick
- D. Female Anopheles mosquito

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Xenopsylla cheopis** — Oriental rat flea; vector of Yersinia pestis (plague) from rat reservoir to humans. Phlebotomus = Leishmania; Ixodes = Lyme disease, RMSF; Anopheles = malaria.
</details>

### Q8 — Post-flood mosquito control chemical

Which chemical is used for mosquito control in post-flood conditions?

- A. Zinc phosphide
- B. Malathion
- C. Lindane
- D. Paris green

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Malathion** — Organophosphate insecticide; used for space spraying and residual spraying for mosquito control in disaster situations. Paris green = larval control (historical). DDT = residual spraying (restricted use). Zinc phosphide = rodenticide.
</details>

### Q9 — Hospital floor blood disinfection

Best disinfectant for blood-contaminated hospital floors?

- A. Ethyl alcohol
- B. Chlorhexidine
- C. Sodium hypochlorite (1%)
- D. Formaldehyde

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Sodium hypochlorite** — 1% NaOCl (10,000 ppm) for blood spills; effective against bacteria, viruses (including HIV, HBV), fungi. Recommended by WHO/CDC for environmental decontamination. Chlorhexidine = skin antiseptic (not floor disinfection).
</details>

### Q10 — Air Quality Index: AQI = 407

AQI value of 407 falls in which category?

- A. Moderately polluted
- B. Poor

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. Severe** — India's AQI scale: 0–50 = Good; 51–100 = Satisfactory; 101–200 = Moderate; 201–300 = Poor; 301–400 = Very Poor; 401–500 = Severe. AQI 407 = Severe (health emergency; avoid outdoor activities).

### Q12 — QALY calculation

Japan female life expectancy 87 years; intervention adds 15 quality-adjusted years at utility 0.8. Metric used?

- A. HALE
- B. DALY
- C. DFLE
- D. QALY

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. QALY (Quality-Adjusted Life Year)** — QALY = life years gained × utility value (0–1); combines quantity and quality of life. 15 years × 0.8 = 12 QALYs. Used in cost-utility analysis for health economic decisions.
</details>

Which disaster preparedness measures should a PHC adopt?

### Q14 — Most cost-effective rabies reduction strategy

Most cost-effective approach to reduce human rabies?

- A. Test all dogs for rabies
- B. Reduce stray dog population and vaccinate all dogs
- C. Increase laboratory capacity
- D. Increase passive surveillance

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Reduce stray dog population and vaccinate all dogs** — Dogs are the source of 99% of human rabies deaths; mass dog vaccination (≥ 70% coverage) interrupts transmission. More cost-effective than post-exposure prophylaxis alone. WHO target: zero dog-mediated human rabies by 2030.
</details>

### Q15 — WHO STEPS approach: excluded component

Which assessment is NOT part of the WHO STEPwise approach to NCD surveillance?

- A. Therapeutic assessment

### Q16 — ITP most effective treatment

Most effective treatment for chronic/recurrent ITP?

- A. IV immunoglobulins
- B. Steroids
- C. Splenectomy
- D. Blood transfusion

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Splenectomy** — Spleen is primary site of platelet destruction (anti-platelet IgG-coated platelets phagocytosed by splenic macrophages) and autoantibody production; splenectomy gives durable remission in ~70%. IV IgG/steroids are for acute management/temporisation.
</details>

### Q17 — Malaria surveillance: annual blood examination rate

Most effective indicator for measuring malaria surveillance effectiveness?

- A. Annual Parasite Index (API)
- B. Annual Blood Examination Rate (ABER)
- C. Slide positivity rate (SPR)
- D. Slide falciparum rate (SFR)

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Annual Blood Examination Rate (ABER)** — Measures the proportion of the population at risk that underwent blood examination for malaria in a year; best indicator of surveillance effort/quality. API = confirmed cases per 1000 population (measures disease burden, not surveillance effort).
</details>

### Q18 — Confounding: incorrect statement

Which is an INCORRECT statement about confounding factors?

- A. Can be reduced by matching in study design
- B. Associated with both the exposure and the outcome
- C. Distributed equally in both study and control groups
- D. Associated with the exposure

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Distributed equally in both groups** — A confounding variable is unequally distributed between study groups (that is what makes it a confounder and creates bias). If equally distributed, it cannot confound. Confounders are controlled by matching, stratification, or multivariate analysis.
</details>

### Q19 — Breast cancer prevention level (Leavell & Clark)

Mammography screening for early breast cancer represents which level of prevention?

- A. Specific protection
- B. Early diagnosis and prompt treatment
- C. Disability limitation
- D. Rehabilitation

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Early diagnosis and prompt treatment (Secondary prevention)** — Screening detects pre-clinical disease → early treatment → better prognosis. Primary prevention = reduce incidence (e.g., reduce alcohol). Tertiary prevention = disability limitation, rehabilitation.
</details>

### Q20 — Vitamin A for keratomalacia: dosing schedule

Correct Vitamin A dosing for a child with keratomalacia?

- A. 200,000 IU immediately + repeat at 24 hours
- B. 100,000 IU immediately + 1 week later
- C. 100,000 IU at three intervals
- D. 200,000 IU immediately + 24 hours + 2 weeks

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. 200,000 IU immediately + at 24 hours + at 2 weeks** — WHO schedule for Vit A deficiency with xerophthalmia/keratomalacia (children ≥ 12 months): 200,000 IU oral × 3 doses (Day 1, Day 2, Day 8–14). < 12 months: 100,000 IU per dose.
</details>
