### Q1 — Saccule embryological origin

From which structure does the saccule develop?

- A. Sacculus anterior
- B. Sacculus posterior
- C. Pars superior
- D. Pars inferior

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. Pars inferior** — The membranous labyrinth develops from the otocyst; pars inferior gives rise to the saccule and cochlear duct; pars superior gives rise to the utricle and semicircular canals.
</details>

### Q2 — Surgical procedure to enlarge external auditory canal

Surgical procedure to enlarge the cartilaginous portion of the outer ear canal?

- A. Meatoplasty
- B. Tympanoplasty
- C. Myringoplasty
- D. Otoplasty

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Meatoplasty** — Enlargement of the external auditory meatus; part of canal wall down mastoidectomy/radical mastoidectomy to ensure adequate aeration and cleaning of the mastoid bowl.
</details>

### Q3 — Cough reflex when external ear canal is scratched

Which nerve mediates cough when the external acoustic canal is scratched?

- A. Auriculotemporal nerve
- B. Auricular branch of vagus (Arnold's nerve)
- C. Great auricular nerve
- D. Facial nerve

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Auricular branch of vagus (Arnold's nerve)** — Supplies posterior and inferior EAC; stimulation → cough reflex (Arnold's ear-cough reflex). Clinical relevance: explains why syringing can trigger cough/vasovagal.
</details>

### Q4 — Tubercular otitis media: NOT a feature

Which finding does NOT characterise tubercular otitis media?

- A. Ear ache
- B. Multiple perforations
- C. Pale granulation tissue
- D. Foul-smelling discharge

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Ear ache** — Tubercular CSOM is classically painless (unlike regular CSOM which can be painful). Features: multiple perforations, pale/oedematous granulations, progressive hearing loss, painless discharge.
</details>

### Q5 — Battle sign in skull fracture

Ecchymosis behind the ear (post-auricular) in skull fracture is called:

- A. Battle sign
- B. Bezold abscess
- C. Mastoiditis sign
- D. Griesinger sign

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Battle sign** — Delayed ecchymosis over the mastoid (due to tracking of blood from petrous temporal bone fracture). Appears 12–24 hours after injury. Griesinger sign = oedema over mastoid with sigmoid sinus thrombosis.
</details>

### Q6 — Mitomycin-C application in ENT

Mitomycin-C (topical antifibrotic) is used in which ENT condition?

- A. Subglottic stenosis
- B. Rhinocerebral mucormycosis
- C. Adenoidectomy
- D. Tympanoplasty

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Subglottic stenosis** — Applied topically after endoscopic dilation/CO₂ laser to inhibit fibrosis and prevent re-stenosis. Also used in dacryocystorhinostomy (DCR) to prevent ostium stenosis.
</details>

### Q7 — Post-thyroidectomy failed extubation: most likely cause

Breathing difficulties and failed extubation after total thyroidectomy — most likely cause?

- A. Superior laryngeal nerve injury
- B. Unilateral recurrent laryngeal nerve injury
- C. Bilateral recurrent laryngeal nerve injury
- D. Haematoma

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Bilateral recurrent laryngeal nerve injury** — Both vocal cords in paramedian position → adducted → complete airway obstruction. Unilateral RLN injury → hoarseness only. Bilateral RLN → respiratory distress, stridor, emergency tracheostomy.
</details>

### Q8 — Atrophic rhinitis additional finding

Patient with nasal obstruction, anosmia, foul-smelling discharge (ozaena). Additional finding?

- A. Roomy/wide nasal cavity
- B. Nasal polyps
- C. Inferior turbinate hypertrophy
- D. Foreign body

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Roomy (paradoxically wide) nasal cavity** — Atrophic rhinitis (ozaena): mucosal atrophy + turbinate atrophy → paradoxically spacious nasal cavity; patient feels congested (paradoxical obstruction) despite wide space. Green crusts, foetid odour.
</details>

### Q9 — Refractory epistaxis: next step after packing

Epistaxis persists after pinching and nasal packing. Next step?

- A. Ligation of external carotid artery
- B. Ligation of internal carotid artery
- C. Ligation/embolisation of sphenopalatine artery
- D. Ligation of maxillary artery

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Sphenopalatine artery** — Primary supply for posterior epistaxis; endoscopic sphenopalatine artery ligation (ESPAL) is gold standard after packing fails. IMA ligation is next if ESPAL fails; ECA ligation is a last resort.
</details>

### Q10 — Post-tonsillectomy haemorrhage management

Bleeding from tonsillar fossa in recovery room post-tonsillectomy. Immediate management?

- A. Shift to OT, remove clots, cauterise/ligate vessel
- B. Shift to OT, IV antibiotics, pack tonsillar fossa
- C. Anticoagulants, repeated gargling, wait 24 hours
- D. Blood transfusion and observe

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Shift to OT, remove clots, cauterise/ligate vessel** — Secondary haemorrhage post-tonsillectomy requires return to theatre; remove clot (concealing bleeding vessel), identify and ligate/cauterise. Do NOT blindly pack; do NOT give anticoagulants.
</details>

### Q11 — Serous otitis media in 5-year-old

5-year-old with reduced hearing; retracted opaque TM with fluid visible. Diagnosis?

- A. Myringitis bullosa
- B. Serous otitis media (glue ear / OME)
- C. Acute otitis media
- D. Pneumotympanum

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Serous otitis media (OME)** — Otitis media with effusion: dull, retracted TM; amber/blue fluid; conductive hearing loss; painless. Most common cause of hearing loss in children. Managed with watchful waiting ± grommet insertion.
</details>

### Q12 — Incus identification (image)

Identify the middle ear ossicle shown:

- A. Malleus
- B. Incus
- C. Stapes
- D. Vomer

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Incus** — "Anvil-shaped"; articulates with malleus (malleoincudal joint) and stapes (incudostapedial joint). Long process of incus is most commonly necrosed in CSOM; Stapes = "stirrup"; Malleus = "hammer".
</details>

### Q13 — Post-COVID mucormycosis: confirmatory investigation

60-year-old with uncontrolled DM, facial swelling, black nasal lesions post-COVID. Confirmatory investigation?

- A. X-ray paranasal sinuses
- B. Biopsy with histopathological examination
- C. Serum ferritin
- D. HbA1c

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Biopsy with histopathological examination** — Rhinocerebral mucormycosis (Mucor): wide, non-septate (pauci-septate) hyphae, right-angle branching on PAS/GMS stain. Culture can confirm species. Black eschar = tissue infarction due to angioinvasion.
</details>

### Q14 — Facial nerve palsy: eye closure failure, salivary drooling

Inability to close eye, salivary drooling, mouth deviation to opposite side. Nerve involved?

- A. Facial nerve (CN VII)
- B. Trigeminal nerve (CN V)
- C. Oculomotor nerve (CN III)
- D. Glossopharyngeal nerve (CN IX)

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Facial nerve (CN VII)** — LMN facial palsy: all ipsilateral facial muscles paralysed (including frontalis) → Bell's phenomenon (eyeball rolls up on attempted closure), drooping mouth, nasolabial fold flattening, salivary drooling.
</details>

### Q15 — Heimlich manoeuvre for conscious choking adult

Procedure for conscious choking patient at a restaurant?

- A. Heimlich's manoeuvre
- B. Back slap
- C. Chest thrust
- D. Blind finger sweep

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Heimlich's manoeuvre (abdominal thrusts)** — For conscious choking adult: 5 back blows + 5 abdominal thrusts (Heimlich) cycle until object expelled. Blind finger sweeps are no longer recommended (can push object deeper).
</details>

### Q16 — Keratosis obturans: true statement

Which statement about keratosis obturans is correct?

- A. Failure of epithelial migration along posterior meatal wall
- B. Widening of meatus with possible facial nerve palsy
- C. Associated with bronchiectasis and sinusitis
- D. All of the above

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. All of the above** — Keratosis obturans: plug of desquamated keratin in medial EAC; failure of epithelial migration; painful; associated with bronchiectasis/sinusitis. Widens/erodes the bony canal (can cause FN palsy). Distinct from external auditory canal cholesteatoma.
</details>

### Q17 — Laryngeal papilloma (image)

Grape-like vocal cord lesions on laryngoscopy. Diagnosis?

- A. Reinke's oedema
- B. Malignancy
- C. Tracheomalacia
- D. Laryngeal papilloma

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. Laryngeal papilloma** — Recurrent respiratory papillomatosis (RRP); caused by HPV 6 and 11 (low-risk types); most common benign laryngeal tumour in children. Treated by CO₂ laser/microdebrider; high recurrence rate.
</details>

### Q18 — Inspiratory stridor location

Inspiratory stridor is most commonly associated with which lesion site?

- A. Supraglottic
- B. Subglottic
- C. Tracheal
- D. Bronchial

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Supraglottic** — Supraglottic lesions → inspiratory stridor. Subglottic/tracheal → biphasic stridor. Bronchial → expiratory wheeze. Epiglottitis, croup (supraglottic oedema) → inspiratory stridor.
</details>

### Q19 — Trotter's method for epistaxis

The technique shown for initial epistaxis control (pressure on the Kiesselbach's area) is:

- A. Epley's manoeuvre
- B. Trotter's method
- C. McGovern's technique
- D. Valsalva manoeuvre

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Trotter's method** — Pinching the cartilaginous nose (alae) firmly for 5–10 minutes with the patient sitting forward. Compresses Kiesselbach's plexus (Little's area) — site of 90% of epistaxis. First-line management.
</details>

### Q20 — Juvenile nasopharyngeal angiofibroma features

Recurrent profuse unilateral epistaxis in an adolescent male with a vascular nasopharyngeal mass and bowing of the posterior wall of the maxillary sinus. Diagnosis?

- A. Antrochoanal polyp
- B. Rhinosporidiosis
- C. Juvenile nasopharyngeal angiofibroma (JNA)
- D. Rhinoscleroma

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Juvenile nasopharyngeal angiofibroma (JNA)** — Benign but locally aggressive vascular tumour; exclusively in adolescent males; Holman-Miller sign (anterior bowing of posterior wall of maxilla on CT); highly vascular (do NOT biopsy blind); embolisation + surgery.
</details>
