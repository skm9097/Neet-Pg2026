### Q1 — Saccule embryological origin

From which structure does the saccule develop?

- A. Sacculus anterior
- B. Sacculus posterior
- C. Pars superior
- D. Pars inferior

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. Pars inferior** — The membranous labyrinth develops from the otocyst; pars inferior gives rise to the saccule and cochlear duct; pars superior gives rise to the utricle and semicircular canals.
</details>

### Q5 — Battle sign in skull fracture

### Q7 — Post-thyroidectomy failed extubation: most likely cause

Breathing difficulties and failed extubation after total thyroidectomy — most likely cause?

- A. Superior laryngeal nerve injury
- B. Unilateral recurrent laryngeal nerve injury
- C. Bilateral recurrent laryngeal nerve injury

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

### Q9 — Refractory epistaxis: next step after packing

Epistaxis persists after pinching and nasal packing. Next step?

- A. Ligation of external carotid artery
- B. Ligation of internal carotid artery

### Q10 — Post-tonsillectomy haemorrhage management

### Q13 — Post-COVID mucormycosis: confirmatory investigation

60-year-old with uncontrolled DM, facial swelling, black nasal lesions post-COVID. Confirmatory investigation?

- A. X-ray paranasal sinuses
- B. Biopsy with histopathological examination

### Q18 — Inspiratory stridor location

- A. Supraglottic

### Q19 — Trotter's method for epistaxis

The technique shown for initial epistaxis control (pressure on the Kiesselbach's area) is:

- A. Epley's manoeuvre
- B. Trotter's method
- C. McGovern's technique
- D. Valsalva manoeuvre

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Trotter's method** — Pinching the cartilaginous nose (alae) firmly for 5–10 minutes with the patient sitting forward. Compresses Kiesselbach's plexus (Little's area) — site of 90% of epistaxis. First-line management.
</details>

### Q20 — Juvenile nasopharyngeal angiofibroma features

Recurrent profuse unilateral epistaxis in an adolescent male with a vascular nasopharyngeal mass and bowing of the posterior wall of the maxillary sinus. Diagnosis?

- A. Antrochoanal polyp
- B. Rhinosporidiosis
- C. Juvenile nasopharyngeal angiofibroma (JNA)
- D. Rhinoscleroma

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Juvenile nasopharyngeal angiofibroma (JNA)** — Benign but locally aggressive vascular tumour; exclusively in adolescent males; Holman-Miller sign (anterior bowing of posterior wall of maxilla on CT); highly vascular (do NOT biopsy blind); embolisation + surgery.
</details>

**D. Tympanoplasty** — Complete TM absence requires tympanoplasty (Type I = myringoplasty is only for perforations, not total absence). When ossicles also need repair, higher types apply.
</details>

### Q707 — Pure tone audiogram interpretation (image)

A 27-year-old male with 2 months of reduced left-ear hearing. PTA audiogram shows air-bone gap on the left. Most likely?

- A. Left ear SNHL
