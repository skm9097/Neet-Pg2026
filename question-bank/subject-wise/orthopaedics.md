## Joint Disease

### Q1 — Osteoarthritis distribution (PIP, DIP, CMC sparing wrist/MCP)

PIP, DIP and 1st CMC involvement with sparing of wrist/MCP joints. Diagnosis?

- A. Rheumatoid arthritis
- B. Psoriatic arthritis
- C. Pseudogout
- D. Osteoarthritis

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. Osteoarthritis** — Classic OA distribution: DIP (Heberden's nodes), PIP (Bouchard's nodes), 1st CMC (thumb base squaring). RA = MCP, PIP, wrists.
</details>

---
### Q2 — Heberden's nodes location

Heberden's nodes are seen at:

- A. Hip
- B. Knee
- C. MCP
- D. DIP

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. DIP joints** — Distal interphalangeal joint bony swellings of osteoarthritis. Bouchard's nodes = PIP joints. Both are bony, NOT inflammatory.
</details>

---
### Q3 — RA: symmetric small joint with anti-CCP positive

45-year-old woman with symmetric PIP/MCP/wrist involvement and elevated RF + anti-CCP. Diagnosis?

- A. Osteoarthritis
- B. Rheumatoid arthritis
- C. Gout
- D. Psoriatic arthritis

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Rheumatoid arthritis** — Anti-CCP is highly specific (~95%) and predicts erosive disease. Classic distribution: symmetric MCPs, PIPs, wrists, MTPs.
</details>

---
### Q4 — Joint typically spared in osteoarthritis

Which joint is characteristically SPARED in OA?

- A. Hip
- B. Knee
- C. MCP
- D. Spine

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. MCP joints** — Primary OA spares MCPs (and wrists, ankles). MCP involvement suggests RA, haemochromatosis, or psoriatic arthritis. OA classically affects DIPs, PIPs, CMC, hip, knee, spine, 1st MTP.
</details>

### Q5 — Ankylosing spondylitis (young male, morning stiffness, heel pain)

20-year-old male with 2-year lower backache, morning stiffness, bilateral heel pain. Most likely diagnosis?

- A. Tuberculosis of spine
- B. Disc prolapse
- C. Mechanical back pain
- D. Ankylosing spondylitis

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. Ankylosing spondylitis** — Inflammatory back pain (improves with activity), morning stiffness, enthesitis (heel pain), HLA-B27 association, anterior uveitis. Bamboo spine on later X-ray.
</details>

## Paediatric Orthopaedics

### Q6 — Pulled elbow (nursemaid's elbow)

A maid spins a child by hands; child later refuses to use the arm. Diagnosis?

- A. Olecranon fracture
- B. Fracture head of radius
- C. Elbow dislocation
- D. Pulled elbow (radial head subluxation)

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. Pulled elbow** — Radial head subluxation from annular ligament; common in toddlers (1–4 years) with axial traction. Easy reduction: supination + flexion (or hyperpronation).
</details>

### Q7 — Late complication of supracondylar humerus fracture

Most common late complication of supracondylar humerus fracture if left untreated?

- A. Pulled elbow
- B. Dinner-fork deformity
- C. Osteoarthritis
- D. Cubitus varus (gunstock deformity)

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. Cubitus varus** — From malunion of supracondylar fracture. Acute complication: Volkmann's ischaemic contracture (brachial artery injury). Dinner-fork = Colles' fracture.
</details>

### Q8 — Scurvy in 5-year-old with bilateral knee pain

5-year-old with bilateral knee pain, normal BMD, X-ray showing metaphyseal changes (Pelkan spurs, Wimberger ring). Diagnosis?

- A. Rickets
- B. Metaphyseal dysplasia

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. Scurvy** — Vitamin C deficiency in child: ground-glass osteopenia, Wimberger ring (sclerotic epiphyseal ring), Frenkel line (white line at metaphysis), Trummerfeld zone, Pelkan spurs.

### Q9 — Osteogenesis imperfecta on prenatal scan

13-week IUS shows fetus with multiple long bone fractures. Likely diagnosis?

- A. Achondroplasia
- B. Cretinism
- C. Marfan syndrome
- D. Osteogenesis imperfecta

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

## Trauma & Fractures

### Q11 — Garden's Stage 4 femoral neck

Garden's Stage 4 femoral neck fracture features:

- A. Displacement from acetabulum
- B. Partial alignment with intact trabeculae
- C. Angulation/rotation with disrupted trabeculae
- D. Trabecular alignment of acetabulum and femoral head but not the neck

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. Trabecular alignment of acetabulum and femoral head but not the neck** — Garden's IV: completely displaced fracture but realigned trabeculae (rotational realignment). High risk of avascular necrosis → hemiarthroplasty/THR.
</details>

### Q13 — Compartment syndrome (tibia fracture)

Closed tibial fracture, severe disproportionate pain, pain on passive dorsiflexion, loss of sensation in 1st web space. Next step?

- A. Elevate and observe
- B. Immediate fasciotomy
- C. Opioid analgesics
- D. Apply cast

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

</details>

### Q14 — Volkmann's ischaemic contracture

10-year-old with forearm tense, tender, decreased sensation, marked limitation of wrist/finger extension after supracondylar fracture. Diagnosis?

- A. Radial nerve injury
- B. Volkmann's ischaemic contracture
- C. Compartment syndrome (acute)
- D. Flexor tendon injury

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Volkmann's ischaemic contracture** — Sequelae of forearm compartment syndrome (brachial artery injury); fibrosis of deep flexors → claw hand. Preventable if early fasciotomy done.
</details>

## Knee Pathology

### Q30 — ACL injury tests

Soccer player with positive Lachman and anterior drawer test:

- A. MCL
- B. LCL
- C. ACL
- D. PCL

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. ACL (anterior cruciate ligament)** — Lachman is the most sensitive test (30° flexion, hands on femur and tibia). Anterior drawer (90° flexion) also positive. Pivot shift test confirms rotational instability.
</details>

### Q31 — Valgus stress test

Stress test shown for medial knee stability:

- A. EKG stress test
- B. Treadmill stress test
- C. Valgus stress test
- D. Varus stress test

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Valgus stress test** — Tests medial collateral ligament (MCL); applied with knee at 30° flexion. Varus stress test = LCL.
</details>

### Q32 — Pseudogout (rhomboid, positively birefringent)

45-year-old man, joint discomfort, rhomboid, positively birefringent crystals. Diagnosis?

- A. Gout
- B. Rheumatoid arthritis
- C. Osteoarthritis
- D. Pseudogout (CPPD)

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. Pseudogout (CPPD)** — Calcium pyrophosphate dihydrate crystals: rhomboid, weakly positively birefringent. Gout: needle-shaped, strongly negatively birefringent monosodium urate.
</details>

## Nerve Injury

### Q33 — Neurotmesis

Complete severance of a peripheral nerve is called:

- A. Neurapraxia
- B. Axonotmesis
- C. Neurotmesis
- D. Neuroma

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Neurotmesis** — Seddon's classification: neurapraxia (focal demyelination, conduction block) → axonotmesis (axon disrupted, endoneurium intact) → neurotmesis (complete severance; needs surgical repair).
</details>

---
### Q34 — Pen test for median nerve

The "Pen test" assesses:

- A. Posterior interosseous nerve
- B. Ulnar nerve
- C. Musculocutaneous nerve
- D. Median nerve

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. Median nerve** — Pen test: patient touches the pen held above the thumb in radial abduction; tests abductor pollicis brevis (median nerve, recurrent branch). Failure = carpal tunnel/median nerve injury.
</details>

---
### Q35 — End-to-side neurorrhaphy

End-to-side neurorrhaphy of cutaneous nerve is used for:

- A. Motor regeneration
- B. Sensory regeneration
- C. Mixed motor and sensory
- D. Autonomic only

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Mixed motor and sensory neuron regeneration** — Axons from a donor nerve sprout collaterally into the side of a recipient nerve to restore sensation/motor function without sacrificing donor function.
</details>

### Q36 — Cobb's angle

Method of scoliosis measurement on X-ray:

- A. Bohler's angle
- B. Ferguson angle
- C. Bauman's angle
- D. Cobb's angle

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. Cobb's angle** — Angle between lines perpendicular to the most tilted vertebrae at the upper and lower ends of the curve. > 10° = scoliosis; > 50° in adults often needs surgery.
</details>

**A. Complete tear of supraspinatus** — Painful arc (60°–120° of shoulder abduction) requires an intact but impinged supraspinatus; a complete tear abolishes the arc because the tendon no longer impinges — the arm may even drop (positive drop-arm test).

</details>

### Q120 — Rheumatoid arthritis management with deformity

Management of rheumatoid arthritis in a patient with established deformity:

- A. Steroids alone
- B. Methotrexate with steroids
- C. Methotrexate with anti-TNF agent
- D. NSAIDs only until disease modifying drugs fail

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Methotrexate with anti-TNF agent** — Combination of methotrexate + anti-TNF (e.g., etanercept, adalimumab) is superior to monotherapy in limiting joint destruction and disability in established RA with deformity.

</details>

### Q121 — Vitamin D deficiency in a child

A child presents with bony deformities shown on X-ray. The deformities are due to deficiency of which vitamin?

- A. Vitamin A
- B. Vitamin D
- C. Vitamin K
- D. Vitamin C

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Vitamin D** — Nutritional rickets (vitamin D deficiency) causes defective mineralisation of growing bone: bowing of long bones, widened growth plates, cupping/fraying of metaphyses, frontal bossing, rachitic rosary.

</details>
