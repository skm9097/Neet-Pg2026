### Q1 — Proteasome function

The proteasome is primarily responsible for:

- A. Protein folding
- B. Post-translational modification
- C. Protein degradation
- D. Protein sorting

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Protein degradation** — The 26S proteasome degrades ubiquitin-tagged proteins; barrel-shaped multicatalytic protease in cytoplasm and nucleus. Ubiquitin-proteasome pathway = main route for intracellular protein turnover.
</details>

### Q2 — UV absorbance at 280 nm

Which amino acid is responsible for UV absorbance at 280 nm?

- A. Leucine
- B. Proline
- C. Arginine
- D. Tryptophan

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. Tryptophan** — Aromatic amino acids (Trp, Tyr, Phe) absorb UV; tryptophan absorbs maximally at 280 nm and contributes most to protein UV absorbance. Used to quantify protein concentration.
</details>

Which food source has the maximum Vitamin B12 content?

- A. Animal products

- D. Vegetarian diet

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Animal products** — B12 (cobalamin) is found exclusively in animal products (liver, meat, fish, eggs, dairy). Strict vegans are at risk of B12 deficiency. Plant foods have no B12 unless fortified.

### Q4 — Apolipoprotein B48 synthesis mechanism

ApoB-48 (intestinal form) is derived from ApoB-100 mRNA by:

- A. RNA alternate splicing
- B. RNA editing
- C. DNA editing
- D. RNA interference

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. RNA editing** — APOBEC1 enzyme converts CAA (glutamine codon) to UAA (stop codon) at position 2153 of APOB mRNA in intestinal cells → truncated ApoB-48 used in chylomicrons. Liver uses full-length ApoB-100 for VLDL/LDL.
</details>

### Q5 — Van den Bergh reaction in obstructive jaundice

In obstructive (cholestatic) jaundice, van den Bergh reaction gives:

- A. Direct positive
- B. Indirect positive
- C. Both positive
- D. Coproporphyrin positive

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Direct (immediate) positive** — Conjugated (direct) bilirubin is water-soluble; reacts directly with diazo reagent without alcohol. Obstructive jaundice = elevated conjugated (direct) bilirubin → direct van den Bergh reaction.
</details>

Night blindness with follicular hyperkeratosis (phrynoderma) and impaired immunity suggests deficiency of:

### Q7 — Oligomycin blocks mitochondrial respiration

Which drug blocks mitochondrial respiration by inhibiting ATP synthase (Complex V)?

- A. Rotenone (Complex I)
- B. Antimycin A (Complex III)
- C. Oligomycin
- D. 2,4-dinitrophenol

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Oligomycin** — Blocks the proton channel (F₀ subunit) of ATP synthase; inhibits oxidative phosphorylation. DNP = uncoupler (proton leak; heat generation; not an inhibitor). Rotenone blocks Complex I; antimycin A blocks Complex III.
</details>

### Q9 — GAG maintaining corneal transparency

Which glycosaminoglycan maintains corneal transparency?

- A. Keratan sulfate
- B. Chondroitin sulfate
- C. Heparan sulfate
- D. Hyaluronic acid

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

### Q10 — Fasting blood glucose at 7 AM: source

Fasting blood glucose in the early morning primarily comes from:

- A. Hepatic glycogenolysis
- B. Hepatic gluconeogenesis
- C. Muscle glycogenolysis
- D. Dietary glucose

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Hepatic glycogenolysis** — After overnight fast: first ~8–12 hours = hepatic glycogenolysis (main source). Beyond that: gluconeogenesis takes over. Muscle glycogen is not released into blood (no glucose-6-phosphatase). Dawn phenomenon = early morning rise via cortisol/GH.
</details>

### Q11 — G6PD deficiency and haemolytic anaemia (primaquine)

Primaquine-induced haemolytic anaemia in G6PD deficiency involves which metabolic pathway?

- A. Glycolysis
- B. Gluconeogenesis
- C. Hexose monophosphate (pentose phosphate) pathway
- D. Luebering-Rapoport pathway

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Hexose monophosphate (pentose phosphate) pathway** — G6PD is the rate-limiting enzyme of HMP shunt → generates NADPH → maintains glutathione reduced → protects RBCs from oxidative stress. Deficiency → oxidant drugs (primaquine, dapsone) → Heinz bodies → haemolysis.
</details>

Fatty acids cross the inner mitochondrial membrane via:

### Q14 — Single nucleotide mutation creating a stop codon

A point mutation creating a premature stop codon (UAA/UAG/UGA) is called:

- A. Missense mutation
- B. Nonsense mutation
- C. Stop mutation (same as nonsense)
- D. Silent mutation

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Nonsense mutation** — Single nucleotide change converts an amino acid codon to a stop codon → premature chain termination → truncated, usually non-functional protein. Missense = different amino acid; silent = same amino acid.
</details>

### Q15 — DNA packaging protein

DNA is packaged into chromatin using:

- A. Histones
- B. Glycoproteins
- C. Nucleic acid
- D. Adenine

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Histones** — Basic proteins (rich in Lys, Arg); 8 histones form nucleosome core (H2A, H2B, H3, H4, ×2 each); H1 is linker histone. ~147 bp DNA wrapped 1.65 turns around core = nucleosome.
</details>

### Q17 — OTC deficiency: elevated glutamine

Which enzyme deficiency elevates glutamine (and ammonia)?

- A. Arginosuccinate lyase
- B. α-galactosidase A
- C. Ornithine transcarbamoylase (OTC)
- D. Arginase

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Ornithine transcarbamoylase (OTC)** — X-linked, most common urea cycle defect; ammonia accumulates → converted to glutamine (glutamine synthetase) → elevated plasma glutamine; elevated urinary orotic acid; low citrulline.
</details>

### Q18 — Methyl donor for norepinephrine → epinephrine conversion

Which amino acid provides the methyl group for conversion of norepinephrine to epinephrine?

- A. Tyrosine
- B. Tryptophan
- C. Phenylalanine
- D. Methionine

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. Methionine** — S-adenosylmethionine (SAM, derived from methionine) is the universal methyl donor; PNMT (phenylethanolamine N-methyltransferase) uses SAM to methylate NE → epinephrine in adrenal medulla.
</details>

### Q19 — Zellweger syndrome brain accumulation

In cerebrohepatorenal (Zellweger) syndrome, which substance accumulates in the brain?

- A. Glucose
- B. Very long chain fatty acids (VLCFA)
- C. Lactic acid
- D. Triglycerides

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Very long chain fatty acids (VLCFA)** — Zellweger syndrome: absent peroxisomes → failure of peroxisomal β-oxidation of VLCFA → accumulation in brain, liver, kidneys. Severe hypotonia, seizures, hepatomegaly; fatal in infancy.
</details>

### Q20 — Krebs cycle: α-ketoglutarate diminished by excess ammonia

Which Krebs cycle intermediate is diminished when excess ammonia is converted to glutamate?

- A. Malate
- B. Oxaloacetate
- C. Fumarate
- D. α-ketoglutarate

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. α-ketoglutarate** — Excess NH₃ + α-ketoglutarate → glutamate (glutamate dehydrogenase); depletes α-KG from Krebs cycle → impaired energy metabolism. Explains brain toxicity in hyperammonaemia (hepatic encephalopathy).
</details>
