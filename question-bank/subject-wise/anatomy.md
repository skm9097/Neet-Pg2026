### Q1 — Anatomical snuff box lateral boundary

Which structures form the **lateral** boundary of the anatomical snuff box?

- A. Extensor pollicis brevis and abductor pollicis longus
- B. Extensor pollicis longus and abductor pollicis brevis
- C. Extensor pollicis longus and extensor pollicis brevis
- D. Abductor pollicis longus and abductor pollicis brevis

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Extensor pollicis brevis and abductor pollicis longus** — Lateral (radial) boundary. Medial (ulnar) boundary = extensor pollicis longus. Floor = scaphoid and trapezium; radial artery passes through.
</details>

### Q2 — Facial nerve trunk identification

Which landmark is NOT used for identifying the facial nerve trunk?

- A. Retrograde dissection from distal branch
- B. Tragal pointer
- C. Inferior belly of omohyoid muscle
- D. Posterior belly of digastric

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Inferior belly of omohyoid** — Omohyoid is a neck muscle with no relationship to the parotid/facial nerve. Standard landmarks: tragal pointer (1 cm medial and inferior), posterior belly of digastric, tympanomastoid suture, stylomastoid foramen.
</details>

### Q3 — Fetal anatomical closure timing

Which statement regarding anatomical closures is **incorrect**?

- A. Foramen ovale closes by 3 to 5 days
- B. Ductus venosus closes by 2 to 3 weeks
- C. Right umbilical vein is absent after birth
- D. Ductus arteriosus closes by 2 to 3 weeks

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Foramen ovale closes by 3–5 days** — Functional closure is immediate at birth (pressure reversal), but anatomical closure (fusion) takes weeks to months. Patent foramen ovale persists in ~25% of adults.
</details>

### Q4 — Superior cerebellar artery thrombosis

A patient presents with ataxia and incoordination. Which artery is thrombosed?

- A. Posterior cerebral
- B. Middle cerebral
- C. Internal carotid
- D. Superior cerebellar

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. Superior cerebellar artery** — SCA occlusion: ipsilateral limb ataxia, Horner syndrome, contralateral pain/temperature loss; also affects nucleus dentatus and spinothalamic tract.
</details>

### Q5 — Sternothyroid and thyroid swelling

Which structure prevents upward extension of thyroid swelling by attaching to the thyroid cartilage?

- A. Pretracheal fascia
- B. Sternothyroid
- C. Thyrohyoid membrane
- D. Ligament of Berry

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Sternothyroid** — Inserts on the thyroid cartilage oblique line; mechanically tethers the thyroid, preventing cephalad expansion. Hence thyroid swellings move on swallowing but rarely extend superiorly to the thyroid cartilage.
</details>

### Q6 — Aneurysm at optic chiasm

An aneurysm most likely to damage the optic chiasma arises from:

- A. Anterior communicating artery
- B. Anterior choroidal artery
- C. Middle cerebral artery
- D. Anterior cerebral artery

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Anterior communicating artery** — AComm lies immediately above the optic chiasm; aneurysm rupture/expansion → bitemporal hemianopia. Most common site for saccular aneurysms.
</details>

### Q7 — Phrenicocolic ligament and spleen

Which structure prevents vertical/downward spleen descent in massive splenomegaly?

- A. Lienorenal ligament
- B. Gastrosplenic ligament
- C. Phrenicocolic ligament
- D. Lienophrenic ligament

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Phrenicocolic ligament** — Connects left colic flexure to diaphragm; acts as a "hammock" supporting the spleen's lower pole. Explains why the spleen enlarges diagonally toward right iliac fossa.
</details>

### Q8 — Circumvallate papillae histology

Identify the papillae described as having a circular groove surrounding them:

- A. Fungiform
- B. Circumvallate
- C. Filiform
- D. Foliate

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Circumvallate (vallate) papillae** — Large, 8–12 in number, arranged in a V-shape (sulcus terminalis); surrounded by a deep groove; taste buds in walls of groove; Von Ebner's glands open into the groove for lipase.
</details>

### Q9 — Musculocutaneous nerve injury (elbow flexion + supination loss)

A 7-year-old with humerus fracture has elbow flexion difficulty, supination loss, and lateral forearm sensory loss. Which nerve is injured?

- A. Median nerve
- B. Radial nerve
- C. Musculocutaneous nerve
- D. Ulnar nerve

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Musculocutaneous nerve** — Innervates biceps brachii (elbow flexion, supination), brachialis (elbow flexion), and coracobrachialis; terminates as lateral cutaneous nerve of forearm (sensory to lateral forearm).
</details>

### Q10 — Lingual nerve in submandibular surgery

During submandibular gland resection, which nerve is most likely damaged?

- A. Nerve to mylohyoid
- B. Inferior alveolar nerve
- C. Lingual nerve
- D. Hypoglossal nerve

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Lingual nerve** — Runs in close proximity to submandibular duct; carries taste and general sensation from anterior 2/3 of tongue; also carries chorda tympani (secretomotor to submandibular gland).
</details>

### Q11 — Falciform ligament

The marked peritoneal fold (structure A) gives rise to:

- A. Gastrosplenic ligament

- D. Gastrophrenic ligament

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

### Q12 — Saphenous nerve injury after varicose vein surgery

Post-varicose vein surgery: sensory loss in medial leg and foot. Which nerve?

- A. Sural nerve
- B. Superficial peroneal nerve
- C. Deep peroneal nerve
- D. Saphenous nerve

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. Saphenous nerve** — Terminal branch of femoral nerve; runs alongside great saphenous vein → at risk during vein stripping; supplies medial leg and medial foot.
</details>

### Q13 — Islets of Langerhans histology

Identify the structure shown as clustered endocrine cells:

- A. Lymph node
- B. Glomerulus
- C. Spleen
- D. Pancreatic islet cells

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. Pancreatic islet cells (Islets of Langerhans)** — α-cells (glucagon), β-cells (insulin, ~70%), δ-cells (somatostatin), PP cells (pancreatic polypeptide). Richly vascularised endocrine clusters in exocrine pancreas.
</details>

### Q14 — Fibrocartilage identification

Cartilage with collagen fibres arranged in parallel bundles is:

- A. Non-articular hyaline cartilage
- B. Articular hyaline cartilage
- C. Elastic (yellow) cartilage
- D. White fibrocartilage

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. White fibrocartilage** — Contains abundant Type I collagen in parallel bundles; found in intervertebral discs (annulus fibrosus), symphysis pubis, menisci; withstands compression and shear.
</details>

### Q15 — Trigeminal nerve division for migraine

Unilateral throbbing headache with photophobia, lacrimation, hemifacial pain on tooth clenching. Which division is involved?

- A. V1 (ophthalmic)
- B. V2 (maxillary)
- C. V3 (mandibular)
- D. All divisions

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. V1 (ophthalmic division)** — Trigeminal neuralgia most commonly involves V2 and V3; but for migraine with photo/phonophobia and periorbital pain, the ophthalmic division (V1) and trigeminal-vascular system are implicated.
</details>

### Q16 — Head rotation joint

Which joint permits rotation of the head (looking left and right)?

- A. Atlanto-occipital
- B. Atlanto-axial (atlanto-axial)
- C. C2–C3
- D. C3–C4

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Atlanto-axial joint** — Pivot joint; the dens (odontoid process) of axis acts as a pivot; ~50% of cervical rotation occurs here. Atlanto-occipital = nodding (flexion/extension).
</details>

### Q17 — Right coronary artery: false statement

Which statement about the right coronary artery is **false**?

- A. Diameter less than left coronary artery
- B. Arises from right aortic sinus
- C. Gives rise to circumflex coronary branch
- D. Right conal artery is its first branch

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Gives rise to circumflex coronary branch** — The circumflex artery is a branch of the LEFT coronary artery (left main → LAD + circumflex). RCA gives conus, SA nodal, right marginal, and PDA branches.
</details>

### Q18 — Omphalocele embryology

Omphalocele formation is based on:

- A. Excessive intestinal length
- B. Physiological hernia failing to return
- C. Liver herniation
- D. Umbilicus herniation

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Physiological hernia failing to return** — Normal fetal midgut herniates into umbilical cord at week 6 and returns by week 10. Failure to return = omphalocele (covered by amnion). Gastroschisis = lateral defect, no sac.
</details>

### Q19 — Internal capsule fibre type

The fibres at the genu of the internal capsule in a brain scan are:

- A. Short association fibres
- B. Long association fibres
- C. Projection fibres
- D. Commissural fibres

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Projection fibres** — The internal capsule contains projection fibres (corticospinal, corticobulbar, thalamocortical, corticopontine). Commissural fibres pass through corpus callosum; association fibres are ipsilateral.
</details>

### Q20 — Calot's triangle boundaries

Which structure does NOT form the boundaries of Calot's triangle?

- A. Common hepatic duct
- B. Cystic duct
- C. Cystic artery
- D. Gallbladder

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. Gallbladder** — Calot's triangle (hepatocystic triangle): bounded by cystic duct (below), common hepatic duct (medially), and liver inferior surface (above). The cystic artery runs within it. Gallbladder is not a boundary.
</details>
