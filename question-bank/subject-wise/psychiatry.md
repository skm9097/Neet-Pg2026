### Q1 — Formication in cocaine abuse

A patient describes feeling insects crawling under the skin (formication). Which substance?

- A. Cannabis
- B. Cocaine
- C. Amphetamine
- D. Alcohol

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Cocaine** — Cocaine-induced formication ("cocaine bugs" / Magnan's sign); tactile hallucination of insects crawling under skin due to sympathomimetic excess. Also in alcohol withdrawal delirium tremens.
</details>

### Q2 — Grief with psychotic features

60-year-old widower with guilt, somatic delusions, persistent profound sadness. Diagnosis?

- A. Normal grief reaction
- B. Psychotic depression
- C. Delusional disorder
- D. Schizophrenia

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Psychotic depression (Major depressive disorder with psychotic features)** — Depression with mood-congruent delusions (guilt, poverty, somatic) and/or hallucinations. Requires antidepressant + antipsychotic or ECT.
</details>

### Q3 — Postpartum blues management

Preferred management for postpartum blues (days 3–5)?

- A. Fluoxetine
- B. Cognitive behavioural therapy
- C. Lithium carbonate
- D. Support and education

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. Support and education** — Postpartum blues: transient, self-limiting (< 2 weeks), mild tearfulness, mood lability; no pharmacotherapy needed. Reassure and support. Postpartum depression (> 2 weeks, significant impairment) → antidepressants.
</details>

### Q4 — Restless legs syndrome: first-line drug

25-year-old with "creepy crawling" leg sensations at night, relieved by movement. First-line treatment?

- A. Gabapentin
- B. Pramipexole (dopamine agonist)
- C. Vitamin B12
- D. Iron tablets

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Gabapentin** — Current guidelines (2016 IRLSSG): alpha-2-delta ligands (gabapentin enacarbil, pregabalin) are first-line for RLS. Dopamine agonists (pramipexole, ropinirole) are also effective but risk augmentation with long-term use.
</details>

### Q5 — Panic disorder (exam-related symptoms)

MBBS student with sweating, palpitations, sense of imminent danger before exams; symptoms resolve after. Diagnosis?

- A. Panic disorder
- B. Conversion disorder
- C. Social anxiety disorder
- D. Generalised anxiety disorder

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Panic disorder** — Recurrent discrete episodes of intense fear/discomfort with somatic symptoms (palpitations, sweating, trembling), peaking within minutes. Note: if fear is specifically social situations → social anxiety disorder; if excessive worry → GAD.
</details>

### Q6 — Therapist's emotional response to patient

The therapist's conscious or unconscious emotional reactions to the patient are called:

- A. Countertransference
- B. Transference
- C. Dissociation
- D. Preoccupation

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Countertransference** — Therapist's feelings toward patient (can be positive or negative); must be recognised and managed. Transference = patient's feelings toward therapist (based on past relationships).
</details>

### Q7 — Delirium tremens

3 days of alcohol abstinence → irritability, disorientation, paranoia, agitation, hallucinations. Diagnosis?

- A. Delirium tremens
- B. Wernicke's encephalopathy
- C. Korsakoff psychosis
- D. Alcohol-induced psychosis

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Delirium tremens** — Onset 48–72 hours after last drink; peak at 3–5 days; features: global confusion, tremor, autonomic hyperactivity, vivid (often visual/tactile) hallucinations. Mortality if untreated; treat with benzodiazepines + thiamine.
</details>

### Q8 — Handwashing skill: type of learning

Teacher demonstrates handwashing; students practise at school and home. Learning domain?

- A. Cognitive
- B. Psychomotor
- C. Affective
- D. Affective and cognitive

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Psychomotor** — Bloom's taxonomy: psychomotor domain = physical/motor skills requiring practice (e.g., handwashing, surgical technique). Cognitive = knowledge/reasoning; affective = attitudes/values.
</details>

### Q9 — Formal thought disorder: tangential speech

Disrupted thought flow with tangential, loosely connected speech. Diagnosis?

- A. Obsession
- B. Derailment (knight's move thinking)
- C. Delusion
- D. Thought insertion

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Derailment** — Loose associations / derailment: ideas slip off track onto unrelated tangents. Formal thought disorder in schizophrenia. Flight of ideas (mania) = rapid, connected associations. Thought insertion = alien thought placed in mind (FRS of schizophrenia).
</details>

### Q10 — Adolescent mania

16-year-old with overfamiliarity, racing thoughts, elevated mood, increased sexuality, pseudo-hallucinations. Diagnosis?

- A. Mania
- B. Hypomania
- C. Cyclothymia
- D. Schizomania

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Mania** — Elevated/irritable mood + grandiosity + decreased sleep + pressured speech + increased goal-directed activity; severe enough to require hospitalisation or cause marked impairment. Hypomania = similar but milder, no hospitalisation needed.
</details>

### Q11 — Mental Healthcare Act 2017: voluntary admission duration

Maximum duration for self-admission under MHA 2017?

- A. 48 hours
- B. 7 days
- C. 30 days
- D. 90 days

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. 30 days** — Under MHA 2017: voluntary (independent) patient can self-admit for up to 30 days; can leave with 24 hours notice. Supported admission (by nominated representative) has different provisions.
</details>

### Q12 — EEG in deep sleep

Sawtooth waves on EEG indicate which sleep stage?

- A. Stage 1 (NREM)
- B. Stage 2 (NREM)
- C. Stage 3 (NREM/slow-wave)
- D. REM sleep

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. REM sleep** — Sawtooth waves are characteristic of REM sleep (also low-voltage mixed-frequency activity, muscle atonia, rapid eye movements, vivid dreaming). Stage 2 NREM has sleep spindles and K-complexes; Stage 3 has delta waves.
</details>

### Q13 — Major depression with psychotic features (DSM-5)

Middle-aged female: 6-month depression + command hallucination to harm herself. DSM-5 diagnosis?

- A. Major depressive disorder with psychotic features
- B. Schizophrenia
- C. Schizoaffective disorder
- D. Delusional disorder

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Major depressive disorder with psychotic features** — Hallucinations/delusions that occur only during depressive episodes = MDD with psychotic features (mood-congruent). Schizoaffective = psychosis present even when mood symptoms absent.
</details>

### Q14 — Bulimia nervosa

16-year-old female with food cravings, excessive eating, and self-induced vomiting. Diagnosis?

- A. Anorexia nervosa
- B. Bulimia nervosa
- C. Atypical depression
- D. Binge eating disorder

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Bulimia nervosa** — Recurrent binge eating + compensatory behaviours (vomiting, laxatives, fasting, exercise); normal or above-normal weight. Russell's sign (calluses on dorsum of hand), dental erosion, parotid enlargement.
</details>

### Q15 — Postpartum blues (Day 4)

Day 4 postpartum: tearfulness, mood swings, occasional insomnia. Diagnosis?

- A. Postpartum depression
- B. Postpartum blues
- C. Postpartum psychosis
- D. Postpartum anxiety

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Postpartum blues** — Baby blues: onset day 3–5, peaks day 5, resolves by day 10–14; transient tearfulness, lability, fatigue. No medication needed; reassurance. PPD = > 2 weeks; PP psychosis = onset within 1–2 weeks, severe/psychotic.
</details>

### Q16 — Smoking cessation: nicotine receptor drug

Drug targeting nicotine receptors for smoking cessation?

- A. Busulfan
- B. Acamprosate
- C. Varenicline
- D. Gabapentin

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Varenicline** — Partial agonist at α4β2 nicotinic acetylcholine receptors; reduces craving (agonist effect) and blocks reward from smoking (antagonist effect). Acamprosate = alcohol dependence (GABA/glutamate); bupropion = another smoking cessation drug.
</details>

### Q17 — Somnambulism: incorrect statement

Which statement about somnambulism (sleepwalking) is INCORRECT?

- A. Disorder of sleep arousal
- B. Person has full consciousness during episode
- C. Terminates with awakening or confusion
- D. May have neurological associations

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Person has full consciousness** — Sleepwalking occurs during NREM Stage 3 slow-wave sleep; the person is NOT conscious — they have no awareness and typically no memory of the episode. NREM parasomnias = disorders of arousal from deep sleep.
</details>

### Q18 — Delusional disorder: NOT a risk factor

Which factor does NOT contribute to development of delusional disorder?

- A. Recent immigration
- B. Social isolation
- C. Family history of delusional disorder
- D. Young age

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. Young age** — Delusional disorder typically onset in middle to late adulthood (40–55 years). Risk factors: immigration, social isolation, sensory impairment, family history of paranoid disorders. Young age is NOT a risk factor.
</details>

### Q19 — Sexual dysfunction in premature ejaculation

Premature ejaculation affects which phase of the sexual response cycle?

- A. Desire phase
- B. Arousal phase
- C. Orgasm phase
- D. Pain (dyspareunia)

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Orgasm phase** — Ejaculation is part of the orgasm phase; premature ejaculation = ejaculation occurring before or shortly after penetration. Treatment: SSRIs (dapoxetine), behavioural techniques (squeeze/stop-start).
</details>

### Q20 — Confusion Assessment Method (CAM) application

The Confusion Assessment Method (CAM) diagnostic tool is used for:

- A. Delirium
- B. Dementia
- C. Schizophrenia
- D. Depression

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Delirium** — CAM criteria: (1) Acute onset with fluctuating course + (2) Inattention + (3) Disorganised thinking OR (4) Altered level of consciousness. Positive CAM = delirium. Sensitive and specific for bedside delirium screening.
</details>
