## Toxicology

### Q1 — Drug causing physical dependence

Which drug most commonly leads to physical dependence?

- A. Ketamine
- B. Heroin
- C. LSD
- D. Phencyclidine

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Heroin** — Opioids cause strong physical dependence with severe withdrawal. LSD/Ketamine = psychological but not physical dependence. PCP = mostly psychological.
</details>

### Q2 — Datura poisoning antidote

Boy ate fruit, developed dry hot skin, mydriasis, urinary retention, agitation. Poison and antidote?

- A. Datura — Pralidoxime
- B. Datura — Physostigmine
- C. Yellow oleander — Digoxin antibody
- D. Yellow oleander — Physostigmine

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Datura — Physostigmine** — Datura (atropine, scopolamine, hyoscyamine): anticholinergic toxidrome ("dry as a bone, blind as a bat, red as a beet, mad as a hatter, hot as a hare"). Antidote: physostigmine (crosses BBB).
</details>

### Q3 — Arsenic in skeletonised remains

Which poison persists in skeletonised remains?

- A. Arsenic
- B. Carbon monoxide
- C. Hydrogen sulphide
- D. Phosphorus

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Arsenic** — Heavy metal; concentrates in hair, nails, bones; remains detectable years after death. Used in forensic toxicology for historic exhumations.
</details>

### Q5 — Cocaine toxicity: NOT a feature

Young IV cocaine user has diaphoresis, headache, coronary spasm. Which symptom CONTRADICTS cocaine?

- A. Myocardial ischaemia
- B. Bradycardia
- C. Hyperthermia
- D. Agitation

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Bradycardia** — Cocaine is sympathomimetic → TACHYCARDIA, hypertension, hyperthermia, myocardial ischaemia, mydriasis, agitation. Bradycardia would suggest β-blocker or opioid.
</details>

### Q6 — Aldrich-Mees lines and rain-drop pigmentation

Aldrich-Mees lines (transverse white nail bands) + rain-drop pigmentation:

- A. Arsenic
- B. Thallium
- C. Cadmium
- D. Lead

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Arsenic** — Chronic arsenic poisoning: Mees lines (nails), rain-drop hyperpigmentation, palmoplantar hyperkeratosis, peripheral neuropathy, increased skin cancer risk.
</details>

### Q7 — Lead poisoning enzyme inhibition

Battery recycling worker with foot drop, anaemia, memory loss, elevated blood lead. Which enzyme is inhibited?

- A. δ-aminolevulinic acid (ALA) dehydratase
- B. Uroporphyrinogen III synthase
- C. Uroporphyrinogen I synthase
- D. Pyruvate dehydrogenase

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. ALA dehydratase** — Lead inhibits ALA dehydratase and ferrochelatase → impaired heme synthesis → microcytic anaemia + elevated free erythrocyte protoporphyrin + basophilic stippling.
</details>

### Q8 — Toxic substance in Indian spinach

Toxic component in Indian spinach (and rhubarb, beet leaves):

- A. Cyanide
- B. Solanine
- C. Hypoglycin
- D. Oxalic acid

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. Oxalic acid** — Renal toxicity (calcium oxalate stones, ATN); also chelates calcium → tetany. Acute poisoning: GI corrosive injury, hypocalcaemia.
</details>

## Wounds

### Q9 — Defence wounds location

Wound on palmar surface of 1st web space, sustained during defence:

- A. Defence wound
- B. Antemortem wound (other)
- C. Postmortem wound
- D. Hesitation cuts

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Defence wound** — Caused while warding off attack; typically on palmar aspect of hands, forearms (ulnar side), or fingers. Indicates the victim was conscious and resisted.
</details>

### Q10 — Incised-looking laceration

Wound appearing linear and incised but caused by blunt object (over bony surface):

- A. Laceration
- B. Incised wound
- C. Laceration-looking incised wound
- D. Incised-looking laceration

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. Incised-looking laceration** — Over scalp/eyebrow/shins: blunt impact splits skin against underlying bone → linear wound mimicking incised wound. Edges are abraded/contused (unlike true incised wounds).
</details>

---

### Q11 — Pistol close shot (powder burns, no soot)

Gunshot wound with powder burns but no soot or stippling:

- A. Shotgun, intermediate range
- B. Shotgun, close range
- C. Pistol, near shot
- D. Pistol, close shot

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Pistol, near shot** — Range classification by surrounding features: contact (muzzle imprint), close (soot + tattoo), near (tattoo only, no soot), distant (no powder marks). Note: "powder burns" terminology is imprecise — refers to tattooing.
</details>

### Q12 — Quaternary blast injury

Quaternary blast injuries include:

- A. Primary (over-pressure)
- B. Secondary (penetrating fragments)
- C. Tertiary (displacement)
- D. Other (burns, crush, psychiatric trauma)

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. Other (psychiatric trauma, burns, toxic exposure)** — Quaternary = all other injuries from blast (burns, crush, inhalation, exacerbation of existing illness, PTSD).
</details>

### Q13 — Abrasion healing timeline

Time taken for abrasion to scab and fall off (complete healing):

- A. < 24 hours
- B. 1–3 days

- A. Diffuse vascular injury
- B. Diffuse axonal injury

### Q17 — Adipocere formation

Adipocere formation:

- A. Preservation by saponification
- B. Requires high temperature
- C. Occurs in cool dry climate
- D. Forms within minutes

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Preservation by saponification** — Hydrolysis of body fat into fatty acids + their conversion to soap-like substance (adipocere). Forms in cool, moist conditions (e.g., bodies in water) over weeks-months. Preserves body features.
</details>

### Q18 — Marbling on autopsy

Post-mortem marbling (greenish-brown discoloration of veins):

- A. Within 12 hours
- B. Due to deoxygenated blood pooling
- C. Result of bacterial activity
- D. Seen in lightning strike

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Bacterial activity** — Putrefaction: bacteria from gut migrate via vessels, produce H₂S which combines with haemoglobin → sulfhaemoglobin → greenish discoloration along superficial veins. Starts ~24–48 hours post-mortem.
</details>

### Q19 — Skin changes in 36-hour water immersion

Skin changes after 36-hour immersion in water:

- A. Torture phalanges
- B. Hanging > 72 hours
- C. Immersion in water for 36 hours

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Immersion in water for 36 hours** — Washerwoman's skin (white, wrinkled, sodden skin), peeling of skin and nails ("glove and stocking peel"); progressively more pronounced with longer immersion.
</details>

## Identification

### Q29 — POCSO age estimation by carpal bones

X-ray shows all carpal bones visible, 1st metacarpal epiphysis not yet united. Age?

- A. 4 years
- B. 7 years
- C. 10 years
- D. 13 years

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. 13 years** — All carpal bones appear by 6–7 years; pisiform last (8–10); 1st metacarpal epiphysis fuses around 16–18 years in females, 18–20 in males. So if visible but not fused = ~13–15.
</details>

### Q30 — Superfecundation (different fathers for twins)

Twins with different DNA fathers. Phenomenon?

- A. Superfetation
- B. Superfecundation
- C. Suppositious child
- D. Atavism

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Superfecundation** — Fertilisation of two ova in the same menstrual cycle by sperm from two different intercourses (possibly different partners). Superfetation = different cycles (very rare).
</details>

### Q31 — Identical twin differences

Identical (monozygotic) twins are similar in ALL except:

- A. Blood group
- B. DNA fingerprinting
- C. Fingerprint pattern
- D. Iris colour

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Fingerprint pattern** — Fingerprints are determined by intrauterine environment (not just genes) → identical twins have different fingerprints. DNA fingerprinting and blood groups are identical.
</details>

In sexual assault of a young child, the hymen is usually:

Which is NOT a form of asphyxia?
