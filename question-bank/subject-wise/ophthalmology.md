### Q1 — Red-green colour blindness (Protanopia)

A 45-year-old male with difficulty distinguishing red and green colours since childhood, normal visual acuity. Diagnosis?

- A. Protanopia
- B. Tritanopia
- C. Deuteranomaly
- D. Cyanopia

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Protanopia** — Red blindness (absent L-cones); X-linked recessive; ~1% of males. Deuteranopia = green blindness (absent M-cones). Tritanopia = blue blindness (rare, autosomal).
</details>

### Q2 — Bilateral retinoschisis

40-year-old with bilateral peripheral vision loss and symmetric peripheral retinal abnormalities (neurosensory retinal splitting), no fluorescein leakage. Diagnosis?

- A. Retinal detachment
- B. Diabetic retinopathy
- C. Retinitis pigmentosa
- D. Retinoschisis

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. Retinoschisis** — Splitting within the neurosensory retina (between inner and outer layers). Bilateral, symmetric, usually inferotemporal. Differs from retinal detachment (separation between retina and RPE).
</details>

### Q3 — Subconjunctival haemorrhage

60-year-old with sudden painless red patch on sclera, no visual disturbance or discharge. Diagnosis?

- A. Subconjunctival haemorrhage
- B. Conjunctivitis
- C. Episcleritis
- D. Scleritis

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Subconjunctival haemorrhage** — Bright red, flat, painless, sharply demarcated; spontaneous or with cough/sneeze/Valsalva/anticoagulation. Resolves in 7–14 days without treatment.
</details>

### Q4 — Ocular myasthenia gravis

35-year-old woman with fluctuating diplopia, ptosis, fatigable extraocular muscle weakness, positive Tensilon test. Diagnosis?

- A. Multiple sclerosis
- B. Ocular myasthenia gravis
- C. Thyroid eye disease
- D. Chronic progressive external ophthalmoplegia

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Ocular myasthenia gravis** — Antibodies to acetylcholine receptors; ptosis + diplopia worsening with use; ice-pack test positive; Tensilon test confirms. Pupil spared (key distinguishing feature). Rx: pyridostigmine, steroids, thymectomy.
</details>

### Q5 — Ectropion surgical repair

42-year-old with 3-week eye redness, lower eyelid turned outward (ectropion), excessive watering. Procedure?

- A. Wheeler procedure
- B. Quickert procedure
- C. Kuhnt-Szymanowski procedure (Kuhnt surgery)
- D. Lateral tarsal split

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Kuhnt-Szymanowski procedure** — For involutional ectropion: combined horizontal lid shortening + lateral canthal tightening. Quickert = entropion repair.
</details>

### Q6 — Primary congenital glaucoma surgery

Surgical procedure of choice for primary congenital glaucoma?

- A. Trabeculectomy
- B. Ahmed valve implantation
- C. Goniotomy
- D. Cyclophotocoagulation

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Goniotomy** — Angle surgery; opens trabecular meshwork from inside the anterior chamber. Trabeculotomy (ab externo) is alternative. Both have high success rates if cornea is clear.
</details>

### Q7 — Severe ptosis with poor LPS function

Severe ptosis with absent levator palpebrae superioris function. Treatment?

- A. Excision of LPS
- B. Frontalis sling surgery
- C. Levator resection
- D. Müllerectomy

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Frontalis sling surgery** — Indicated when LPS function < 4 mm (poor); uses fascia lata, silicone, or Mersilene to attach lid to frontalis muscle. Patient elevates lid by raising eyebrow.
</details>

### Q8 — Lymphangioma/capillary haemangioma laser

Laser for vaporising lymphangiomas and capillary haemangiomas:

- A. Nd:YAG laser
- B. CO₂ laser
- C. Argon laser
- D. Excimer laser

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. CO₂ laser** — Wavelength 10,600 nm; absorbed by water; cuts and vaporises soft tissue. Used for cutaneous/mucosal vascular and lymphatic lesions.
</details>

### Q9 — Keratoconus feature

Characteristic feature of keratoconus:

- A. Irregular astigmatism
- B. Central corneal scarring
- C. Peripheral corneal thinning
- D. Pigment deposition

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Irregular astigmatism** — Conical protrusion of central/inferior cornea; central (not peripheral) thinning. Findings: Vogt's striae, Fleischer ring, Munson's sign. Rx: rigid gas-permeable contact lens, INTACS, corneal cross-linking, keratoplasty.
</details>

### Q10 — Cystoid macular oedema diagnosis

Diagnostic tool for cystoid macular oedema (CME)?

- A. Snellen chart
- B. Fluorescein angiography
- C. Ishihara test
- D. Amsler grid

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Fluorescein angiography** — CME on FA: petalloid hyperfluorescent pattern (late phase). OCT is now the gold standard (non-invasive, shows intraretinal cysts). Amsler grid = patient self-monitoring tool.
</details>

### Q11 — Acute anterior uveitis drug of choice

Drug to relieve pain and prevent posterior synechiae in acute anterior uveitis?

- A. Topical corticosteroids
- B. Cycloplegics
- C. NSAIDs
- D. Immunomodulators

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Cycloplegics** — Atropine/homatropine/cyclopentolate dilate pupil → prevents iris-lens adhesions (posterior synechiae) + reduce ciliary spasm pain. Topical steroids also used for inflammation.
</details>

### Q12 — Episcleritis treatment

Drug of choice for episcleritis?

- A. Topical corticosteroids
- B. Topical antibiotics
- C. Topical or oral NSAIDs
- D. Topical lubricants

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Topical or oral NSAIDs** — Episcleritis is self-limiting; NSAIDs (oral flurbiprofen, naproxen) shorten course. Topical steroids if NSAIDs fail. Scleritis (more severe) = requires oral NSAIDs ± steroids.
</details>

### Q13 — Filamentary keratitis vs filamentous keratitis

Drug for FILAMENTOUS (fungal) keratitis?

- A. Tobramycin
- B. Moxifloxacin
- C. Natamycin
- D. Fluconazole

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Natamycin** — 5% topical natamycin is first-line for filamentous fungal keratitis (Fusarium, Aspergillus). Voriconazole is alternative. Note: filamentary keratitis ≠ filamentous keratitis (filamentary = epithelial mucus strands).
</details>

### Q14 — Hypermetropia and accommodation

Hypermetrope of +4D with near point of 20 cm has to exercise accommodation of:

- A. +11D
- B. +9D
- C. +1D
- D. None of the above

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. +9D** — Total accommodation needed = correction for hyperopia (4D) + power for near point of 20 cm (5D) = 9D.
</details>

### Q15 — Pseudoexfoliation syndrome (already covered in 2025 set)

Elderly patient with white dandruff-like deposits on anterior lens capsule. Diagnosis?

- A. Anterior capsular opacity
- B. Iris cyst
- C. Pseudoexfoliation syndrome
- D. Pigment dispersion syndrome

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Pseudoexfoliation syndrome** — Greyish-white flaky material on lens capsule and pupillary margin; risk of secondary open-angle glaucoma and zonular weakness during cataract surgery.
</details>

### Q16 — Cornea / Endothelium function

Which layer of the cornea primarily maintains its moisture and optical clarity?

- A. Epithelium
- B. Bowman's layer
- C. Stroma
- D. Endothelium

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. Endothelium** — Corneal endothelium (single layer) maintains deturgescence by active Na-K-ATPase pump; does not regenerate in adults. Endothelial cell count <500/mm² → corneal decompensation.

</details>

### Q17 — Corneal ulcer / Fungal characteristic

Distinguishing feature of a fungal corneal ulcer compared to bacterial?

- A. Hypopyon
- B. Satellite lesions
- C. Dendritic pattern

Additional row of eyelashes located posterior to the grey line of the lid margin?

### Q19 — Proptosis / Grave's ophthalmopathy

Middle-aged woman, bilateral proptosis, restricted extra-ocular movements, chemosis, hyperthyroidism — diagnosis?

- A. Orbital pseudotumor
- B. Lymphoma
- C. Grave's ophthalmopathy (thyroid eye disease)
- D. Orbital cellulitis

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

### Q20 — Visual function test / Macular vs optic nerve

Photostress test helps differentiate between which two conditions?

- A. Glaucoma vs cataract
- B. Macular disease vs optic nerve disease
- C. Amblyopia vs refractive error
- D. Corneal opacity vs vitreous haze

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Macular disease vs optic nerve disease** — Photostress recovery time >50 seconds = macular dysfunction. Normal recovery in optic nerve disease. Color desaturation test also helps differentiate (optic nerve = color desaturation).

</details>

### Q21 — Retinal detachment / Shifting fluid

Shifting fluid sign is characteristic of which type of retinal detachment?

- A. Rhegmatogenous retinal detachment
- B. Tractional retinal detachment
- C. Exudative (serous) retinal detachment
- D. Retinoschisis

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Exudative (serous) retinal detachment** — Fluid shifts with position (gravity-dependent), no retinal break, bullous elevation. Causes: Vogt-Koyanagi-Harada, malignant hypertension, choroidal tumor. No shifting fluid in rhegmatogenous (subretinal fluid trapped).

</details>

### Q22 — Strabismus / Paralytic vs concomitant

Secondary deviation > primary deviation, forced duction test negative — type of squint?

- A. Concomitant (comitant) squint
- B. Paralytic (incomitant) squint
- C. Accommodative squint
- D. Sensory squint

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Paralytic squint** — Secondary deviation (non-paretic eye fixing) > primary deviation (paretic eye fixing) = Hering's law of equal innervation. Forced duction test negative = no mechanical restriction.

</details>

### Q23 — Strabismus / Accommodative esotropia

Convergent squint in a small child is most commonly associated with?

- A. Myopia
- B. Hyperopia (hypermetropia)
- C. Astigmatism
- D. Anisometropia

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Hyperopia** — Accommodative esotropia: hyperopic child over-accommodates → excessive convergence (AC/A ratio) → esotropia. Treat with full hyperopic correction (glasses); may need bifocals.

</details>

### Q24 — Uveitis / Sympathetic ophthalmia

Sympathetic ophthalmia occurs after which type of trauma?

- A. Blunt trauma without penetration
- B. Chemical burns
- C. Penetrating trauma to the uveal tissue
- D. Radiation exposure

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Penetrating trauma to uveal tissue** — Autoimmune panuveitis of the contralateral (sympathizing) eye after penetrating injury to uvea; mediated by T-cells sensitized to uveal antigens (retinal S-antigen). Prevent with early enucleation of severely injured eye.

</details>

### Q25 — Diplopia / Abducens nerve palsy

Unilateral AND bilateral abducens (CN VI) palsy — most likely cause respectively?

- A. Orbital trauma; raised ICP
- B. Cavernous sinus thrombosis; raised ICP
- C. Raised ICP; brainstem lesion
- D. Brainstem lesion; cavernous sinus thrombosis

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Cavernous sinus thrombosis (unilateral); raised ICP (bilateral)** — Bilateral VI palsy is a false localizing sign of raised ICP (CN VI has long intracranial course). Unilateral = cavernous sinus/petrous apex/pontine lesion.

</details>

### Q26 — Pupil / Wernicke's hemianopic pupil

Wernicke's hemianopic pupil indicates lesion at?

- A. Optic nerve
- B. Optic chiasma
- C. Optic tract
- D. Lateral geniculate body

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Optic tract** — Pupil light reflex fibers leave the optic tract before LGB → optic tract lesion causes hemianopic pupillary reaction (light in blind hemifield → no pupil response). Also homonymous hemianopia with incongruous defect.

</details>

### Q27 — Conjunctiva / Giant papillary conjunctivitis

Contact lens wearer with watering, redness, foreign body sensation, large papillae on tarsal conjunctiva?

- A. Vernal keratoconjunctivitis
- B. Giant papillary conjunctivitis
- C. Trachoma
- D. Follicular conjunctivitis

### Q28 — Diabetic retinopathy / PDR management

Diabetic with preretinal hemorrhages, neovascularization at disc (NVD), VA 6/9 — management?

- A. Intravitreal anti-VEGF only
- B. Pan-retinal photocoagulation (PRP)
- C. Vitrectomy
- D. Observation with monthly review

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Pan-retinal photocoagulation (PRP)** — High-risk PDR: NVD + vitreous/preretinal hemorrhage → PRP (1200–1600 burns) reduces neovascularization. Anti-VEGF as adjunct. Good VA preserved (6/9) → PRP preferred over vitrectomy.

</details>

### Q29 — Lens / Pseudoexfoliation syndrome

Grey-white fibrillary material deposited on anterior lens surface — diagnosis?

- A. Anterior capsule fibrosis

### Q30 — Ophthalmology / Retinoblastoma

White pupillary reflex (leukocoria), enucleation done, Flexner-Wintersteiner rosettes on biopsy?

- A. Medulloepithelioma
- B. Astrocytic hamartoma
- C. Coats' disease
- D. Retinoblastoma

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. Retinoblastoma** — Malignant intraocular tumor of children; leukocoria; Flexner-Wintersteiner rosettes (photoreceptor differentiation) on histology. Homer-Wright rosettes = neuroblastoma. RB1 gene mutation.

</details>

### Q31 — Optics / Accommodation calculation

Hypermetrope +4D with near point at 20 cm — total accommodation required?

- A. 4D
- B. 5D
- C. 9D
- D. 1D

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. 9D** — Accommodation needed = correction for distance (4D) + near point demand (1/0.2m = 5D) = 9D total. Near point = 20 cm = 5D. Hypermetrope already using 4D for distance → needs 9D total for near.

</details>
