### Q1 — Orphan Annie eye nuclei

A midline neck swelling biopsy shows "Orphan Annie eye" (empty-looking) nuclei. Diagnosis?

- A. Follicular carcinoma
- B. Papillary carcinoma
- C. Toxic nodular goitre
- D. Medullary carcinoma

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Papillary thyroid carcinoma** — Ground-glass/Orphan Annie eye nuclei (nuclear clearing due to marginated chromatin), nuclear grooves, and psammoma bodies are hallmarks. Most common thyroid malignancy.
</details>

### Q2 — Rapidly progressive GN (crescents): investigation

51-year-old male, hematuria, pedal edema, creatinine 9, renal biopsy shows crescents. Which investigation confirms etiology?

- A. ANA
- B. Anti-GBM antibodies
- C. HIV RNA
- D. Urine immunoelectrophoresis

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Anti-GBM antibodies** — Goodpasture syndrome: anti-GBM antibody causes linear IgG deposition on GBM + alveolar BM (pulmonary-renal syndrome). Crescentic GN = rapidly progressive GN Type I.
</details>

### Q3 — Myxoid retroperitoneal mass with t(12;16)

50-year-old with retroperitoneal mass; biopsy shows myxoid features; molecular analysis: t(12;16). Diagnosis?

- A. Myxoid liposarcoma
- B. Lipoma
- C. Spindle lipoma
- D. Polymorphic lipoma

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Myxoid liposarcoma** — t(12;16)(q13;p11) creates FUS-DDIT3 fusion. Myxoid matrix with lipoblasts and plexiform capillary pattern. Most common liposarcoma subtype in thigh/retroperitoneum.
</details>

### Q4 — Large friable irregular valve vegetations

"Large, friable, irregular vegetation on heart valves" is characteristic of:

- A. Infective endocarditis
- B. Rheumatic heart disease
- C. Libman-Sacks endocarditis
- D. Non-bacterial thrombotic (marantic) endocarditis

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Infective endocarditis** — Large, irregular, destructive vegetations that destroy valve leaflets. Rheumatic: small, warty vegetations on closure lines. Libman-Sacks (SLE): small, flat, on both sides of leaflets.
</details>

### Q5 — Macrocytic anemia in alcoholic

23-year-old male, Hb 9, MCV 101, PBS shows macrocytic RBCs and hypersegmented neutrophils. Diagnosis?

- A. Lead poisoning
- B. Iron deficiency anaemia
- C. Hemolytic anaemia
- D. Chronic alcoholism

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. Chronic alcoholism** — Causes macrocytosis via direct toxic effect on marrow + folate deficiency. Hypersegmented neutrophils suggest megaloblastic change. Check B12/folate, reticulocyte count.
</details>

### Q6 — Black pigment in hilar lymph node

70-year-old with SCC of lung; hilar lymph node shows 1 cm black pigment. Substance?

- A. Anthracotic pigment

- C. Lipochrome (lipofuscin)

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Anthracotic pigment** — Carbon/coal dust particles accumulate in macrophages and lymph nodes; inert, common in urban/smoker autopsies. "Anthracosis" = black pigmentation of lungs/nodes.
</details>

### Q7 — CML: most sensitive investigation

30-year-old male, massive splenomegaly, TLC 150,000/µL, 60% neutrophils, 6% basophils, 4% eosinophils, M:E ratio 18:1. Most sensitive investigation?

- A. Immunophenotyping
- B. LAP score
- C. FISH and PCR
- D. Flow cytometry

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. FISH and PCR** — Detect BCR-ABL1 fusion (Ph chromosome, t(9;22)) with highest sensitivity. LAP score is decreased in CML (useful but less sensitive than molecular methods). Gold standard = RT-PCR for BCR-ABL transcripts.
</details>

### Q8 — Turner syndrome

Girl with short stature, webbed neck, thick calves, shield-shaped chest, ankle swelling, increased carrying angle. Diagnosis?

### Q9 — Benzene exposure and cancer predisposition

Occupational benzene exposure: predisposed to which cancer?

- A. Leukemia
- B. Lung cancer
- C. Bladder cancer
- D. Skin cancer

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Leukemia (AML)** — Benzene is a myelotoxin and human carcinogen; causes aplastic anaemia and increases risk of AML. Also associated with MDS, ALL, and NHL.
</details>

### Q10 — Iron deficiency anemia: what is NOT increased

In iron deficiency anemia, which parameter is NOT increased?

- A. Transferrin saturation
- B. RBC protoporphyrin
- C. Total iron binding capacity (TIBC)
- D. Soluble transferrin receptor

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Transferrin saturation** — In IDA: serum iron ↓, TIBC ↑, transferrin saturation ↓ (< 15%), ferritin ↓, RBC protoporphyrin ↑, soluble transferrin receptors ↑.
</details>

### Q11 — Telomere theory of aging

Correct statement about telomere theory of aging?

- A. Telomere stability associated with aging
- B. Abnormal telomerase activation associated with aging
- C. Decreased telomere length associated with aging
- D. Increased telomere length associated with aging

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Decreased telomere length associated with aging** — Each cell division → telomere shortening → replicative senescence (Hayflick limit). Cancer cells reactivate telomerase to maintain telomere length (immortality).
</details>

### Q12 — Beta thalassemia on Hb electrophoresis

Child with recurrent chest infections, abdominal pain, delayed growth, bone problems, icterus, mild splenomegaly. Hb electrophoresis shown. Diagnosis?

- A. Beta thalassemia
- B. HbC disease
- C. Sickle cell disease
- D. Acute chest syndrome

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Beta thalassemia** — β-thalassemia major: severe haemolytic anaemia, hepatosplenomegaly, bony changes (hair-on-end skull), growth retardation. Hb electrophoresis: predominantly HbF, absent or markedly reduced HbA.
</details>

### Q13 — Warburg metabolism in cancer cells

Why do neoplastic cells utilise Warburg (aerobic glycolysis) metabolism?

- A. Decreases glucose utilisation
- B. Forms metabolic intermediates needed for growth and multiplication
- C. Provides more ATP production
- D. Prevents apoptosis

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Forms metabolic intermediates needed for cell growth** — Warburg effect: glucose → lactate even in O₂ presence. Generates biosynthetic precursors (nucleotides, amino acids, lipids) via pentose phosphate pathway, supporting rapid proliferation. ATP production is less efficient but intermediates are more important.
</details>

### Q14 — Mechanical valve haemolytic anaemia

Elderly patient, hemoglobinuria, elevated bilirubin and LDH, schistocytes on PBS. Associated with?

- A. Splenomegaly
- B. Mechanical second heart valve
- C. Increased HbA2
- D. Goitre

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Mechanical heart valve** — Microangiopathic haemolytic anaemia (MAHA): mechanical shear of RBCs → schistocytes (helmet cells), hemoglobinuria, elevated LDH/bilirubin, low haptoglobin. Also in DIC, TTP, HUS.
</details>

### Q15 — NETs: false statement

All are true about neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs) EXCEPT:

- A. Detected in blood during sepsis
- B. Produced in response to bacterial infection
- C. Contain mitochondrial DNA
- D. Are chromatin with antibacterial enzymes

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Contain mitochondrial DNA** — NETs are extracellular webs of nuclear chromatin (histones + DNA) decorated with antimicrobial proteins (elastase, MPO, defensins). They trap bacteria but also contribute to tissue injury. The DNA is nuclear, not mitochondrial.
</details>

### Q16 — Fragile X syndrome: false feature

All are features of Fragile X syndrome EXCEPT:

- A. Trinucleotide (CGG) repeat mutation in FMR-1 gene
- B. Intellectual disability (mental retardation)
- C. Microorchidism
- D. Large everted ears

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Microorchidism** — Fragile X causes MACRO-orchidism (large testes), not micro-orchidism. Other features: long face, prominent jaw, large ears, autism spectrum features, mitral valve prolapse.
</details>

### Q17 — Hemochromatosis: liver microscopy

55-year-old with hepatomegaly, dark tan without sun exposure. Liver microscopy shows iron overload. Diagnosis?

- A. Wilson's disease
- B. Autoimmune hepatitis
- C. Alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency
- D. Hemochromatosis

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. Hemochromatosis** — HFE gene mutation (C282Y); triad: liver cirrhosis + diabetes + skin bronzing ("bronze diabetes"). Prussian blue stain shows iron in hepatocytes. Elevated serum ferritin, transferrin saturation > 45%.
</details>

### Q18 — Punched-out esophageal ulcers

"Punched-out ulcers" in the esophagus are caused by:

- A. Herpes simplex virus
- B. Cytomegalovirus
- C. Candida
- D. Gastric acid

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Herpes simplex virus** — HSV esophagitis: small, well-demarcated "punched-out" ulcers with raised edges. CMV: large, solitary, shallow ulcers. Candida: white plaques/pseudomembranes (not ulcers). Common in immunocompromised.
</details>
