## Anatomy & Embryology

### Q1 — Wolffian duct remnants

Wolffian (mesonephric) duct remnants in females are found in:

- A. Pouch of Douglas
- B. Leaves of broad ligament
- C. Uterovesical pouch
- D. Iliac fossa

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Leaves of broad ligament** — Gartner's duct (mesonephric duct remnant) lies in the broad ligament. May form Gartner's duct cysts in the lateral vaginal wall.

The most common site of fertilisation in the fallopian tube is:

- A. Ampulla
- B. Isthmus

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

## Mullerian Anomalies

### Q7 — Uterus didelphys: NOT a complication

Uterus didelphys is NOT typically complicated by:

- A. Recurrent abortion
- B. Endometriosis
- C. Preterm labour
- D. Transverse lie

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. Transverse lie** — Two separate uterine horns; foetus typically lies longitudinally. Recurrent loss, preterm labour, endometriosis (retrograde menstruation) are common complications.
</details>

### Q8 — Unicornuate uterus on HSG

HSG showing a single banana-shaped uterine cavity with one fallopian tube suggests:

- A. Normal uterus
- B. Unicornuate uterus
- C. Bicornuate uterus
- D. Arcuate uterus

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Unicornuate uterus** — Failure of one Müllerian duct to develop. Associated with renal anomalies (ipsilateral renal agenesis in 40%). Increases miscarriage/preterm risk.
</details>

## Pregnancy Management

### Q13 — Warfarin transition at 36 weeks

Pregnant woman on warfarin at 36 weeks. Management?

- A. Stop warfarin, start LMWH
- B. Stop warfarin, start unfractionated heparin
- C. Continue warfarin until delivery
- D. Stop warfarin, start LMWH + aspirin

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Stop warfarin, start LMWH** — Warfarin crosses placenta (teratogen + foetal bleeding). Switch to LMWH (does not cross placenta) by ~36 weeks; stop LMWH 24 hours before planned delivery.
</details>

### Q15 — Renal agenesis → oligohydramnios

Severe oligohydramnios in second trimester. Most likely foetal cause?

- A. Renal agenesis
- B. Tracheoesophageal fistula
- C. Cardiac abnormalities
- D. Ureteric stricture

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Renal agenesis** — Bilateral renal agenesis → no foetal urine production → severe oligohydramnios (anhydramnios) → Potter sequence (pulmonary hypoplasia, characteristic facies).
</details>

### Q16 — Magnesium sulfate toxicity

Pregnant woman on MgSO4 develops loss of deep tendon reflexes, respiratory depression. Diagnosis?

- A. MgSO4 toxicity
- B. DKA
- C. Eclampsia
- D. Diabetes insipidus

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Magnesium sulfate toxicity** — Mg 7–10 mEq/L: loss of DTRs; 10–13: respiratory paralysis; > 13: cardiac arrest. Antidote: calcium gluconate IV.
</details>

### Q17 — Acute fatty liver of pregnancy timing

Acute fatty liver of pregnancy typically occurs in:

- A. First trimester

- C. Third trimester

**C. Third trimester** — AFLP: rare but life-threatening; late 3rd trimester; LCHAD deficiency in foetus. Microvesicular fatty infiltration. Treatment: immediate delivery.

### Q18 — Hepatitis B in pregnancy

Pregnant woman positive for HBsAg at 14 weeks. True statement?

- A. Significantly increased maternal morbidity

- D. Lamivudine is the first-line drug

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. HBsAg is the first serological marker** — Appears 1–10 weeks post-exposure; precedes symptoms. Most carriers are asymptomatic. Neonatal HBV vaccination + HBIG prevents vertical transmission.

### Q19 — Vaginal changes in pregnancy

Vaginal changes during pregnancy include:

- A. Decreased Lactobacilli
- B. Increased glycogen metabolism
- C. pH > 6
- D. Epithelial thinning

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Increased glycogen metabolism** — Estrogen → epithelial thickening + glycogen accumulation → metabolised by Lactobacilli → lactic acid → acidic pH (3.5–4.5) → protective against infection.
</details>

### Q20 — Gestational diabetes diagnosis

WHO/IADPSG fasting glucose threshold for GDM diagnosis?

- A. 106 mg/dL
- B. 126 mg/dL
- C. 116 mg/dL
- D. 130 mg/dL

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. 126 mg/dL** — Overt diabetes if FBG ≥ 126 mg/dL or HbA1c ≥ 6.5% (uses WHO/DIPSI criteria). GDM (IADPSG): FBG ≥ 92, 1-hr ≥ 180, 2-hr ≥ 153 mg/dL on 75-g OGTT.
</details>

## Pulmonary Physiology in Pregnancy

### Q21 — Decreased parameter in pregnancy

Which respiratory parameter DECREASES in pregnancy?

- A. Respiratory rate
- B. Vital capacity
- C. Functional residual capacity (FRC)
- D. Inspiratory capacity

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Functional residual capacity** — FRC decreases ~20% (diaphragm elevation by gravid uterus); minute ventilation, tidal volume, inspiratory capacity all INCREASE. Respiratory rate is unchanged. PCO₂ drops (compensated respiratory alkalosis).
</details>

## Intrapartum Care

### Q22 — Episiotomy: mediolateral over midline

Advantage of mediolateral over midline episiotomy?

- A. Ease of repair
- B. Quick healing
- C. Less blood loss
- D. Less extension of incision (less 3rd/4th degree tear)

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. Less extension of incision** — Midline has higher risk of extension to anal sphincter (3rd/4th degree tears). Mediolateral has more blood loss and pain but safer for sphincter.
</details>

### Q23 — Velamentous cord insertion complication

Velamentous insertion of umbilical cord (vasa previa). Risk?

- A. Premature rupture of membranes
- B. Postpartum haemorrhage
- C. Foetal exsanguination during labour
- D. Foetal neural tube defects

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Foetal exsanguination** — Vasa previa: foetal vessels traversing membranes over the cervical os; rupture during labour → rapid foetal blood loss → foetal death. Diagnosis: TVUS Doppler; delivery by elective C-section.
</details>

### Q24 — PPH causes: which is NOT

Which is NOT a cause of postpartum haemorrhage?

- A. Placenta previa
- B. Retained placental tissue
- C. Endometritis
- D. Placental polyp

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Placenta previa** — Causes antepartum haemorrhage, not PPH directly. PPH causes (4 Ts): Tone (atony), Trauma, Tissue (retained), Thrombin (coagulopathy). Endometritis → secondary PPH.
</details>

### Q25 — Stress urinary incontinence

Urinary incontinence on coughing/sneezing in multiparous woman:

- A. Overflow incontinence
- B. Urge incontinence
- C. Stress incontinence
- D. Neurogenic bladder

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Stress urinary incontinence** — Loss of urethral support from pelvic floor weakness; leakage on increased intra-abdominal pressure. Treated with pelvic floor exercises (Kegel), mid-urethral sling (TVT/TOT).
</details>

### Q26 — Hymen identification

Hymen with two openings separated by a thin band. Type?

- A. Imperforate hymen
- B. Semilunar hymen
- C. Septate hymen
- D. Annular hymen

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Septate hymen** — A thin band of tissue divides the vaginal opening into two. Imperforate = no opening (cryptomenorrhoea); semilunar = crescentic; annular = circular (most common type).
</details>

After successful vesicovaginal fistula repair:

### Q28 — Induction of labour: NOT a contraindication

Which is NOT an absolute contraindication to labour induction?

- A. Pelvic tumour
- B. Active herpes infection

### Q49 — Johnson's formula for fetal weight
