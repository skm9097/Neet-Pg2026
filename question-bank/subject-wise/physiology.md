### Q1 — High altitude pulmonary oedema: NOT used

Headache and breathlessness at 3000 m altitude. Which is NOT used in management?

- A. IV digoxin
- B. Immediate descent
- C. Oxygen
- D. Acetazolamide

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. IV digoxin** — Not part of HAPE/AMS management. Immediate descent is most effective. Oxygen, acetazolamide (prevention), nifedipine (pulmonary vasodilator for HAPE), dexamethasone (for HACE), sildenafil are used.
</details>

### Q2 — Highest prolactin level timing

Prolactin level is highest during:

- A. 24 hours after ovulation
- B. 24 hours after delivery
- C. REM sleep
- D. After exercise

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. 24 hours after delivery** — Prolactin peaks immediately after delivery for milk production. Note: prolactin also has nocturnal surges, peaks during REM sleep, and rises with stress/exercise — but delivery has the highest physiological peak.
</details>

### Q3 — JVP "c" wave

The "c" wave in JVP represents:

- A. Tricuspid bulge into RA during isovolumetric ventricular contraction
- B. RA contraction
- C. Passive RA filling
- D. Passive RA emptying

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Tricuspid bulge into RA during isovolumetric contraction** — JVP waves: a (atrial contraction), c (tricuspid bulge from RV pressure), x (atrial relaxation), v (atrial filling), y (atrial emptying through open tricuspid).
</details>

### Q4 — GBM charge-dependent filtration

Which protein in glomerular basement membrane provides charge-selective filtration?

- A. Fibronectin
- B. Heparan sulfate
- C. Collagen IV
- D. Entactin

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Heparan sulfate** — Negatively charged GAG in GBM repels negatively-charged albumin → prevents proteinuria. Loss of heparan sulfate (minimal change disease) → albuminuria.
</details>

### Q5 — Vitamin K-dependent factors and γ-carboxyglutamate

Warfarin therapy decreases gamma-carboxyglutamate residues in which factor?

- A. Factor II (prothrombin)
- B. Factor XI
- C. Tissue factor (III)
- D. Factor V

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Factor II (prothrombin)** — Vitamin K-dependent factors (II, VII, IX, X + Proteins C, S) require γ-carboxylation of glutamate residues to bind calcium. Warfarin inhibits Vitamin K epoxide reductase → ↓γ-carboxyglutamate.
</details>

### Q6 — Sodium deficit calculation

70 kg male with serum Na 120 mEq/L. Total Na deficit?

- A. 280 mEq
- B. 480 mEq
- C. 840 mEq
- D. 1400 mEq

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. 840 mEq** — Na deficit = TBW × (target Na − current Na). TBW = 0.6 × 70 = 42 L. Deficit = 42 × (140 − 120) = 840 mEq. Note: replacement must be slow (≤ 8–10 mEq/L per 24 h) to avoid ODS.
</details>

### Q7 — Nerve fibre sensitivity (Saturday night palsy)

Paresis without numbness after arm compression during sleep. Which fibre is more affected?

- A. A fibres more sensitive to pressure than C fibres
- B. A fibres more sensitive to hypoxia than B
- C. A fibres more susceptible to pressure than C
- D. A fibres more susceptible to hypoxia than C

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. A fibres more susceptible to pressure than C fibres** — A (myelinated, motor + touch + proprioception) > B > C (unmyelinated, pain + temperature). Pressure preferentially blocks A fibres → motor weakness with preserved pain (Saturday night palsy).
</details>

### Q8 — Baroreceptor reflex

Baroreceptor reflex is an example of:

- A. Feedforward
- B. Positive feedback
- C. Negative feedback
- D. A & B

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Negative feedback** — ↑BP → carotid sinus stretch → ↑afferent firing → medulla → ↓sympathetic + ↑parasympathetic → ↓HR + ↓BP. Classic negative feedback.
</details>

### Q9 — Paracrine signalling

Cells signalling neighbouring cells via local diffusion:

- A. Paracrine
- B. Autocrine
- C. Endocrine
- D. Merocrine

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Paracrine** — Local diffusion to nearby cells (e.g., histamine, prostaglandins, growth factors). Autocrine = cell signals itself. Endocrine = blood-borne distant signalling.
</details>

### Q10 — Cystometrogram: Laplace law segment

In a cystometrogram, which segment reflects Laplace's law?

- A. Segment Ia (residual urine)
- B. Segment Ib (Laplace law)
- C. Micturition fails in segment II
- D. Micturition in Ib

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Segment Ib is due to Laplace law** — Bladder volume increases without pressure rise initially (Ia); slight rise in Ib follows Laplace's law (P = 2T/r). Steep rise in Segment II = stretched detrusor + micturition reflex.
</details>

### Q11 — Hypothalamic thermostat reset: changes NOT occurring

Resetting hypothalamic thermostat from A to C (febrile response). Which is NOT triggered?

- A. Shivering
- B. Vasoconstriction
- C. Sweating
- D. Epinephrine secretion

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Sweating** — Fever: hypothalamic set-point ↑ → body heat-conserving responses (shivering, vasoconstriction, piloerection, epinephrine release). Sweating occurs when fever breaks (set-point returns to normal).
</details>

### Q12 — Myogenic autoregulation

In myogenic autoregulation, afferent arterioles contract in response to:

- A. NO
- B. Noradrenaline
- C. Calcium
- D. Adenosine

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Calcium** — Stretch of arteriolar smooth muscle → opens stretch-activated Ca²⁺ channels → calcium influx → contraction → maintained GFR despite BP changes. Tubuloglomerular feedback uses adenosine.
</details>

### Q13 — Decerebrate rigidity: NOT characteristic

Which is NOT a feature of decerebrate rigidity?

- A. Rigidity in all body muscles
- B. Increased γ-efferent discharge
- C. Increased motor neuron excitability
- D. Mid-collicular lesion

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Rigidity in all body muscles** — Decerebrate rigidity primarily affects antigravity (extensor) muscles, not all muscles. Caused by mid-collicular lesion → unopposed lateral vestibular and reticular drive → extensor rigidity.
</details>

### Q14 — Decorticate posturing

Decorticate posturing is characterised by:

- A. Cortex/basal ganglia removal
- B. Lower limb flexion with upper limb extension
- C. Pronounced rigidity
- D. Upper limb flexion with lower limb extension

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. Upper limb flexion with lower limb extension** — Decorticate: flexion of arms, fists clenched, legs extended. Lesion above red nucleus (cerebrum). Decerebrate (all extension): lesion below red nucleus (brainstem). Decerebrate is worse prognosis.
</details>

### Q15 — RBC preservation (ACD vs CPDA)

RBC preservation in ACD alone vs ACD + adenine + phosphate:

- A. 35 and 42 days
- B. 21 and 35 days
- C. 28 and 45 days
- D. 35 and 28 days

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. 21 days and 35 days** — ACD (Acid Citrate Dextrose): 21 days. CPDA-1 (Citrate Phosphate Dextrose Adenine): 35 days. SAGM additive: up to 42 days.
</details>

### Q16 — Functional residual capacity

FRC is:

- A. Volume expired after normal inspiration
- B. Volume after forced expiration
- C. ERV + RV (expiratory reserve + residual volume)
- D. TV + IRV

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. ERV + RV** — FRC = volume remaining in lungs after normal tidal expiration = ERV + RV ≈ 2400 mL. Cannot be measured by spirometry (helium dilution or body plethysmography needed).
</details>
