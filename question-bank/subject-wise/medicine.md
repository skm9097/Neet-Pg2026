### Q1 — Huntington's disease

Which is true about Huntington's disease?

- A. Trinucleotide (CAG) repeat disorder
- B. Loss of function mutation
- C. Abnormal CUG repeats
- D. Chromosome 6 mutation

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Trinucleotide (CAG) repeat disorder** — HTT gene on chromosome 4; CAG repeats > 36 → toxic gain of function of mutant huntingtin. Autosomal dominant; caudate atrophy; chorea, dementia, psychiatric symptoms.
</details>

### Q2 — Achalasia cardia pathophysiology

Which is true about achalasia cardia?

- A. LES relaxation present
- B. Increased peristalsis
- C. Decreased NO and VIP release from myenteric plexus
- D. Decreased LES tone

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Decreased NO and VIP release** — Degeneration of inhibitory myenteric neurons (release NO/VIP) → unopposed excitatory input → LES fails to relax + aperistalsis. Bird's beak on barium; manometry gold standard.
</details>

### Q3 — Tertiary hypothyroidism

Which type of hypothyroidism results from hypothalamic failure?

- A. Primary thyroid hypothyroidism
- B. Secondary (pituitary) hypothyroidism
- C. Tertiary (hypothalamic) hypothyroidism
- D. Peripheral hypothyroidism

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Tertiary (hypothalamic) hypothyroidism** — Low TRH → low TSH → low T3/T4. All three levels (TRH, TSH, T4) are low. Distinguished from secondary by TRH stimulation test (TSH rises in tertiary, not secondary).
</details>

### Q4 — Child-Pugh classification

A patient with liver disease scores: bilirubin 3 mg/dL (2 pts), albumin 2.5 g/dL (3 pts), PT INR 2.5 (3 pts), ascites moderate (2 pts), encephalopathy Grade II (2 pts). Total = 12 pts. Class?

- B. Class B
- C. Class C
- D. Class D

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Class C** — Child-Pugh: A = 5–6, B = 7–9, C = 10–15. Class C = poor prognosis (1-year survival ~45%); MELD score has replaced it for organ allocation.

### Q5 — Normal pressure hydrocephalus triad

Elderly woman with gait apraxia, urinary incontinence, and cognitive decline. Diagnosis?

- A. Normal pressure hydrocephalus
- B. Frontotemporal dementia
- C. Parkinson's disease
- D. Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Normal pressure hydrocephalus (NPH)** — Hakim-Adams triad: "Wet, Wacky, Wobbly" — urinary incontinence + dementia + magnetic gait (small steps, wide base). CT/MRI shows hydrocephalus disproportionate to atrophy; responds to LP/shunting.
</details>

### Q6 — Medication overuse headache

A patient with daily headaches who takes analgesics > 10 days/month; headaches worsen with medication and improve with withdrawal. Diagnosis?

- A. Medication overuse headache
- B. Tension headache
- C. Chronic migraine
- D. Cluster headache

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Medication overuse headache (MOH)** — Rebound headache; > 10 days/month of triptans/ergots or > 15 days/month of simple analgesics for > 3 months. Management: gradual withdrawal + prophylaxis.
</details>

### Q7 — Mitral stenosis with AF: anticoagulation

### Q13 — Bilateral facial palsy: sarcoidosis

Bilateral facial nerve palsy. Most likely cause from the options?

- A. Sarcoidosis (Heerfordt syndrome)
- B. Tuberculosis
- C. Lymphoma
- D. Hypersensitivity pneumonitis

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

### Q14 — Endocarditis prophylaxis: NOT indicated

Endocarditis prophylaxis is NOT required in:

- A. Prior infective endocarditis
- B. Atrial septal defect (secundum type)
- C. Unrepaired cyanotic congenital heart disease
- D. Prosthetic heart valves

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Atrial septal defect (secundum type)** — AHA 2007 guidelines: IE prophylaxis only for highest-risk cardiac conditions (prosthetic valve, prior IE, unrepaired cyanotic CHD, cardiac transplant with valvulopathy). Isolated secundum ASD is NOT an indication.
</details>

### Q16 — Duke's criteria: major criterion

Which is a MAJOR Duke criterion for infective endocarditis?

### Q18 — Non-AIDS-defining illness

Which is NOT an AIDS-defining illness?

- A. HIV encephalopathy
- B. Invasive cervical cancer
- C. TB at any site
- D. Oral candidiasis

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. Oral candidiasis** — Oral candidiasis is an AIDS-related condition but NOT on the CDC AIDS-defining illness list. TB (pulmonary or extrapulmonary), cervical cancer, HIV encephalopathy ARE AIDS-defining. Recurrent pneumonia, PCP, CMV retinitis, etc. are also AIDS-defining.
</details>

### Q19 — AML: stem cell transplant donor of choice

AML in first complete remission: best stem cell donor?

- A. Brother (partial HLA match)
- B. Umbilical cord blood
- C. HLA-identical sibling brother, blood group O+
- D. Unrelated HLA-matched donor

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. HLA-identical sibling, O+ blood group** — HLA-matched related donor (sibling) is gold standard for allogeneic SCT; lower GVHD, better outcomes. Blood group O+ is universal donor for RBC but HLA matching is critical.
</details>

### Q20 — PJP diagnosis

HIV patient with CD4 80, bilateral diffuse ground-glass opacities, O₂ sats dropping on exertion. Diagnosis?

- A. Staphylococcal pneumonia
- B. Pneumococcal pneumonia
- C. Tuberculosis
- D. Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia (PJP)

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia (PJP)** — CD4 < 200, insidious onset, bilateral ground-glass opacities (CXR: "bat-wing"), LDH elevated, desaturation on exertion. Dx: BAL with GMS/DFA stain. Rx: TMP-SMX + steroids if PaO₂ < 70 mmHg.
</details>

- A. HIV DNA PCR
- B. Cord blood ELISA
- C. Western blot
- D. Third-generation antibody assay

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. HIV DNA PCR** — Antibody tests are unreliable <18 months due to transplacental maternal antibodies; virological DNA/RNA PCR is used for early infant diagnosis.

</details>

### Q594 — Calcitonin in hyperparathyroidism

Calcitonin levels are increased in:

- A. Hyperthyroidism
- B. Hypoparathyroidism
- C. Hyperparathyroidism

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Production of which hormone is increased in obesity?

### Q602 — Gout with normal serum uric acid

A chronic alcoholic has acute pain and swelling of the great toe, synovial fluid with raised leukocytes, and a normal serum uric acid level. Most likely diagnosis?

- A. Pseudogout
- B. Acute gout
- C. Reactive arthritis
- D. Septic arthritis

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Acute gout** — Serum urate is often normal or low during an acute flare; diagnosis rests on monosodium urate crystals in synovial fluid (negatively birefringent needles).

</details>
