## Airway & Ventilation

### Q1 — Last structure pierced in lumbar puncture

Last structure pierced before entering the lumbar cistern during LP?

- A. Ligamentum flavum
- B. Dura mater
- C. Arachnoid membrane
- D. Pia mater

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Arachnoid membrane** — Order: skin → SC tissue → supraspinous ligament → interspinous ligament → ligamentum flavum → epidural space → dura mater → arachnoid mater → CSF. Pia is adherent to spinal cord and not pierced.
</details>

### Q2 — Local anaesthetic systemic toxicity (LAST) treatment

Young male on 0.25% bupivacaine becomes unresponsive with no pulse. Best management?

- A. CPR with 20% intralipid
- B. CPR with sodium bicarbonate
- C. CPR with dobutamine
- D. CPR with calcium

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. CPR with 20% Intralipid (lipid emulsion)** — LAST treatment: lipid rescue therapy (1.5 mL/kg bolus, then infusion). Lipid sink theory — sequesters lipophilic LA away from cardiac tissue. Avoid vasopressin/calcium channel blockers.
</details>

Best oxygen delivery device for COVID-19 patient needing high FiO₂?

### Q6 — Capnography in bronchospasm (asthma)

Asthma patient with shark-fin shaped capnograph. Indicates:

### Q9 — Capnogram alveolar plateau

Marked phase on capnogram showing constant EtCO₂:

- A. Expiratory upstroke
- B. Alveolar plateau
- C. Inspiratory downstroke
- D. Inspiratory baseline

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Alveolar plateau (Phase III)** — Constant EtCO₂ reflecting alveolar gas. End-tidal value is read at the end of Phase III. Phase 0 = inspiration, I = dead space, II = ascending, III = plateau.
</details>

---
### Q10 — Capnogram Phase 1 dead space

Capnogram Phase 1 represents:

- A. Dead space ventilation
- B. Alveolar plateau
- C. Mixed expiratory gas
- D. Inspiration

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Dead space ventilation** — Phase 1: exhalation of CO₂-free anatomical dead space. Phase 2: mixed dead space + alveolar gas (ascending). Phase 3: pure alveolar (plateau).
</details>

Main adverse effect of laryngeal mask airway (LMA):

- A. Vocal cord damage
- B. Pneumothorax

## Drugs

### Q15 — Curare mechanism

Curare-type muscle relaxants act by:

- A. Persistent depolarisation
- B. Competitive blockade of ACh receptors
- C. Stimulation of ACh receptors
- D. Calcium channel inhibition

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Competitive blockade of ACh receptors** — Non-depolarising NMBA (tubocurarine, vecuronium, rocuronium, atracurium, pancuronium) competitively block nicotinic ACh receptors at NMJ. Reversed by neostigmine + glycopyrrolate.
</details>

### Q16 — IV anaesthetic without myocardial depression

IV induction agent with minimal cardiovascular depression:

- A. Etomidate
- B. Propofol
- C. Thiopentone
- D. Ketamine

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Etomidate** — Cardiovascular stable; ideal for hemodynamically compromised patients. Side effects: adrenocortical suppression (avoid in sepsis), myoclonus, PONV. Ketamine raises BP/HR but causes sympathetic stimulation.
</details>

### Q19 — Local anaesthetic safe in liver disease

Local anaesthetic preferred in patients with severe liver disease:

- A. Lidocaine
- B. Bupivacaine
- C. Ropivacaine
- D. Procaine

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. Procaine** — Ester-type LA; hydrolysed by plasma cholinesterase, not liver. Amide LAs (lido, bupiva, ropi) are metabolised by liver CYP → caution in liver disease.
</details>

### Q20 — Succinylcholine side effect (NOT bronchospasm)

Which is NOT a typical side effect of succinylcholine?

- A. Hyperkalaemia
- B. Bronchospasm
- C. Malignant hyperthermia
- D. Bradycardia

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Bronchospasm** — Suxamethonium can cause hyperkalaemia (especially in burns/SCI/myopathy), MH (in susceptible), bradycardia, increased ICP/IOP/intragastric pressure, myalgia, prolonged apnea (pseudocholinesterase deficiency). Bronchospasm is uncommon (less so than histamine releasers).
</details>

### Q79 — Parameter measured by pulse oximetry
