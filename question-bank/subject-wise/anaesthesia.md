## Airway & Ventilation

### Q1 — Last structure pierced in lumbar puncture

Last structure pierced before entering the lumbar cistern during LP?

- A. Ligamentum flavum
- B. Dura mater
- C. Arachnoid membrane
- D. Pia mater

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Arachnoid membrane** — Order: skin → SC tissue → supraspinous ligament → interspinous ligament → ligamentum flavum → epidural space → dura mater → arachnoid mater → CSF. Pia is adherent to spinal cord and not pierced.
</details>

### Q2 — Local anaesthetic systemic toxicity (LAST) treatment

Young male on 0.25% bupivacaine becomes unresponsive with no pulse. Best management?

- A. CPR with 20% intralipid
- B. CPR with sodium bicarbonate
- C. CPR with dobutamine
- D. CPR with calcium

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. CPR with 20% Intralipid (lipid emulsion)** — LAST treatment: lipid rescue therapy (1.5 mL/kg bolus, then infusion). Lipid sink theory — sequesters lipophilic LA away from cardiac tissue. Avoid vasopressin/calcium channel blockers.
</details>

### Q3 — Head tilt-chin lift

Image shows airway manoeuvre with head extension and jaw lift:

- A. Head tilt-chin lift
- B. Jaw thrust
- C. Head extension alone

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Head tilt-chin lift** — Basic airway opening manoeuvre. Avoid if cervical spine injury suspected (use jaw thrust instead). Both lift tongue away from posterior pharynx.

### Q4 — Non-rebreathing mask for COVID

Best oxygen delivery device for COVID-19 patient needing high FiO₂?

- A. Venturi mask
- B. Hudson mask
- C. Nebuliser
- D. Non-rebreathing mask

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. Non-rebreathing mask** — Delivers FiO₂ up to ~90% with reservoir bag and one-way valves. Venturi mask gives precise FiO₂ but lower (24–60%). Used before escalation to HFNC/NIV/intubation.
</details>

### Q5 — Mallampati Class II

Patient with base of uvula visible, soft and hard palate seen. Mallampati class?

- A. Class I
- B. Class II

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

### Q6 — Capnography in bronchospasm (asthma)

Asthma patient with shark-fin shaped capnograph. Indicates:

- A. Normal alveolar plateau
- B. Curare cleft
- C. Bronchospasm (shark fin)
- D. Hypoventilation

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Bronchospasm (shark fin)** — Upsloping expiratory phase due to airway obstruction; longer exhalation needed for alveolar gas to escape. Classic in asthma/COPD exacerbation.
</details>

### Q7 — Tuohy needle for epidural

Needle used for epidural anaesthesia:

- A. Quincke needle
- B. Whitacre needle
- C. Sprotte needle
- D. Tuohy needle

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. Tuohy needle** — Curved tip (Huber point) allows catheter direction into epidural space; depth markings; characteristic alternating dark/light bands. Quincke/Whitacre/Sprotte = spinal needles.
</details>

### Q8 — Mapleson circuit valve pressure

Maximum pressure setting for Mapleson breathing circuit relief valve:

- A. 10–20 mmHg
- B. 30–40 mmHg
- C. 10–20 cm H₂O
- D. 30–40 cm H₂O

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. 30–40 cm H₂O** — Adjustable pressure-limiting (APL) valve protects against barotrauma; opens at ~30–40 cm H₂O during spontaneous ventilation.
</details>

### Q9 — Capnogram alveolar plateau

Marked phase on capnogram showing constant EtCO₂:

- A. Expiratory upstroke
- B. Alveolar plateau
- C. Inspiratory downstroke
- D. Inspiratory baseline

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Alveolar plateau (Phase III)** — Constant EtCO₂ reflecting alveolar gas. End-tidal value is read at the end of Phase III. Phase 0 = inspiration, I = dead space, II = ascending, III = plateau.
</details>

---

### Q10 — Capnogram Phase 1 dead space

Capnogram Phase 1 represents:

- A. Dead space ventilation
- B. Alveolar plateau
- C. Mixed expiratory gas
- D. Inspiration

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Dead space ventilation** — Phase 1: exhalation of CO₂-free anatomical dead space. Phase 2: mixed dead space + alveolar gas (ascending). Phase 3: pure alveolar (plateau).
</details>

### Q11 — Curare cleft on capnograph

A notch in the alveolar plateau during spontaneous ventilation indicates:

- A. Hypoventilation
- B. Bronchospasm
- C. Curare cleft (partial paralysis breaking through)
- D. Equipment malfunction

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Curare cleft** — Patient attempting to breathe spontaneously despite partial neuromuscular blockade → notch in capnograph plateau. Suggests need for re-paralysis or reversal.
</details>

### Q13 — Murphy's eye

A device with "Murphy's eye" is:

- A. Endotracheal tube
- B. LMA
- C. Combitube
- D. Suction catheter

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Endotracheal tube** — Murphy's eye is a small lateral hole near the distal tip; provides alternate airway if main lumen blocked by secretions or mucosal apposition.
</details>

### Q14 — LMA aspiration risk

Main adverse effect of laryngeal mask airway (LMA):

- A. Vocal cord damage
- B. Pneumothorax
- C. Pulmonary aspiration
- D. Tracheal stenosis

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Pulmonary aspiration** — LMA does not seal airway as well as ETT; risk of regurgitation/aspiration. Contraindicated in full stomach, severe reflux, pregnancy.
</details>

## Drugs

### Q15 — Curare mechanism

Curare-type muscle relaxants act by:

- A. Persistent depolarisation
- B. Competitive blockade of ACh receptors
- C. Stimulation of ACh receptors
- D. Calcium channel inhibition

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Competitive blockade of ACh receptors** — Non-depolarising NMBA (tubocurarine, vecuronium, rocuronium, atracurium, pancuronium) competitively block nicotinic ACh receptors at NMJ. Reversed by neostigmine + glycopyrrolate.
</details>

### Q16 — IV anaesthetic without myocardial depression

IV induction agent with minimal cardiovascular depression:

- A. Etomidate
- B. Propofol
- C. Thiopentone
- D. Ketamine

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Etomidate** — Cardiovascular stable; ideal for hemodynamically compromised patients. Side effects: adrenocortical suppression (avoid in sepsis), myoclonus, PONV. Ketamine raises BP/HR but causes sympathetic stimulation.
</details>

### Q17 — Desflurane NOT for paediatric induction

Which is NOT used for inhalational induction in paediatric anaesthesia?

- A. Halothane
- B. Sevoflurane
- C. Desflurane
- D. Nitrous oxide

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Desflurane** — Pungent; causes airway irritation, breath-holding, laryngospasm in children → NOT for induction. Sevoflurane is the agent of choice for paediatric inhalational induction.
</details>

### Q18 — IV agent without injection pain

Which IV induction agent does NOT cause injection pain?

- A. Ketamine
- B. Propofol
- C. Etomidate
- D. Thiopentone

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Ketamine** — Minimal pain on injection (can be given IM too). Propofol/etomidate cause venous irritation/pain. Mitigation: pretreatment with lignocaine.
</details>

### Q19 — Local anaesthetic safe in liver disease

Local anaesthetic preferred in patients with severe liver disease:

- A. Lidocaine
- B. Bupivacaine
- C. Ropivacaine
- D. Procaine

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. Procaine** — Ester-type LA; hydrolysed by plasma cholinesterase, not liver. Amide LAs (lido, bupiva, ropi) are metabolised by liver CYP → caution in liver disease.
</details>

### Q20 — Succinylcholine side effect (NOT bronchospasm)

Which is NOT a typical side effect of succinylcholine?

- A. Hyperkalaemia
- B. Bronchospasm
- C. Malignant hyperthermia
- D. Bradycardia

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Bronchospasm** — Suxamethonium can cause hyperkalaemia (especially in burns/SCI/myopathy), MH (in susceptible), bradycardia, increased ICP/IOP/intragastric pressure, myalgia, prolonged apnea (pseudocholinesterase deficiency). Bronchospasm is uncommon (less so than histamine releasers).
</details>

### Q21 — Recovery position

Recovery position is indicated for:

- A. Conscious patient with pulse and breathing
- B. Unconscious patient with pulse and breathing
- C. Unconscious patient with pulse but no breathing
- D. Unconscious patient without pulse

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Unconscious with pulse and breathing** — Lateral recumbent position; maintains airway, prevents aspiration. Not used if pulseless (start CPR) or in suspected spine injury.
</details>

### Q22 — AED for cardiac patient

Portable microprocessor device delivering electric shock for cardiac arrest:

- A. Defibrillator (manual)
- B. ECG monitor
- C. Pacemaker
- D. Automated External Defibrillator (AED)

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. AED** — Public-access defibrillator; analyses rhythm automatically; delivers biphasic shock for shockable rhythms (VF, pulseless VT). Voice-prompts guide layperson use.
</details>

### Q23 — BiPAP for COPD

Non-invasive ventilatory support device for COPD exacerbation:

- A. BiPAP
- B. Mechanical ventilator
- C. Volume-cycled ventilator
- D. CPAP

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. BiPAP (Bilevel Positive Airway Pressure)** — Delivers IPAP (inspiratory) and EPAP (expiratory). Reduces work of breathing in COPD exacerbation. CPAP delivers continuous pressure (better for cardiogenic pulmonary oedema).
</details>

### Q24 — Halothane and malignant hyperthermia

Anaesthetic gas associated with MH trigger:

- A. Propofol
- B. Nitrous oxide
- C. Etomidate
- D. Halothane

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. Halothane** — All volatile anaesthetics (halothane, sevoflurane, isoflurane, desflurane) + succinylcholine can trigger MH in susceptible individuals (RYR1 gene mutation). Rx: dantrolene.
</details>

### Q25 — Atracurium histamine release

Patient on atracurium develops flushing and hypotension. Cause?

- A. Atracurium histamine release
- B. Allergic reaction to propofol
- C. Anaphylaxis
- D. Hyperthermia

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Atracurium causes histamine release** — Especially with rapid bolus. Cisatracurium (an isomer) does NOT cause histamine release.
</details>

### Q26 — Stable narrow-complex SVT management

60-year-old with narrow-complex tachycardia at 180 bpm, stable, no chest pain. First-line drug?

- A. Synchronised cardioversion
- B. Amiodarone
- C. Adenosine
- D. Observation

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Adenosine** — Rapid IV bolus (6 mg, then 12 mg if needed) for stable narrow-complex SVT. Causes transient AV block, terminates re-entrant SVT. Cardioversion if unstable.
</details>

Active ingredients in EMLA cream:
