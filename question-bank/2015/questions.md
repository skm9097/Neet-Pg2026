## Anatomy

### Q1 — External carotid artery: NOT a branch

All of the following are branches of the external carotid artery **except**:

- A. Superior thyroid artery
- B. Anterior ethmoidal artery
- C. Occipital artery
- D. Posterior auricular artery

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Anterior ethmoidal artery** — A branch of the **ophthalmic artery** (from internal carotid), not the ECA. ECA branches (mnemonic: Some Anatomists Like Freaking Out Poor Medical Students): Superior thyroid, Ascending pharyngeal, Lingual, Facial, Occipital, Posterior auricular, Maxillary, Superficial temporal.

</details>

### Q2 — Cranial nerve palsy localisation (III, IV, VI + V1)

Paralysis of 3rd, 4th, 6th nerves with involvement of the ophthalmic division of the 5th nerve localises the lesion to:

- A. Cavernous sinus
- B. Apex of orbit
- C. Brainstem
- D. Base of skull

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Cavernous sinus** — The cavernous sinus contains CN III, IV, V1, V2, and VI. Apex of orbit syndrome (Rochon-Duvigneaud) involves III, IV, V1, VI and the optic nerve (II). Brainstem lesions usually cause crossed signs (ipsilateral cranial nerves + contralateral long-tract signs).

</details>

### Q3 — Carpal tunnel contents: NOT included

The carpal tunnel contains all of the following **except**:

- A. Median nerve
- B. Flexor pollicis longus tendon
- C. Flexor carpi radialis tendon
- D. Flexor digitorum superficialis tendons

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Flexor carpi radialis tendon** — FCR passes **outside** the carpal tunnel, in its own osteofibrous sheath within the flexor retinaculum. Carpal tunnel contains: median nerve + 9 tendons (FPL, 4× FDS, 4× FDP).

</details>

### Q4 — Femoral ring boundaries: NOT a boundary

The femoral ring is bounded by the following structures **except**:

- A. Femoral vein
- B. Inguinal ligament
- C. Femoral artery
- D. Lacunar ligament

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Femoral artery** — Femoral ring boundaries: Anterior = inguinal ligament; Posterior = pectineal ligament/superior ramus of pubis; Medial = lacunar ligament; Lateral = femoral vein. The femoral **artery** lies lateral to the femoral vein, outside the femoral ring.

</details>

### Q5 — Vas deferens: false statement

All of the following statements regarding vas deferens are true **except**:

- A. Terminal part is dilated to form ampulla
- B. It crosses the ureter in the region of the ischial spine
- C. It passes lateral to the inferior epigastric artery at the deep inguinal ring
- D. It is separated from the base of bladder by the peritoneum

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. Separated from base of bladder by peritoneum** — FALSE. The vas deferens is in **direct contact** with the base of the bladder (no peritoneum between them). The terminal part forms the ampulla, it crosses the ureter near the ischial spine ("water under the bridge"), and passes lateral to the inferior epigastric artery at the deep inguinal ring.

</details>

### Q6 — Chorda tympani nerve: false statement

The following statements concerning the chorda tympani nerve are true **except** that it:

- A. Carries secretomotor fibres to the submandibular gland
- B. Joins the lingual nerve in the infratemporal fossa
- C. Is a branch of the facial nerve
- D. Contains postganglionic parasympathetic fibres

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. Contains postganglionic parasympathetic fibres** — FALSE. Chorda tympani carries **preganglionic** parasympathetic fibres (from superior salivatory nucleus) that synapse in the submandibular ganglion; postganglionic fibres then innervate the submandibular and sublingual glands. It also carries taste from anterior 2/3 of tongue.

</details>

### Q7 — Sacrococcygeal joint type

The type of joint between the sacrum and the coccyx is a:

- A. Symphysis
- B. Synostosis
- C. Synchondrosis
- D. Syndesmosis

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Symphysis** — The sacrococcygeal joint is a secondary cartilaginous joint (symphysis) with a fibrocartilaginous disc between the apex of the sacrum and the base of the coccyx. May ossify (synostosis) in old age.

</details>

### Q8 — Prostatic urethra: false feature

The prostatic urethra is characterised by all of the following **except** that it:

- A. Is the widest and most dilatable part of the urethra
- B. Presents a concavity posteriorly
- C. Lies closer to the anterior surface of the prostate
- D. Receives prostatic ductules along its posterior wall

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Presents a concavity posteriorly** — FALSE. The prostatic urethra presents a concavity **anteriorly** (urethral crest projects on the posterior wall). It is the widest and most dilatable part, lies closer to the anterior surface, and prostatic ductules open on the posterior wall (in the prostatic sinuses lateral to the urethral crest).

</details>

### Q9 — Uterine lymphatic drainage: NOT a destination

The following group of lymph nodes receives lymphatics from the uterus **except**:

- A. External iliac
- B. Internal iliac
- C. Superficial inguinal
- D. Deep inguinal

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. Deep inguinal** — Lymphatic drainage of uterus: fundus → para-aortic (along ovarian vessels); body → external iliac; cervix → internal iliac, external iliac, sacral; round ligament → **superficial** inguinal. Deep inguinal nodes are NOT a drainage site for the uterus.

</details>

### Q10 — Epiphyseal plate growth processes: NOT a feature

All of the following physiological processes occur during growth at the epiphyseal plate **except**:

- A. Proliferation and hypertrophy
- B. Calcification and ossification
- C. Vasculogenesis and erosion
- D. Replacement of red bone marrow with yellow bone marrow

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. Replacement of red bone marrow with yellow bone marrow** — FALSE. Red-to-yellow marrow conversion is a separate developmental process occurring in the medullary cavity (begins in distal long bones in infancy, progresses centrally). At the epiphyseal plate, chondrocytes undergo: resting → proliferation → hypertrophy → calcification → ossification, with vascular invasion (vasculogenesis) and matrix erosion.

</details>

<!-- SOURCE: medicallegends.blogspot.com AIPGMEE 2015 Anatomy | OPTIONS_GENERATED: false -->

### Q11 — Benign prostatic hypertrophy: lobe enlarged

Benign prostatic hypertrophy results in obstruction of the urinary tract. The condition is associated with enlargement of the:

- A. Entire prostate gland
- B. Lateral lobes
- C. Median lobe
- D. Posterior lobes

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Median lobe** — Median lobe enlargement (transition zone) causes obstruction of the internal urethral orifice → BPH symptoms. Posterior lobe is the typical site for prostate **cancer** (peripheral zone). Lateral lobes also enlarge but predominantly the median lobe causes obstruction.

</details>

### Q12 — Per rectal examination: NOT palpable anteriorly

In an adult male, on per rectal examination, the following structures can be felt anteriorly **except**:

- A. Internal iliac lymph nodes
- B. Bulb of the penis
- C. Prostate
- D. Seminal vesicle (when enlarged)

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Internal iliac lymph nodes** — Internal iliac nodes lie **deep in the pelvis along the iliac vessels** and are NOT palpable on PR examination. Anteriorly palpable on PR: prostate (normal/enlarged), bulb of penis, enlarged seminal vesicles, bladder base, sigmoid colon.

</details>

- B. Chlamydia trachomatis

### Q15 — Placental Site Trophoblastic Tumour *(2015)*

True about placental site trophoblastic disease is:

- A. Highly malignant behaviour
- B. Hysterectomy followed by chemoradiation is the treatment of choice
- C. Secretes human placental lactogen (HPL)
- D. Contains syncytiotrophoblasts mainly

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Secretes human placental lactogen (HPL)** — PSTT arises from placental bed intermediate trophoblasts, not syncytiotrophoblasts. It secretes HPL (not hCG), usually runs a benign course, and hysterectomy alone (without routine chemoradiation) is the standard treatment.

### Q16 — Crowning Definition *(2015)*

Crowning is defined as:

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

### Q21 — Gestational Hypertension — Not a Risk Factor *(2015)*

Which is NOT a risk factor for gestational hypertension:

- A. Obesity
- B. Smoking
- C. Primigravida
- D. Factor V Leiden mutation

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

### Q22 — MTP Misoprostol Timing (FDA Protocol) *(2015)*

In medical termination of pregnancy, according to FDA protocol, misoprostol is given after how many hours of mifepristone:

- A. 24 hours
- B. 48 hours
- C. 72 hours

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

### Q24 — Most Common Ovarian Tumour to Tort in Pregnancy *(2015)*

Which ovarian tumour is most prone to undergo torsion during pregnancy:

- A. Serous cystadenoma
- B. Mucinous cystadenoma
- C. Dermoid cyst
- D. Theca lutein cyst

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Dermoid cyst (benign cystic teratoma)** — Because of its fat content dermoid cysts are lighter than fluid-filled tumours and torse more easily; ~15% undergo torsion. The most common ovarian tumour in pregnancy overall is serous cystadenoma, but dermoid is the most common one diagnosed (and to torse) during pregnancy.

</details>

### Q27 — Hot Flushes Mechanism *(2015)*

Hot flushes are experienced as a result of:

- A. Increased noradrenaline alone
- B. Decreased oestrogen alone
- C. Increased noradrenaline and decreased oestrogen
- D. Increased noradrenaline and oestrogen

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

- B. Macafee and Johnson regimen
