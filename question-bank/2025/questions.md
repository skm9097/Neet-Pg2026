## Physiology

### Q12 — V/Q mismatch in pulmonary embolism (graph)

In pulmonary embolism with complete blood flow obstruction, the affected lung units move to which point on the V/Q graph?

- A. A
- B. B
- C. C
- D. D

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. D** — Complete obstruction of blood flow (Q=0, V remains) → infinite V/Q ratio. Point D represents pure dead space ventilation (ventilated but not perfused).
</details>

### Q13 — Starling forces calculation (interstitial hydrostatic pressure)

Capillary: Pc = 18 mmHg, πc = 27 mmHg, πi = 7 mmHg. No net fluid movement. Interstitial hydrostatic pressure (Pi)?

- A. 0 mmHg
- B. +1 mmHg
- C. +2 mmHg
- D. −2 mmHg

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. −2 mmHg** — Starling equilibrium: Pc − Pi = πc − πi → 18 − Pi = 27 − 7 = 20 → Pi = 18 − 20 = −2 mmHg. Normal interstitial pressure is slightly subatmospheric.
</details>

### Q14 — High altitude: mechanism of symptoms

Patient at 3700 m with headache, nausea, dyspnea. Explanation?

- A. Hypoxia causing vasodilation
- B. Hypoxia causing vasoconstriction
- C. Metabolic acidosis causes oedema
- D. High PO₂ causing vasoconstriction

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Hypoxia causing vasodilation** — Systemic hypoxia → cerebral vasodilation → increased ICP → headache, nausea. Pulmonary hypoxia causes vasoconstriction (HPV). AMS symptoms are primarily from cerebral oedema.
</details>

### Q15 — Insulin secretion pattern with sustained hyperglycemia

Blood glucose maintained at 2–3× normal. Insulin secretion pattern?

- A. Rapid rise followed by fall
- B. Gradual rise followed by fall
- C. Remains persistently elevated at same level
- D. Initial rise followed by sustained elevation

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. Initial rise followed by sustained elevation** — First-phase (rapid, 1–3 min): release of pre-formed insulin granules. Second-phase (sustained): new insulin synthesis and secretion. Both phases active with sustained hyperglycemia.
</details>

### Q18 — Acid-base at high altitude

What acid-base disturbance occurs at high altitude due to hyperventilation?

- A. Metabolic acidosis
- B. Metabolic alkalosis
- C. Respiratory acidosis
- D. Respiratory alkalosis

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. Respiratory alkalosis** — Hypoxia → hyperventilation → CO₂ washout → ↓pCO₂ → respiratory alkalosis. Renal compensation (HCO₃⁻ excretion) occurs over days.
</details>

### Q19 — Pituitary adenoma, elevated prolactin, amenorrhea mechanism

Woman with amenorrhea and galactorrhea; pituitary adenoma, elevated prolactin. Cause of amenorrhea?

- A. Low GnRH
- B. High pulsed LH
- C. High FSH
- D. Inhibition of GnRH by prolactin

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. Inhibition of GnRH by prolactin** — Hyperprolactinemia → inhibits GnRH pulsatility → decreased LH/FSH → anovulation/amenorrhea. Options A and D are essentially the same mechanism; D is more precise.
</details>

### Q32 — Caspase-dependent cell death types

Which types of cell death involve activation of caspase enzymes?

- A. Necrosis and Apoptosis
- B. Apoptosis and Pyroptosis
- C. Apoptosis and Necroptosis
- D. Apoptosis only

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Apoptosis and Pyroptosis** — Both activate caspases (apoptosis: caspase-3/7; pyroptosis: caspase-1/4/5/11). Necroptosis involves RIP kinases, not caspases. Necrosis is passive.
</details>
