## Anatomy

### Q1 — Ankle ligament after eversion injury

A patient presents with ankle pain and swelling after a forceful **eversion** injury. Which ligament is most likely damaged?

- A. Anterior talofibular ligament
- B. Calcaneofibular ligament
- C. Deltoid ligament
- D. Posterior talofibular ligament

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Deltoid ligament** — The medial collateral (deltoid) ligament resists eversion. Lateral ligaments (ATFL, CFL, PTFL) resist inversion and are injured in inversion sprains.
</details>

## Physiology

### Q2 — Mitochondrial inheritance disorder

Which of the following is a mitochondrial inheritance disorder?

- A. Kearns–Sayre syndrome
- B. Williams syndrome
- C. Achondroplasia
- D. Cystic fibrosis

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Kearns–Sayre syndrome** — Caused by mtDNA deletions; features progressive external ophthalmoplegia, pigmentary retinopathy, cardiac conduction defects.
</details>

### Q3 — High altitude acid–base

A man climbing a mountain experiences physiological changes. What is the **primary** acid–base abnormality?

- A. Metabolic acidosis
- B. Metabolic alkalosis
- C. Respiratory acidosis
- D. Respiratory alkalosis

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D. Respiratory alkalosis** — Hypoxia → hyperventilation → CO₂ washout → respiratory alkalosis. Renal compensation (bicarbonate excretion) takes days.
</details>

## Biochemistry

### Q4 — Enzyme defect: scurvy-like features

A child with fractures, petechiae, perifollicular hemorrhages, and gum bleeding presents. Which enzyme is deficient?

- A. Lysyl oxidase
- B. Prolyl hydroxylase
- C. Tyrosinase
- D. Alkaline phosphatase

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Prolyl hydroxylase** — Vitamin C is a cofactor for prolyl & lysyl hydroxylase in collagen synthesis. Deficiency → scurvy.
</details>

### Q5 — Jaundice corrected by Vitamin K

A person with jaundice and a bleeding history whose coagulation normalizes after Vitamin K injection. Probable cause?

- A. Biliary obstruction
- B. Alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency
- C. Autoimmune hepatitis
- D. Factor XI deficiency

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Biliary obstruction** — Bile-salt deficiency → fat-soluble vitamin malabsorption (incl. Vit K) → impaired II, VII, IX, X synthesis. Parenteral Vit K bypasses absorption defect.
</details>

## Pathology

### Q6 — Jaundice, splenomegaly, high MCHC

A 16-year-old boy with jaundice, splenomegaly, similar paternal history, and high MCHC. Most likely diagnosis?

- A. Iron deficiency anaemia
- B. Hereditary spherocytosis
- C. Thalassemia major
- D. Autoimmune haemolytic anaemia

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Hereditary spherocytosis** — Autosomal dominant. Spectrin/ankyrin defect → spherocytes → splenic destruction. Raised MCHC is classic.
</details>

### Q7 — CD marker quantification

Which laboratory technique most commonly compares and quantifies CD markers on cells?

- A. ELISA
- B. Western blot
- C. Flow cytometry
- D. Immunohistochemistry

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Flow cytometry** — Single-cell analysis; gold standard for surface marker quantification (lymphoma immunophenotyping, CD4 counts).
</details>

## Pharmacology

### Q8 — Benralizumab target

Benralizumab acts on which receptor?

- A. IL-5 receptor α
- B. IL-4 receptor
- C. IL-1 receptor
- D. TNF-α receptor

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. IL-5 receptor α** — Used in severe eosinophilic asthma. Mepolizumab/reslizumab bind IL-5 itself; benralizumab binds the IL-5Rα on eosinophils.
</details>

### Q9 — Migraine prophylaxis with CAD history

A woman with migraine, family history of CAD, previously treated with sumatriptan. Drug of choice for **prophylaxis**?

- A. Propranolol
- B. Topiramate
- C. Ergotamine
- D. Naratriptan

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Propranolol** — First-line prophylaxis for migraine. Triptans/ergots are abortive and contraindicated in CAD.
</details>

## Microbiology

### Q10 — Group A Strep attachment

Which bacterial component enables **Group A Streptococcus** attachment to pharyngeal epithelium?

- A. Lipoprotein
- B. Lipoteichoic acid
- C. Capsule
- D. Flagella

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Lipoteichoic acid** — Anchors to fibronectin on epithelial cells. M-protein also contributes to virulence (anti-phagocytic).
</details>

### Q11 — Sewage worker, jaundice + AKI

A 25-year-old sewage worker with fever, weakness, elevated bilirubin, decreased urine output, conjunctival redness. Diagnosis?

- A. Brucellosis
- B. Weil's disease
- C. Enteric fever
- D. Hepatitis A

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. Weil's disease (severe leptospirosis)** — Triad of jaundice, AKI, haemorrhage in occupational exposure. Conjunctival suffusion is classic.
</details>

## Forensic Medicine

### Q12 — Garlic odour, rice-water diarrhoea

Patient with abdominal pain, throat irritation, bloody vomiting, rice-water diarrhoea, garlicky breath. Agent?

- A. Arsenic
- B. Phosphorus
- C. Aluminium phosphide
- D. Croton seeds

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Arsenic** — Acute poisoning: GI symptoms, garlicky breath, rice-water stools. Chronic: Mees lines, hyperpigmentation, hyperkeratosis.
</details>

### Q13 — Consent in alleged rape (minor)

A 16-year-old girl and 23-year-old male are examined after rape allegations; the girl states it was consensual and no injuries found. Legal status of consent?

- A. Consent invalid — girl under 18
- B. No punishment — consensual
- C. No punishment — no injuries
- D. Parents must prove non-consensual

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Consent invalid — girl under 18.** Under POCSO Act / IPC, consent of a minor is legally invalid.
</details>

## Ophthalmology

### Q16 — White flakes on anterior lens

An elderly patient with white dandruff-like deposits on the anterior lens surface on slit-lamp exam. Diagnosis?

- A. Pseudoexfoliation syndrome
- B. Iris cyst
- C. Persistent pupillary membrane
- D. Pigment dispersion syndrome

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Pseudoexfoliation syndrome** — Greyish "dandruff" on lens capsule and pupillary margin; risk factor for open-angle glaucoma and complicated cataract surgery (zonular weakness).
</details>

### Q17 — Intacs ring indication

Which condition is treated using an Intacs ring?

- A. Keratoconus
- B. Cataract
- C. Corneal ulcer
- D. Glaucoma

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Keratoconus** — Intrastromal corneal ring segments flatten the cone in keratoconus.
</details>

### Q18 — Lateral canthus mass in adolescent

A 15-year-old female with a painless, gradually increasing mass lateral to the lateral canthus for 10 years. Diagnosis?

- A. Dermoid cyst
- B. Capillary hemangioma
- C. Lacrimal gland tumor
- D. Epidermoid cyst

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Dermoid cyst** — Congenital; most commonly at fronto-zygomatic suture (super-temporal orbit). Slow-growing, painless.
</details>

## Pediatrics

### Q19 — Hurler syndrome accumulation

A child with developmental delay, coarse features, and α-L-iduronidase deficiency. What accumulates?

- A. Dermatan + Chondroitin sulfate
- B. Dermatan sulfate only
- C. Dermatan + Heparan sulfate
- D. Heparan + Chondroitin sulfate

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Dermatan + Heparan sulfate** — Hurler syndrome (MPS I). α-L-iduronidase deficiency leads to dermatan & heparan sulfate accumulation.
</details>

### Q20 — ADA deficiency

A 6-month-old with recurrent infections, failure to thrive, and adenosine deaminase deficiency. Associated immunodeficiency?

- A. SCID
- B. Hypogammaglobulinemia
- C. DiGeorge syndrome
- D. Wiskott–Aldrich syndrome

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. SCID** — ADA deficiency causes ~15% of SCID. Toxic deoxyadenosine accumulates in lymphocytes.
</details>

## Obstetrics & Gynaecology

### Q21 — Hormonal profile days 21–25

A woman with regular 28-day cycle. Hormonal profile during days 21–25?

- A. Low estrogen, high progesterone, low LH/FSH
- B. Low estrogen, low progesterone, low LH/FSH
- C. Low estrogen, high progesterone, high LH/FSH
- D. High estrogen, high progesterone

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Low estrogen, high progesterone, low LH/FSH** — Mid–late luteal phase. Corpus luteum produces progesterone; high progesterone feedback suppresses LH/FSH.
</details>

### Q22 — Controlled cord traction

During vaginal delivery, the resident applies counter-pressure above the pubic symphysis while gently pulling the umbilical cord. Maneuver?

- A. Controlled cord traction (Brandt–Andrews)
- B. Manual placental removal
- C. Uterine massage
- D. Bimanual uterine compression

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Controlled cord traction** — Active management of third stage; combined with oxytocin to reduce postpartum haemorrhage.
</details>

## Surgery

### Q23 — Circumferential full-thickness burns

A 30-year-old female with circumferential full-thickness and deep partial-thickness lower limb burns. Procedure performed?

- A. Escharotomy
- B. Debridement
- C. Excision to healthy tissue
- D. Early skin grafting

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Escharotomy** — Circumferential full-thickness burns cause a tourniquet effect → compartment syndrome. Escharotomy relieves pressure.
</details>

Identify the fistula according to Park's classification.

- A. Intersphincteric
- B. Suprasphincteric
- C. Extrasphincteric
- D. Trans-sphincteric high

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Intersphincteric** — Most common type (~70%); tract runs between internal and external sphincter.

## Medicine

### Q25 — ODS after rapid sodium correction

A patient with seizures and sodium 98 mEq/L. After 24 hours, sodium corrected to 110 mEq/L. Patient develops mutism and altered sensorium. Investigation?

- A. MRI head
- B. LP for CSF biochemistry
- C. Brainstem evoked potentials
- D. EEG

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. MRI head** — Osmotic demyelination syndrome (central pontine myelinolysis). Limit correction to ≤8–10 mEq/L per 24 h.
</details>

### Q26 — Mitral stenosis with AF

A 30-year-old man with 6-month PND/SOB, elevated JVP, irregularly irregular pulse, hepatomegaly, MDM, past acute rheumatic fever. Which is **NOT** seen?

- A. Pre-systolic accentuation of MDM
- B. Increased embolic stroke risk
- C. Absent 'a' wave in JVP
- D. Right heart failure

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Pre-systolic accentuation** — Lost in AF (no organized atrial contraction). All other features present.
</details>

## Anaesthesia

### Q29 — Malignant hyperthermia

A 35-year-old male on sevoflurane and succinylcholine develops muscle rigidity, fever, tachycardia, rising end-tidal CO₂. Immediate treatment?

- A. Dantrolene
- B. Diazepam
- C. Pancuronium
- D. Vecuronium

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Dantrolene** — Ryanodine receptor blocker. Pathognomonic trigger: volatile anaesthetics + succinylcholine.
</details>

### Q30 — Histamine release from atracurium

A patient receiving a muscle relaxant develops flushing and rashes over the neck and anterior chest. Associated relaxant?

- A. Atracurium
- B. Cisatracurium
- C. Vecuronium
- D. Pancuronium

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A. Atracurium** — Causes histamine release. Cisatracurium (an isomer) does not.
</details>

## Orthopaedics

### Q31 — Rhomboid, positively birefringent crystals

A 40-year-old man with joint discomfort. Synovial fluid shows rhomboid-shaped, **positively** birefringent crystals. Diagnosis?

- A. Gout
- B. Rheumatoid arthritis
- C. Pseudogout
- D. Osteoarthritis

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C. Pseudogout (CPPD)** — Rhomboid, weakly **positive** birefringence. Gout has needle-shaped, negatively birefringent urate crystals.
</details>

### Q32 — Early ankylosing spondylitis

A 20-year-old with chronic back pain (worse in morning, better with activity), history of anterior uveitis, normal lumbar X-ray. Next investigation?

- A. CT spine
- B. MRI sacroiliac joints
- C. X-ray thoracolumbar spine
- D. Bone scan

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B. MRI sacroiliac joints** — Detects sacroiliitis (bone marrow oedema) before X-ray changes appear.
</details>
